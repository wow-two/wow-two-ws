## 1. EU: non-Union OSS for B2C electronic services, the EUR 10,000 threshold, quarterly returns, B2B reverse charge, customer-location evidence

### Takeaway
A seller with no EU establishment gets no small-seller relief: the EUR 10,000 threshold is conditioned on being established in only one Member State, so B2C electronic services are taxed in the customer's Member State from the first euro. The practical route is the optional non-Union OSS (any Member State of identification, one quarterly return, nil returns still required, no tax representative needed per Stripe). B2B sales to EU businesses with a VAT number sit outside OSS and are reverse-charged by the customer.

### Cited Findings
**Threshold (does EUR 10,000 help a non-EU seller?)**
- The EUR 10,000 threshold applies only if (1) the supplier is established, or with no establishment has its permanent address/usually resides, "in only one Member State"; (2) it supplies B2C TBE (telecom, broadcasting, electronic) services taking place in another Member State; and (3) the total does not exceed EUR 10,000 (excl. VAT) in the current and preceding calendar year. Above it, "the general rule applies without exception" (customer's Member State). — [EC Guide to the VAT One Stop Shop (March 2021), pp. 8-9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a316a98a-3b2b-4991-a9c0-3b5905d369fc_en?filename=OSS_guidelines_en.pdf)
- The same Guide says businesses established outside the EU "and have one or more fixed establishment(s) in the EU cannot make use of the threshold" because it requires establishment "in one single Member State (and nowhere else)". — [EC Guide, p. 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a316a98a-3b2b-4991-a9c0-3b5905d369fc_en?filename=OSS_guidelines_en.pdf)
- The EC place-of-taxation page states the rule (Art. 58) as "electronic services are taxed where the customer resides" and describes the threshold as applying where the supplier is established in an EU country different from the customer's. — [EC, Place of taxation](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-directive/place-taxation_en)
- Stripe offers its "small seller" option (EUR 10,000) only to businesses based in the EU, and says a business based outside the EU "might need to register to collect VAT from your first sale in an EU country". — [Stripe docs, Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union.md)

**Non-Union OSS: eligibility, registration, member state of identification (MSI)**
- "Any taxable person, not established in the EU, who supplies services to non-taxable persons" can register; a non-EU business with no establishment or fixed establishment "can choose any Member State to be the Member State of identification"; the MSI issues an individual VAT number (format EUxxxyyyyyz) usable only for the non-Union scheme. — [EC, Register to OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en)
- Being VAT-identified (or required to be) elsewhere in the EU does not prevent using the non-Union scheme. — [EC Guide, p. 5](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a316a98a-3b2b-4991-a9c0-3b5905d369fc_en?filename=OSS_guidelines_en.pdf)
- MSI can be changed voluntarily at any time, effective at the end of a calendar quarter; changes to registration details must be notified by the 10th day of the following month. — [EC, Register to OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en)
- The EC page lists no sales threshold for the non-Union scheme and does not mention a tax representative. Use is optional. — [EC, Register to OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en); [Irish Revenue, non-Union scheme](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-ecommerce/non-union-scheme/index.aspx)
- Stripe states "You don't need to appoint a tax representative to use the OSS non-Union scheme", and that the scheme covers only services to individuals (B2C); goods are not covered. — [Stripe docs, Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union.md); [Stripe docs, register outside US](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/outside-united-states.md)
- Commencement: normally the first day of the calendar quarter following the quarter in which you notify the MSI. Early start: you may start from the date of your first supply if the MSI is informed "by the tenth day of the month following that first supply"; if that deadline is missed, "he is required to register and account for the VAT in the Member State(s) of consumption directly". — [EC Guide, p. 17](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a316a98a-3b2b-4991-a9c0-3b5905d369fc_en?filename=OSS_guidelines_en.pdf); [EC, Register to OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en)
- Irish Revenue (MSI used by Stripe): non-Union registrations "must be completed through the non-Union OSS registration portal"; ROS (VAT OSS section) applies only to non-EU suppliers already registered for another OSS scheme in Ireland. The page does not list required documents or say whether an Irish representative is needed. — [Irish Revenue](https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-ecommerce/non-union-scheme/index.aspx)

**Returns, payment, records**
- Tax period is the calendar quarter; return and payment are due "by the end of the month following the tax period" (Q1 by 30 April, Q2 31 July, Q3 31 October, Q4 31 January); deadlines do not move for weekends; early filing is not allowed. — [EC, Declare and pay in OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en)
- A "nil return" is mandatory when there were no supplies and no corrections. — [EC, Declare and pay in OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en)
- Return in euro; non-euro supplies converted at the ECB rate published on the last day of the tax period; corrections go in a later return (within three years); payment goes to the MSI which distributes to Member States of consumption; no payment plans; reminder on day 10 after the due date, and exclusion possible after reminders for three consecutive quarters unless the unpaid amount per period is under EUR 100. — [EC, Declare and pay in OSS](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en)
- Records must be kept 10 years from the end of the year of the transaction, and must include "information used to determine the place where the customer is established, has his permanent address or usually resides"; failure to supply records within a month of a reminder leads to exclusion. — [EC Guide, Part 4 Records](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a316a98a-3b2b-4991-a9c0-3b5905d369fc_en?filename=OSS_guidelines_en.pdf)

**Customer-location evidence (B2C)**
- Where no specific presumption applies, the customer's location is the place identified "on the basis of two items of non-contradictory evidence"; three items are needed only to rebut a specific presumption (e.g., SIM country code); the items "have to be different and should not duplicate each other" (a billing address later confirmed by self-certification counts as one item; an IP address and geolocation pointing to the same place count as one). — [EC Explanatory Notes (published 3 April 2014), ch. 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- The Art. 24f list is "illustrative" and non-exhaustive (billing address, IP address/geolocation, bank details, SIM country code, location of fixed landline, other commercially relevant information). If evidence conflicts, the supplier decides which items are "most commercially relevant", with priority to the place that best ensures taxation at actual consumption, and conflicting evidence calls for more regular verification. — [EC Explanatory Notes, ch. 9](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- The single-item-of-evidence relief (EU-established B2C supplies under EUR 100,000 per year) is limited to EU establishments; the 2025 consolidated Regulation 282/2011 is the legal text. — via search summary of [EUR-Lex, Regulation 282/2011](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2011/282) (text itself not read)
- Stripe Tax, for cross-border EU digital services, "prioritizes a single address as the customer's location ... instead of comparing two pieces of non-conflicting evidence", but "store[s] and retain[s] all location evidence used in the transaction on the Customer object". — [Stripe docs, Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union.md); address hierarchy in [Stripe docs, customer addresses](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/customer-locations.md)

**B2B**
- B2B services: place of taxation is where the customer is established (Art. 44). — [EC, Place of taxation](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-directive/place-taxation_en)
- Where the customer is a taxable person in another Member State, the supplier "should not charge VAT as it will be for the customer to account for the tax under the reverse charge mechanism". Status test (Art. 18 Reg. 282/2011): a supplier of electronic services "may regard any customer who does not provide him with a VAT identification number as a non-taxable person" (optional); if it treats a customer as taxable without a VAT number, "the burden of proof will ... be on his shoulders". — [EC Explanatory Notes, ch. 5](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)
- Stripe auto-validates EU VAT numbers against VIES, but that "only validates whether or not the tax ID is valid—you still need to verify the customer's name and address"; and "Stripe Tax applies the reverse charge or zero rate ... when the tax ID has the required number format, regardless of the government verification result"; when validation is `unavailable`, "you must manually verify". — [Stripe docs, customer tax IDs](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/customer/tax-ids.md)
- Stripe: if the customer provides an EU VAT number Stripe treats the sale as reverse charge; the invoice must state reverse charge instead of tax; a domestic (non-VAT) tax ID does not trigger reverse charge; Stripe does not support domestic reverse charge and "assumes that all services sold to customers with a business tax ID are eligible for reverse charge". — [Stripe docs, Tax in the EU](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union.md)
- Stripe: for many cross-border B2B services "your customer accounts for VAT through the reverse-charge mechanism. You might not need a separate VAT registration for those sales", but local B2B sales, imports, or EU inventory can still require domestic registration. — [Stripe docs, register outside US](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/use-stripe-to-register/outside-united-states.md)

### Inferences
- For a non-EU seller there is no "grace period": the first B2C EU digital sale creates VAT liability in the customer's country; non-Union OSS is the compliance mechanism rather than an exemption.
- Timing trap: a registration submitted after sales begin normally starts next quarter; only a notification received by the 10th of the month after the first sale can backdate it to the first sale (Stripe's own registration service cannot do this, see section 5). Missing that window pushes you toward direct registrations in each customer country.
- Because OSS returns (including nil returns) are quarterly and mandatory, registering early creates a recurring filing duty even at zero revenue; deferring EU B2C sales (or restricting EU sales to B2B with validated VAT IDs) avoids it but is a commercial decision.
- Stripe's single-address approach may not by itself satisfy the EC's "two non-contradictory items" rule; practical mitigation is to capture a billing address in Checkout and retain card-issuer country and IP evidence (all within the 10-year record duty).

### Gaps
- Primary legal text of Directive 2006/112/EC Arts. 44, 58, 59c and Regulation 282/2011 Arts. 18, 24b, 24f was not read directly (EUR-Lex fetch returned empty); findings rely on the EC Guide (March 2021), EC Explanatory Notes (April 2014), EC web pages, and one search summary. Both EC PDFs are older than October 2026; their rules are still the operative description but amendments since 2021 (including ViDA, Directive 2025/516, phased from 2028) were not reviewed.
- The EC pages do not state in so many words that a purely non-EU supplier cannot use the EUR 10,000 threshold; the conclusion rests on the single-Member-State condition plus the Stripe docs.
- Documents/process for a non-EU person registering in the Irish portal, and any language or e-ID barriers, were not found.
- How to pay Irish Revenue in euro from a US LLC bank account (fees, SEPA/SWIFT) was not researched.

## 2. UK: VAT registration for a non-established seller of digital services to UK consumers

### Takeaway
There is no registration threshold for a business with no UK establishment: it must register from its first UK taxable supply (or when it expects one in the next 30 days). B2C digital services to UK consumers are UK-taxable; B2B services to UK VAT-registered customers are reverse-charged, and a non-established business whose UK supplies are all reverse-charged is not liable (or entitled) to register. There is no OSS equivalent; UK registration is separate.

### Cited Findings
- gov.uk: a business "based outside the UK" that "supply[ies] any goods or services to the UK (or expect[s] to in the next 30 days)" must register "regardless of taxable turnover"; only a trader whose supplies are all zero-rated can seek exemption. — [GOV.UK, Register for VAT](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat)
- HMRC manual: a business with no UK establishment "is liable to register under Schedule 1A of the VAT Act 1994 if it makes taxable supplies of any value"; where an overseas business supplies services to a UK business the reverse charge usually applies so "it is not considered a taxable supply by the overseas business"; a business "is not entitled or liable to register for VAT in the UK" if all its UK supplies are subject to the reverse charge; exceptions include services supplied to private customers. — [HMRC, VATREG37200](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37200)
- Registration options for a non-established taxable person (NETP): the online VAT Registration Service; optionally a UK-based VAT representative (jointly and severally liable, subject to HMRC agreement); or an agent. The page presents these as options, not requirements. — [HMRC, VATREG37370 (updated 1 October 2026)](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37370)
- Digital services to private consumers: "The place of supply of cross-border digital services is the consumer's location"; if a supply is liable to UK VAT and the business is based outside the UK it must register; the page states no threshold or start date. — [GOV.UK, VAT rules for digital services to consumers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers)
- Location evidence (same page): you "must get and keep evidence" of the consumer's normal location (billing address, device IP address, bank details, SIM country code, landline location); micro and small businesses using payment service providers should obtain two pieces of information, e.g. a billing address including country or phone number with dialling code, plus the payment provider's 2-digit country code notification; if the two match, record them; if not, contact the consumer. — [GOV.UK, VAT rules for digital services to consumers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers)
- Business customers (same page): without a VAT number treat the sale as B2C; a customer claiming business status without a number may be accepted on other evidence (e.g., business website, commercial documents) at the supplier's discretion, and "the customer cannot require business-to-business treatment" without a valid VAT number. — [GOV.UK, VAT rules for digital services to consumers](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-vat-rules-if-you-supply-digital-services-to-private-consumers)
- Process details via search summary of HMRC pages: 30-day rule runs from first taxable supply or expectation and registration can be backdated; late registration can lead to HMRC-initiated registration and penalties; mail-forwarding or registered-office addresses do not qualify (address must be the overseas principal place of business); standard return period is quarterly and nil returns are required; Making Tax Digital applies after registration (paper return without exemption can attract up to GBP 400 penalty); overseas bank details cannot be entered at sign-up; payments from abroad can go by CHAPS; a UK representative is optional unless HMRC directs one. — via search summary of [HMRC VATREG37050](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37050), [VATREG37360](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37360), [VATREG37400](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37400), [GOV.UK, VAT returns](https://www.gov.uk/submit-vat-return)
- Stripe: UK is supported (business and customer location, all product tax codes); UK VAT numbers are auto-validated with HMRC (`gb_vat`) and trigger reverse charge in tax calculation. — [Stripe docs, supported countries](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries.md); [Stripe docs, customer tax IDs](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/customer/tax-ids.md)

### Inferences
- The UK is a stand-alone registration: no OSS umbrella, own quarterly returns via MTD-compatible software. The only no-registration path for UK sales is B2B-only sales to UK VAT-registered customers with valid VAT numbers (all reverse-charged).
- The "overseas principal place of business" address requirement (via search summary) may be awkward for a US LLC managed from Tashkent whose Stripe/US address is a registered-agent address; worth checking against the VAT1 notes before applying.

### Gaps
- HMRC processing time for NETP applications was not found. Notice 700/1 and VAT1 notes were not read directly; several process points above come from a search summary.
- Whether Stripe's "Register for me" covers the UK was not found (only EU non-Union OSS and Canada simplified are listed for Stripe-direct; others go via Taxually).

## 3. Other jurisdictions with digital-services VAT/GST on foreign sellers: first-sale vs threshold table

### Takeaway
Registration is required from the first B2C sale (no threshold) in the EU, UK, South Korea, India, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Mexico, Chile, Colombia, and (per Stripe docs) Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan and Russia. Thresholds exist in Norway (NOK 50,000), Switzerland (CHF 100,000), Australia (AUD 75,000), New Zealand (NZ$60,000), Canada (CAD 30,000), Japan (JPY 10 million, base-period test), Singapore (S$1M global and S$100k B2C) and several Asian/African markets. Evidence quality varies: Norway, Switzerland, New Zealand, Canada, Japan are primary; Australia and Singapore are via search summary of the authority pages; the rest are secondary.

## 4. US sales tax: remote-seller economic nexus in 2026, SaaS taxability, and obligations before thresholds

### Takeaway
Under Wayfair a state may require collection from a seller with no physical presence, but states set their own economic-nexus thresholds; below them (and with no physical presence) a foreign-owned US LLC has no sales-tax registration or collection duty. In 2026 most sales-tax states use USD 100,000 (Alabama and Mississippi 250,000; California and Texas 500,000; New York 500,000 plus 100 transactions; Connecticut 100,000 plus 200 transactions); about 15 jurisdictions still have a 200-transaction alternative, a trap for low-priced subscriptions. SaaS is taxable in about 21 states (Anrok, Aug 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Wayfair: "Physical presence is not necessary to create a substantial nexus"; "the physical presence rule of Quill is unsound and incorrect"; South Dakota's law reached sellers with more than USD 100,000 of sales or 200 or more transactions into the state, and the sellers had no employees or real estate there. — [South Dakota v. Wayfair (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/17-494)
- Stripe: a business can have nexus through physical activity (remote employees, inventory) or economic activity; with nexus you must register to collect; Stripe monitors only economic-nexus thresholds outside the home state. — [Stripe docs, Collect tax in the US](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states.md)
- Texas (example official page): remote sellers (internet, phone or catalog sales) with no Texas location, salespeople or representatives must collect once preceding-12-month receipts exceed USD 500,000; Texas counts gross receipts from taxable and nontaxable sales, and a Texas business location, representatives or in-state property (software included) removes remote-seller status. — via search summary of [Texas Comptroller, Remote Sellers](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/sales/remote-sellers.php)

**2026 economic-nexus thresholds (secondary: Kintsugi 2026 guide, reflects Kentucky's 1 Aug 2026 change)** — [Kintsugi, Economic Nexus by State (2026)](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md)
- USD 100,000 sales only (no transaction test): AZ, CO, FL, ID, IL, IN, IA, KS, KY, LA, ME, MA, MO, NM, NC, ND, OK, PA, SC, SD, TN, UT, WA, WI, WY (and AK, local taxes only).
- USD 250,000 only: AL, MS. USD 500,000 only: CA, TX.
- Both tests required: NY (USD 500,000 and 100 transactions, previous four sales-tax quarters); CT (USD 100,000 and 200 transactions, 12 months ending 30 Sept).
- USD 100,000 or 200 transactions (either): AR, DC, GA, HI, MD, MI, MN, NE, NV, NJ, OH, RI, VT, VA, WV.
- No statewide sales tax: DE, MT, NH, OR (AK has local tax via ARSSTC).
- Recent removals of the 200-transaction prong: Alaska 1 Jan 2025, Utah 1 Jul 2025, Illinois 1 Jan 2026, Kentucky 1 Aug 2026. — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md); earlier removals (South Dakota Jul 2023, Louisiana Aug 2023, Indiana Jan 2024, Wyoming Jul 2024, North Carolina Jul 2024; Colorado, Iowa, North Dakota 2019; Wisconsin 2021) via search summary of [Sales Tax Institute, Wyoming](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/wyoming-removes-200-transaction-threshold-from-economic-nexus-rules), [Avalara, Indiana/Wyoming](https://www.avalara.com/avalara/blog/en/north-america/2024/03/indiana-wyoming-drop-remote-seller-transaction-threshold.html), [Fonoa, Utah](https://www.fonoa.com/blog/utah-repeals-200-transaction-sales-tax-threshold-remote-sellers), [TaxCloud, Illinois](https://taxcloud.com/sales-tax-radar/illinois-eliminates-transaction-threshold-2026), [TaxCloud, Kentucky](https://taxcloud.com/sales-tax-radar/kentucky-economic-nexus-threshold-change-2026/)
- Conflict: one source places Tennessee at USD 500,000 while Kintsugi shows USD 100,000; counts of "sales-only" states vary 17 to 29 across guides. — via search summary of [Eightx](https://eightx.co/blog/economic-nexus-thresholds-2026); [TaxCloud 2026 thresholds PDF (March 2026)](https://taxcloud.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026_Sales_Tax_Nexus_Thresholds_TaxCloud.pdf)
- Whether marketplace (app-store) sales count toward a seller's threshold differs by state (e.g., counted in CA, CT, KY; excluded in AZ, CO, FL). — [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md)

**SaaS taxability (secondary: Anrok, last updated 2026-08-10)** — [Anrok, SaaS sales tax by state](https://www.anrok.com/saas-sales-tax-by-state)
- Taxable (21): AZ, CT, DC, HI, KY, LA, MD, MA, NM, NY, PA, PR, RI, SC, SD, TN, TX, UT, VT, WA, WV.
- Conditional (5): AK (local only), CO (some localities; state-level taxable from 1 Jan 2027), IL (local, e.g., Chicago), IA and OH (depends on B2B/B2C).
- Exempt (26 including California, which the page says becomes taxable from 1 Jan 2027).
- Texas treats SaaS as data processing with 80% of the charge taxable and taxes only use in Texas (via search summary of [Texas Comptroller, data processing](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-127.php)); a 2026 Comptroller draft to change Rule 3.330 targeted marketplace/platform fees, not clearly SaaS.
- Stripe lists special registrations relevant to SaaS: Chicago Personal Property Lease Transaction tax (SaaS), Kentucky and Florida communications-services tax, amusement tax, and gross-receipts taxes. — [Stripe docs, register for sales tax, VAT and GST](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/registering.md)

**Before thresholds / monitoring caveats**
- Stripe monitoring uses Stripe-processed sales (minus refunds), assumes your preset tax code and destination taxation, "can't differentiate between retail and wholesale sales" or marketplace sales; some states (Arizona, Indiana, North Carolina) count nontaxable sales and may require zero or information returns. — [Stripe docs, Monitor your obligations](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring.md)
- Marketplace-facilitator laws exist in every state with a sales tax, shifting collection to the platform. — [Stripe docs, Collect tax in the US](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states.md)
- 24 states are members of the Streamlined Sales Tax Agreement but each state still decides what is taxable. — [Stripe docs, Collect tax in the US](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/united-states.md)

### Inferences
- A foreign-owned LLC is treated like any remote seller: no owner-nationality rule appears in the sources. Before crossing a state's threshold, and absent physical presence (employees, contractors performing services in-state, inventory, in-state property), there is no registration or filing duty, although in-state buyers can owe use tax themselves.
- The 200-transaction states are the practical risk for USD 5 to USD 39 monthly plans: a state with the transaction alternative could be crossed with about 17 monthly subscribers if each monthly charge counts as a separate transaction (counting rules for recurring charges were not verified), even when annual revenue from that state is tiny; in states that count nontaxable sales (AZ, IN, NC per Stripe), a registration plus zero return could be needed even if SaaS is exempt.
- With ForeverPin (QR/link SaaS), Transcript Forge (transcription SaaS) and Hijinx (digital game packs) the SaaS table and a separate "digital goods" classification both matter; Pose Coach iOS sales are collected by Apple as marketplace facilitator.

### Gaps
- Official state revenue pages were checked only for Texas (via search summary) and the Supreme Court opinion; all other thresholds and taxability rows are secondary and dated 2026; verify per state before registering.
- Digital goods/games (Hijinx) and AI/transcription services taxability by state were not researched; Anrok's table covers SaaS only.
- Whether recurring subscription charges count as separate "transactions" in the 200-transaction states was not found.
- Out of scope but adjacent: state income/franchise-tax nexus (Texas franchise tax at USD 500,000 gross receipts per a search summary), gross-receipts taxes (Washington B&O, etc.), local home-rule taxes, notice-and-reporting regimes for non-collecting sellers.

## 5. Stripe Tax (October 2026): pricing, threshold monitoring, calculation and collection, filing and partners, what stays with the seller

### Takeaway
Stripe Tax calculates and collects only where you have added a registration (otherwise it returns zero tax); it monitors thresholds and can register you (US states; EU non-Union OSS via Ireland; Canada simplified; others via Taxually) and file for you only with the Tax Complete subscription. Tax Basic is pay-as-you-go at 0.5% per transaction only in jurisdictions where you are registered; Tax Complete starts at USD 90 per month with additional per-registration and per-filing fees outside the US. You still decide whether to register, remit the money, keep evidence, validate B2B tax IDs, and answer to the authorities.

## 6. Apple App Store and Google Play: do the stores collect and remit VAT/GST/sales tax?

### Inferences
- For Pose Coach sold only through the App Store, a US LLC with a US legal-entity address is a non-resident developer for the Exhibit B footnotes, so Apple collects in the footnoted countries (Indonesia, Korea, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Russia, Japan and others); enrolling as an Uzbek individual or entity would instead leave the developer "solely responsible" in Uzbekistan and the other local-developer carve-outs. ## 7. Cross-cutting synthesis: first sale vs threshold, B2B vs B2C, and a minimal setup while revenue is tiny

### Takeaway
Registrations triggered by the first B2C digital sale: EU (via non-Union OSS), UK, South Korea, India, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Mexico, Chile, Colombia, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia. Registrations triggered at thresholds: US states (mostly USD 100,000), Norway, Switzerland, Australia, New Zealand, Canada, Japan, Singapore and several Asian/African markets. B2B sales to businesses with valid tax numbers are mostly reverse-charged and need evidence, not registration. A minimal low-cost setup is Stripe Tax Basic with monitoring and good evidence capture, and a deliberate decision on whether to register for EU OSS and UK VAT or restrict B2C sales there.

### Cited Findings
- First-sale or no-threshold regimes and threshold regimes with sources: see tables in sections 1 to 4 (EU: [EC Guide](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/a316a98a-3b2b-4991-a9c0-3b5905d369fc_en?filename=OSS_guidelines_en.pdf); UK: [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/register-for-vat); Norway: [Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/vat-and-duties/vat/foreign/e-commerce-voec/register); NZ: [IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-for-overseas-businesses/supplying-remote-services-into-new-zealand); Canada: [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/find-out-need-register/cross-border-threshold-amounts.html); Japan: [NTA](https://www.nta.go.jp/english/taxes/consumption_tax/PFQA_en_foreign.pdf); US: [Kintsugi](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/usa/economic-nexus.md)).
- B2B pattern: EU customer accounts under reverse charge ([EC Explanatory Notes](https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/document/download/78103105-cf0c-4949-9162-daab6f5d11d4_en?filename=explanatory_notes_2015_en_0.pdf)); UK: all-reverse-charge NETP not liable to register ([HMRC VATREG37200](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/vat-registration-manual/vatreg37200)); Norway VOEC is B2C only ([Skatteetaten](https://www.skatteetaten.no/en/business-and-organisation/vat-and-duties/vat/foreign/e-commerce-voec/register)); Canada, NZ and Australia exclude sales to registered businesses from the count ([CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/find-out-need-register/cross-border-threshold-amounts.html), [IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/gst-for-overseas-businesses/supplying-remote-services-into-new-zealand)); Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Russia B2B does not trigger registration ([Stripe docs, Uzbekistan](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/asia-pacific/collect-tax.md?tax-jurisdiction-asia-pacific=uzbekistan)); in the US, Anrok flags SaaS taxability as depending on B2B versus B2C only in Iowa and Ohio, and Stripe's monitoring "can't differentiate between retail and wholesale sales" ([Anrok](https://www.anrok.com/saas-sales-tax-by-state); [Stripe docs, Monitor your obligations](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/monitoring.md)).
- Stripe Tax collects nothing without registrations and its pricing is registration-driven ([Stripe docs, customer addresses](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/customer-locations.md); [Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/understanding-stripe-tax-pricing)).
- Store-sold apps: Apple and Google handle most consumer tax in listed regions ([Apple Exhibits](https://developer.apple.com/support/downloads/terms/exhibits/Exhibits-to-Schedule-2-and-3-English.pdf); [Google DDA](https://play.google/developer-distribution-agreement.html)).

### Inferences
- Minimal setup (zero cash cost): (1) keep Stripe Tax Basic on with monitoring and set the preset tax code; (2) collect a billing address and, for B2B, a tax ID in Checkout, and keep card-country and IP evidence for 10 years; (3) segment B2B (valid VAT/GST number) from B2C in pricing and invoices (reverse-charge wording); (4) maintain a country-by-country revenue ledger to see when thresholds (NOK 50,000, CAD 30,000, NZ$60,000, US state tests including 200-transaction states) approach; (5) decide per jurisdiction in the first-sale group whether to register (EU OSS: self-file via Irish portal; UK: HMRC online) or to restrict B2C sales until revenue justifies the quarterly filing duty.
- The decision "register early vs wait" is a risk/cost trade-off, not a legal exemption: every first-sale jurisdiction above treats the first consumer sale as taxable; late EU registration can force direct registration in each customer's country (EC rule) and backdated UK/Canadian liabilities.
- Unlike the VAT/GST regimes, US state sales tax has no general B2B reverse-charge mechanism in these sources: a business customer does not remove a SaaS sale from tax in most states, so a B2B label helps for VAT/GST evidence but not for US state taxability (inference from the Anrok table showing B2B/B2C dependence in only two states).
- Adding 20 to 30 more small products does not multiply registrations (registrations are per jurisdiction and entity, not per product), but each product needs correct tax codes and its own US taxability analysis.
- Because Apple/Google handle most store sales, the registration burden concentrates on the Stripe-processed web SaaS products.
