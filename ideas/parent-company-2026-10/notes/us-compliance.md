## 1. Form 5472 with pro forma Form 1120 for a foreign-owned disregarded entity

### Takeaway
A single-member US LLC wholly owned by a non-US person is a "foreign-owned U.S. DE", treated as a corporation for section 6038A only. In any year with a reportable transaction (explicitly including contributions to and distributions from the entity) it must file Form 5472 attached to a pro forma Form 1120, by fax or mail only, by the 1120 due date (April 15 for a calendar year; Form 7004 extends it six months to October 15). The penalty is $25,000 per failure plus $25,000 per related party per 30-day period beginning 90 days after IRS notice. Each LLC is its own reporting corporation, so each files separately.

### Cited Findings
- **Who is covered.** A "reporting corporation" includes a 25% foreign-owned US corporation "including a foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity (DE)". A foreign-owned US DE is "a domestic DE that is wholly owned by a foreign person" and, for tax years beginning on or after 1 Jan 2017, "is treated as an entity separate from its owner and classified as a corporation for the limited purposes of the requirements under section 6038A". Instructions are Rev. December 2024; the IRS web version was last reviewed 30 Apr 2026 and still shows Rev. 12/2024. (P) — [IRS Instructions for Form 5472 (HTML)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472); [PDF](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **Pro forma Form 1120.** The DE has no income tax return of its own but must file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached "by the due date (including extensions) of that Form 1120". Only the DE's name and address and items B and E on page 1 are completed; "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" is written across the top. Tax year is the owner's US tax year, or the calendar year if none. (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **How to file.** Fax (300 DPI or higher) to 855-887-7737, or mail to IRS, 1973 Rulon White Blvd, M/S 6112, Attn: PIN Unit, Ogden, UT 84201 (not the normal Form 1120 address). "If you are a foreign-owned U.S. DE, you cannot file Form 5472 electronically." (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **Due date.** Corporate returns have been due April 15 (not March 15) since the IRS changed the date in 2017 (P, incidental statement) — [NM Secretary of State release, 15 Feb 2018](https://www.sos.nm.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/20180215_SOS_Celebrates_Passage_of_SB225.pdf). A CPA firm guide gives the same: April 15, extendable to October 15 with Form 7004 (S) — [GW Carter](https://gwcarter.com/the-ultimate-tax-guide-for-nonresident-llc-owners/).
- **Extension.** A DE requests it on Form 7004, filed by the regular due date, with the Form 1120 code in Part I line 1, "Foreign-owned U.S. DE" written across the top, and faxed/mailed to the same Ogden fax/address (not the regular Form 7004 address). (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf). The automatic extension period is "generally 6 months" (Form 7004 instructions, Rev. December 2025). (P) — [IRS Instructions for Form 7004](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i7004.pdf)
- **When filing is NOT required.** Not required if the entity "had no reportable transactions of the types listed in Parts IV and VI of the form and, in the case of a reporting corporation that is a foreign-owned U.S. DE, also had no reportable transactions of the type listed in Part V". (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **What is reportable.** (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
  - Part IV: monetary transactions with a *foreign* related party where money is the sole consideration (examples on the form: amounts borrowed line 17, other amounts received line 21, amounts loaned line 31, interest paid line 32, other amounts paid line 35).
  - Part V (foreign-owned DE only): "any other transaction, as defined by Regulations section 1.482-1(i)(7) not already entered in Part IV. These transactions include amounts paid or received in connection with the formation, dissolution, acquisition, and disposition of the entity, including contributions to, and distributions from, the entity. Describe these on an attached statement."
  - Part VI: nonmonetary and less-than-full-consideration transactions with a foreign related party.
  - "Transactions with a U.S. related party, however, are not required to be specifically identified in Parts IV, V, and VI." Part IV is not completed for domestic related parties.
  - Small amounts: a transaction or series with a foreign related party totaling $50,000 or less "may be reported as '$50,000 or less.'"
- **Capital contributions, owner draws, loans.** Contributions and distributions are Part V items (quoted above); loans from/to the owner and interest are Part IV items. The instructions do not use the word "draws"; a distribution to the owner is the equivalent. A law-firm note says the same: reportable transactions "include amounts paid or received in connection with the formation, dissolution, acquisition and disposition of the entity, including contributions to and distributions from the entity", and the DE has no income tax return requirement but must file the 5472 for each year with reportable transactions (S) — [Loeb & Loeb, Mar 2021](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2021/03/obligations-to-file-form-5472-for-foreign-owned-disregarded-entities).
- **Owner identification.** Owner's US ID number "if any"; a foreign-owned DE enters the owner's foreign TIN "if any" (else None/N/A). A "reference ID number" (alphanumeric, up to 50 characters, assigned by the filer, used consistently every year, no IRS application needed) is required only where no US identifying number is entered. (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf); [HTML version](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472)
- **Penalties.** $25,000 for failing to file when due "and in the manner prescribed" or to maintain required records; a "substantially incomplete" 5472 counts as a failure to file; if the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notification, an additional $25,000 per related party for each 30-day period (or part) after the 90 days; criminal penalties under sections 7203, 7206, 7207 may also apply. The instructions mention no inflation adjustment. (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **Reasonable cause.** Treas. Reg. 1.6038A-4: $25,000 per taxable year (generally once per related party per year); continuation penalty as above; reasonable-cause relief requires a written statement of all facts relied on, signed under penalties of perjury, filed with the service center where the form is due; a more liberal standard applies to corporations with gross receipts of $20,000,000 or less that lacked knowledge of section 6038A, have limited US presence and contact, and comply promptly; reliance on professional advice counts only if reasonable under all circumstances. (P) — [26 CFR 1.6038A-4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.6038A-4)
- **Separate filing per entity and per related party.** "File a separate Form 5472 for each foreign or U.S. person who is a related party with which the reporting corporation had a reportable transaction" (line 1g). A consolidated Form 5472 is allowed only for members of an affiliated group filing a consolidated income tax return; each member is "a separate reporting corporation subject to a separate $25,000 penalty". Because a DE is classified as a corporation separate from its owner for section 6038A, each foreign-owned LLC is its own reporting corporation (the instructions give no group or aggregate filing for DEs). (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **Owner's own US return.** "Because the domestic DE is generally a transparent entity, the foreign person will include (or continue to include) on Form 1040-NR any of the domestic DE's tax items that are subject to reporting." (P) — [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf)
- **Assessment litigation (Form 5471 §6038(b), not §6038A).** The D.C. Circuit (3 May 2024, 100 F.4th 223) reversed the Tax Court in *Farhy* and held §6038(b) penalties assessable; the Second Circuit followed in *Safdieh* (27 Feb 2026); commentary found no appellate ruling on §6038A(d) (Form 5472) directly; the IRS has assessed Form 5472 penalties routinely; reasonable-cause relief remains available. (SS) — [The Tax Adviser, Jul 2024](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2024/jul/dc-circuit-holds-irs-can-assess-sec-6038b-penalties/); [Current Federal Tax Developments, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2026/2/27/assessability-of-section-6038b-penalties-an-analysis-of-safdieh-v-commissioner); [The Tax Adviser, Oct 2023](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/oct/rethinking-international-penalty-administration.html)

### Inferences
- Any LLC whose owner funds it, pays its bills personally, lends to it, or withdraws money in a year has a Part V/IV item and must file. Paying the state formation fee or registered-agent fee out of the owner's pocket is plausibly a "contribution... in connection with the formation", so a newly formed LLC will usually owe its first 5472 for the formation year (due the following April 15, e.g., LLC formed in 2026, filing due 15 Apr 2027). A truly dormant LLC with zero owner flows has no filing duty under exception 1.
- Revenue from customers (Stripe payouts into the LLC's US bank account) is not a related-party transaction; sweeping cash to the owner's personal account is a distribution (Part V).
- Exposure is per entity per year and unrelated to tax owed. With 20–30 product LLCs, penalty exposure and preparation cost scale linearly; there is no group filing for DEs.
- Transactions between two LLCs owned by the same foreign owner are probably "U.S. related party" transactions (not required to be specifically identified), but the instructions do not say so expressly for sister DEs; line 1g's "each foreign or U.S. person who is a related party" wording adds ambiguity. CPA must confirm.
- Fax/mail only means no electronic acknowledgement; a Tashkent-based filer needs an e-fax (300 DPI) or courier and should keep transmission proof. Today (8 Oct 2026) the extended 2025 deadline for an existing calendar-year LLC is 15 Oct 2026.
- An LLC that checks the box to become a corporation stops being a DE and files a real Form 1120 with the 5472 attached, paying 21% (see section 6); SS-4 instructions confirm single-member entities may elect corporate status on Form 8832/2553 ([IRS SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)).

### Gaps
- No IRS text found on first-time penalty abatement or administrative relief specific to §6038A(d); only the regulatory reasonable-cause route.
- No appellate decision found on whether the §6038A(d) penalty can be assessed administratively; only §6038(b) cases (secondary summaries, not read in full).
- Whether personally paid formation/RA fees must be reported, and how to treat commingled personal/business payments: CPA must confirm.
- Whether sister DEs of one owner are "related parties" whose transactions are reportable: CPA must confirm.

## 2. When does a foreign person's SaaS income through a US LLC become effectively connected income (US trade or business)?

### Takeaway
Because the single-member LLC is transparent, the question is whether the *owner* is engaged in a US trade or business through the LLC. For a SaaS run from Uzbekistan with no US office, employees, or dependent agent, practitioner consensus (secondary) is no US trade or business, services income is foreign-source, and no US income tax arises; US customers, a US-formed LLC, and a US bank/Stripe account do not by themselves change that. A US-based worker or agent, a US fixed place of business, or US-source licensing-type income are the real triggers. No IRS ruling or case on "foreign-owned LLC selling SaaS" was found; the conclusion rests on statute-level rules plus professional commentary.

### Cited Findings
- **IRS definition.** ECI is generally "all United States (U.S.) source income connected with the conduct of" a US trade or business; activities must be "considerable, continuous and regular"; "Selling services, products, or merchandise in the U.S. generally counts as a U.S. trade or business, with certain exceptions"; tests are the asset-use test and the business-activities test; "Foreign persons generally are engaged in a U.S. trade or business when personal services are performed in the U.S."; partners in a partnership engaged in a US trade or business are treated as engaged in it. (P) — [IRS: Effectively connected income](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/effectively-connected-income-eci)
- **Publication 519 (2025).** "Generally, you must be engaged in a trade or business during the tax year" to have ECI and "Whether you are engaged in a trade or business in the United States depends on the nature of your activities"; "If you perform personal services in the United States at any time during the tax year, you are usually considered engaged in a trade or business in the United States"; "If you own and operate a business in the United States selling services, products, or merchandise, you are, with certain exceptions, engaged in a trade or business in the United States"; ECI is taxed at graduated rates, non-ECI US-source income at a flat 30% (or lower treaty rate). (P) — [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf)
- **Foreign-source income becomes ECI only with a US office.** Three kinds of foreign-source income (certain rents/royalties from the active business, banking-type dividends/interest, and certain inventory-type sales outside the US) are ECI only if "You have an office or other fixed place of business in the United States to which the income can be attributed" and it is a material factor. (P) — [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf)
- **Source rules.** "All wages and any other compensation for services performed in the United States are considered to be from sources in the United States"; services income is sourced by where performed (Pub 519 source table: personal services "Where services performed"); "U.S. source income also includes rents or royalties for the use of, or for the privilege of using, in the United States, intangible property such as patents, copyrights, secret processes and formulas, goodwill, trademarks, franchises". (P) — [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf)
- **Office / dependent-agent test.** Treas. Reg. 1.864-7: a fixed place of business is a physical facility ("a place, site, structure, or other similar facility") through which the business is carried on; a dependent agent's office counts only if the agent has authority to "negotiate and conclude contracts in the name of" the foreign person and regularly exercises it, or regularly fills orders from the principal's stock of merchandise; independent agents' offices are not attributed; an employee is generally a dependent agent; using another person's office does not by itself create a fixed place if activity is "relatively sporadic or infrequent"; top-management decisions made in a country do not by themselves create a fixed place of business there. (P) — [26 CFR 1.864-7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.864-7)
- **SaaS is a service, not a royalty (classification).** Final regulations (generally effective for tax years beginning on or after 14 Jan 2025, early adoption allowed back to 14 Aug 2019 on conditions) treat "all 'cloud transactions' ... as the provision of services" (Treas. Reg. 1.861-19); EY notes that "Under existing rules, gross income from the provision of services is sourced to the place where the service is performed". Proposed (not final) cloud sourcing rules would use a three-factor place-of-performance test (personnel, tangible property, intangible property); they would apply only once finalised. (S, Big-4 alert) — [EY Tax News, 21 Jan 2025](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-0280-united-states-treasury-releases-guidance-package-on-classifying-and-sourcing-digital-content-and-cloud-transactions)
- **Practitioner consensus.** A CPA-run guide (updated 25 Sep 2026): ECI requires both US-source income and a US trade or business; "having US customers does not make you ETBUS"; "A US employee almost always qualifies" as a dependent agent; one US-based W-2 employee "almost certainly establishes ETBUS"; a virtual office "does not create a physical place of business"; "If all services are performed abroad, the income is foreign-sourced"; SaaS with no US employees, office or operational presence means no ETBUS and no ECI; it is a "facts-and-circumstances test". It does not address servers. (S, vendor selling compliance services) — [Entity Inc](https://www.entity.inc/blog/effectively-connected-income-eci/)
- **Contrasting conservative view.** Another CPA firm notes foreign-source income is ECI only with a US office or fixed place of business, that a US trade or business can be conducted through a dependent agent with no physical presence needed, and recommends assuming income is connected to a US business if unsure. (S) — [GW Carter](https://gwcarter.com/the-ultimate-tax-guide-for-nonresident-llc-owners/)
- **If ECI exists, the filing consequences.** Nonresident alien with no wages subject to US withholding: Form 1040-NR due June 15 (calendar year); to obtain deductions and credits the return must be filed within 16 months of the due date. (P) — [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Factor map (inference from the sources above, not an IRS checklist):**
  - *US office:* a real US office or fixed facility used in the business is a trigger; a registered-agent or virtual address is not.
  - *Employees / contractors:* personal services performed in the US = US trade or business; a US employee or an agent with authority to bind the LLC to contracts is the classic dependent-agent trigger. Independent service providers without contract-binding authority are weaker triggers but still facts-and-circumstances.
  - *Dependent agents:* any US-based person who negotiates and concludes customer contracts regularly (reseller, sales rep, affiliate with binding authority).
  - *US-located servers:* no IRS authority found treating third-party cloud hosting as a foreign person's fixed place of business under domestic law (1.864-7 speaks of physical facilities and agents). The proposed cloud sourcing rules would count rent/depreciation of tangible property used to deliver the service as a place-of-performance factor, so owning or leasing US servers could shift some service income to US source if those rules are finalised. Non-US hosting (the stated plan) is the conservative choice.
  - *US customers:* not a trigger on their own (consensus); the IRS sentence about "selling ... in the United States" is read as selling through US-based business activity.
- Bottom line: the founder's stated facts (no US residence, office or employees; non-US hosting; product built and run from Tashkent) fit the no-US-trade-or-business pattern; keeping it true requires avoiding US-based staff/agents and US-based contract negotiation.
- If an apparent US trade or business arises, the owner would file Form 1040-NR (June 15), be taxed at graduated rates on net ECI, and may face state filings; the Article IV "representation" language in the 1973 treaty (section 5) may limit US taxation but its reading is unsettled.
- App-store and in-app-purchase revenue (Pose Coach, Hijinx paid packs) is not the same fact pattern as SaaS: the cloud regulations classify cloud access as services, while apps/digital content follow separate digital-content classification rules not analysed here.

### Gaps
- No authoritative IRS/Treasury statement on US servers, US customers or US-formed-entity-only presence for a foreign owner; consensus is from practitioners.
- No analysis of digital-content (app) revenue classification or of platform withholding behaviour (Apple/Google/Stripe treat a US LLC as a US payee via W-9 vs owner W-8BEN): CPA must confirm which form the LLC gives each platform.
- Sales-tax and state income/franchise-tax nexus in customer states (post-Wayfair economic nexus, e.g., SaaS taxability) was not researched.
- The Entity Inc article is vendor content; GW Carter has no date; neither cites case law. CPA/attorney must confirm the no-US-trade-or-business position in writing before relying on it.

## 3. State fees and taxes: Delaware, Wyoming, New Mexico; registered agents; state income/franchise tax

### Takeaway
Delaware's LLC annual tax rose from $300 to $400 (law signed 21 May 2026, retroactive to 1 Jan 2026; first $400 payment due 1 Jun 2027) on top of a required Delaware registered agent; Wyoming costs about $60/yr (license tax minimum) plus an agent; New Mexico appears to have no recurring state fee for an LLC but sources conflict. A registered agent costs roughly $100–$250 per state per year. A no-activity LLC owes no state income or franchise tax in Delaware or Wyoming; New Mexico depends on registration and activity triggers.

### Cited Findings
**Delaware**
- Certificate of Formation filing fee is $110 (certified copy $50). "Annual Taxes in the amount of $400 for the limited liability company are due by June 1 of each year following the close of the calendar year in which their Certificate of Formation becomes effective." Division form instructions, rev. 8/2026. (P) — [Delaware Division of Corporations, LLC Certificate of Formation instructions](https://corpfiles.delaware.gov/LLC_Forms/LLC%20Formation.pdf)
- Division tax page: LLC/LP/GP annual tax $400, due on or before June 1; late payment penalty $200 plus 1.5% interest per month on tax and penalty; "There is no requirement to file an Annual Report" for these entities. (P) — [Delaware Division of Corporations, LLC/LP/GP tax instructions](https://corp.delaware.gov/alt-entitytaxinstructions/)
- **Inconsistency on the Division's own site:** other Division pages still say $300 (fetched 8 Oct 2026) — [How to Form](https://corp.delaware.gov/howtoform/); [Tax FAQ](https://corp.delaware.gov/taxfaq/). The $400 figure is confirmed by the enrolled-law text and the form instructions above.
- HB 400 (153rd General Assembly, 2026): Section 23 amends 6 Del. C. §18-1107(b) from $300 to $400; registered series tax $75 to $100; Sections 9, 16 and 23 effective 1 Jan 2026, all other sections 1 Aug 2026; the bill does not change the LLC certificate-of-formation fee or the corporation franchise-tax minimum under 8 Del. C. §503, and raises the foreign-corporation annual report fee from $125 to $250. (P, engrossed text) — [HB 400 engrossment](https://legis.delaware.gov/json/BillDetail/GenerateHtmlDocumentEngrossment?engrossmentId=37900&docTypeId=6)
- Signed by the Governor 21 May 2026; the higher amounts "generally will first be reflected in annual tax payments due in 2027"; LLP annual fee also rises. (S) — [Withum, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.withum.com/?p=142062). Earlier news: Senate passed on party lines, bill went to the Governor around 13 May 2026, "does not touch fees on corporations" (S) — [WHYY](https://whyy.org/articles/delaware-business-fees-taxes-meyer/).
- Delaware law requires entities to "have and maintain a Registered Agent in the State of Delaware" with a physical street address in Delaware. (P) — [Delaware Division of Corporations, How to Form](https://corp.delaware.gov/howtoform/)
- Delaware income tax: LLC income-tax classification follows federal; a single-member disregarded LLC is treated as a sole proprietorship, "the LLC itself does not pay taxes and does not have to file a return with the State of Delaware"; a multi-member LLC classified as a partnership files Form PRT-RTN. (P) — [Delaware Division of Revenue LLC FAQ](https://revenue.delaware.gov/frequently-asked-questions/limited-liability-company-faqs/). The PRT-RTN instructions limit filing to partnerships with income or loss tied to Delaware; nonresident partners with Delaware-source income file PIT-NON. (SS) — [Delaware PRT-RTN 2025 instructions](https://revenuefiles.delaware.gov/2025/PITForms_Instructions/Instructions/PRT-RTN_2025-01_Instructions.pdf)
- Corporation franchise tax (for the C-corp comparison): authorized-shares method minimum $175 (5,000 shares or fewer; $250 for 5,001–10,000; $85 per additional 10,000), assumed-par-value-capital method minimum $400 ($400 per $1 million of assumed par capital), maximum $200,000, use whichever is lower; due March 1; $200 penalty plus 1.5% monthly interest for late reports. (P) — [Delaware franchise tax calculation](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/); [Tax FAQ](https://corp.delaware.gov/taxfaq/)

**Wyoming** (official pages were located but the host refused the fetch tool's connection; figures below are from search-result summaries of the official pages plus secondary sources)
- Annual report and license tax: the greater of $60 or $0.0002 times assets located and employed in Wyoming (break-even at $300,000); due on the first day of the anniversary month of formation; first report due at the first anniversary; delinquent from the 2nd day of the following month; administrative dissolution if not filed within 60 days after the due date; may be filed up to 120 days early; online card payment adds 2.4% (minimum $1). (SS of official SOS pages) — [WY SOS FAQ](https://sos.wyo.gov/faqs.aspx?root=BUS); [WY Business Center annual report page](https://wyobiz.wyo.gov/Business/AnnualReport.aspx); [WY LLC Act (W.S. 17-29-209, -210)](https://sos.wyo.gov/Forms/WyoBiz/Wyoming_Limited_Liability_Company_Act_and_Close_LLC_Supplement.pdf)
- Older figure: a Feb 2021 SOS fee study cited a $50 floor; a 2021 rule notice proposed $60. (SS) — [WY SOS fee schedule](https://sos.wyo.gov/business/docs/businessfees.pdf) (revision shown as June 2021)
- Formation $100; annual fee $60; "data last verified July 2026". (S) — [GlobalSolo formation-cost table](https://www.globalsolo.global/data/formation-costs)
- No state personal income, corporate income or franchise tax for a Wyoming LLC; the recurring state charge is the report/license tax. (SS, several formation-service/blog pages) — [Nolo](https://www.nolo.com/legal-encyclopedia/annual-report-tax-filing-requirements-wyoming-llcs.html); [Bench](https://www.bench.co/blog/tax-tips/wyoming-need-to-know); [LLC University](https://www.llcuniversity.com/wyoming-llc/taxes/)

## 4. FinCEN beneficial ownership (Corporate Transparency Act) status in 2026

### Takeaway
The March 2025 interim final rule was finalised: FinCEN announced a final rule on 11 Aug 2026, published in the Federal Register on 14 Aug 2026 and effective on publication, that permanently removes the BOI reporting requirement for US companies and US persons. A US-formed LLC with an Uzbek owner has no BOI filing; only foreign-formed entities registered to do business in a US state or tribal jurisdiction still report (non-US persons only).

### Cited Findings
- FinCEN (11 Aug 2026): the final rule "permanently removes the requirement for U.S. companies and U.S. persons to report beneficial ownership information"; foreign entities that are reporting companies still report BOI for foreign individuals; FinCEN will delete previously reported information about US persons; the rule "is effective on its publication in the Federal Register"; it links the rule as public-inspection document 2026-16576. (P) — [FinCEN news release](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-permanently-ends-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirements-millions)
- Federal Register: "Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting Requirement Revision", document 2026-16576, published 14 Aug 2026 (the 91 FR 52508 citation comes from search-result metadata; the page itself redirected to a bot challenge and was not read). (SS) — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16576/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision)
- Mayer Brown (20 Aug 2026): issued 11 Aug, published 14 Aug 2026, "became effective immediately upon publication"; reporting companies are "limited to entities formed under the law of a foreign country" that registered to do business in a US state or tribal jurisdiction; they report their own information and BOI of non-US persons only, not US person beneficial owners or US person company applicants; the March 2025 IFR's exclusion of domestic companies becomes permanent; foreign reporting companies no longer report US-person company applicants; US persons need not update FinCEN-ID information; FinCEN anticipates a single sweep to delete US-person data (data filed after 10 Feb 2027 is outside that process); newly registered foreign entities file within 30 days of registration; non-US persons keep a 30-day update/correction duty; penalty provisions unchanged; the rule does not change banks' Customer Due Diligence obligations. (S, law firm) — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/the-final-chapter-fincen-permanently-eliminates-boi-reporting-requirements-for-us-companies-and-us-persons)

(P) — [Federal Register, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/03/26/2025-05199/beneficial-ownership-information-reporting-requirement-revision-and-deadline-extension). Treasury also published a press release on the final rule (SS) — [Treasury sb0603](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0603).

- No FinCEN BOI report, no annual BOI cost, and no penalty exposure for a Delaware/Wyoming/New Mexico LLC owned by an Uzbek resident. The "foreign owner" fact does not bring a US-formed company back into scope; being formed under foreign law and registering in a US state does.

## 5. Income tax treaty between the US and Uzbekistan (does the 1973 US–USSR treaty apply?)

### Takeaway
There is no separate US–Uzbekistan income tax treaty in force. The IRS treats the 1973 US–USSR Convention on Matters of Taxation as still applying to Uzbekistan (and eight other former republics). It is an unusual, exemption-style treaty: royalties (including computer programs) are exempt in the source state, income from commercial activity is taxable only if derived through a "representation" (an office or place of business), independent personal services are exempt unless over 183 days, and there is no dividends article, so US dividends to an Uzbek resident are taxed at 30%.

### Cited Findings
- **IRS position.** "Uzbekistan is one of the former Soviet Republics which are now covered by the treaty with the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS)"; the only treaty document listed is the "Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR) Income Tax Treaty - 1973", with no technical explanation or protocol (page reviewed 8 Aug 2026). (P) — [IRS: Uzbekistan tax treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/uzbekistan-tax-treaty-documents)
- **Publication 901.** "The U.S.-U.S.S.R. treaty remains in effect" for "Armenia, Azerbaijan, Belarus, Georgia, Kyrgyzstan, Moldova, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan", until new treaties are negotiated and ratified; the treaty tables moved to IRS.gov. (P) — [IRS Publication 901](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p901)
- **IRS A–Z treaty list** (reviewed 3 Jan 2026) lists both Uzbekistan and the USSR; the USSR entry carries "CAUTION Treaty Partially Suspended for Belarus" (not Uzbekistan). (P) — [IRS treaties A–Z](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/united-states-income-tax-treaties-a-to-z)
- **Withholding rates (Table 1, Rev. May 2023; tables page reviewed 23 Feb 2026).** Row "Comm. of Independent States*" (asterisk = the nine countries above): interest 0% (footnote: only interest on credits/loans connected with financing trade between the US and the CIS member, not banking interest; Art. III(1)(g)); dividends 30% (general) and 30% (direct-dividend rate), treaty article "None"; pensions/annuities 30%; royalties 0% in every category (industrial equipment, know-how, patents, film/TV, copyrights; Art. III(1)(a)). Table 1 footnote on branch profits tax: "countries to which the U.S.S.R. treaty applies—30% (no direct dividend rate)". (P) — [IRS Table 1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-lbi/tax-treaty-table-1.pdf); [IRS treaty tables page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/tax-treaty-tables)
- **Treaty text** (signed 20 Jun 1973; in force 29 Jan 1976; effective 1 Jan 1976; Senate advice 15 Dec 1975). Indefinite term; either state may terminate on six months' notice before a calendar year-end (Art. XIV). (P) — [IRS: USSR treaty text](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-trty/ussr.pdf)
  - *Residence (Art. II):* "Resident of the Soviet Union" includes an individual "resident in the Soviet Union for purposes of its tax"; "Resident of the United States" is a corporation created under US law or an individual resident for US tax.
  - *Art. III(1)(a):* "rentals, royalties, or other amounts paid as consideration for the use of or right to use literary, artistic, and scientific works, or for the use of copyrights ... computer programs, trademarks, service marks ... or for knowledge, experience, or skill (know-how)" are "subject to tax only in" the recipient's residence state; Art. III(1)(b) covers gains on sale of such rights; Art. III(1)(e) covers "income from the sale of goods or the supplying of services through a broker, general commission agent or other agent of independent status".
  - *Art. IV(1)–(2):* "Income from commercial activity derived in one Contracting State by a resident of the other Contracting State, shall be taxable in the first Contracting State only if it is derived by a representation", defined for US-source income as "an office or other place of business established in the USA by a resident of the Soviet Union in accordance with the laws and regulations in force in the United States". There is no permanent-establishment, dependent-agent or server concept.
  - *Art. VI(2):* income of an individual from personal services in the other state "may be taxed ... but only if the individual is present ... for a period aggregating more than 183 days in the taxable year" (other categories: students, teachers, trainees capped at 1–5 years).
  - *Art. VII:* the treaty does not restrict a state's right to tax its own citizens. *Art. VIII:* applies only to income from activity conducted "in accordance with the laws and regulations in force". No dividends article, no limitation-on-benefits article.
- **Negotiation history, no new treaty found.** Orbitax reported a Feb 2019 meeting raising a possible first income tax treaty (S); coverage cited in a search summary says the two governments agreed to start talks on investment-protection and double-tax accords in Dec 2021 and again after a Sep 2023 ministerial visit, with no 2025–2026 reporting found (SS) — [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Tax-Treaty-between-the-US-an-36522); [Kun.uz, 14 Dec 2021](https://kun.uz/en/news/2021/12/14/uzbekistan-us-to-enter-into-talks-on-investment-protection-agreement); [Kun.uz, 9 Sep 2023](https://kun.uz/en/news/2023/09/09/uzbekistan-usa-to-sign-new-intergovernmental-agreements). PwC's Uzbekistan tax summary (reviewed Sep 2025) does not list the US among Uzbekistan's treaty partners (SS) — [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties). The 2015 FATCA agreement is an information-exchange arrangement, not a rate-reducing treaty (SS).
- **ITIN and treaty claims.** Form W-7 reason "a": "Certain nonresident aliens must get an ITIN to claim certain tax treaty benefits." (P) — [IRS Form W-7 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7)

### Inferences
- **What it covers for this founder:** (a) a fallback argument that US tax on business ("commercial activity") income requires a US "representation", meaning an office or other place of business; the text has no permanent-establishment, dependent-agent or server concept, so the trigger is a US office or place of business (whether a US LLC or a registered-agent address counts is unsettled; see Gaps); (b) 0% US tax on US-source royalties, including for computer programs, if a software licence were ever characterised as a royalty; (c) a 183-day threshold for personal services performed in the US.
- **What it does not cover:** dividends (30%, no reduction), most interest (30% unless trade financing), pensions, and anything requiring a limitation-on-benefits-style analysis (none exists). So a US C-corp paying dividends to the Uzbek owner gets no treaty relief (section 6).
- For a transparent LLC with no US trade or business the treaty is not needed to avoid US income tax; it is a backstop. The treaty does not remove Form 5472 or state obligations.
- Uzbek-side acceptance of the 1973 convention (and Uzbek taxation of the owner's worldwide income) is outside the US sources reviewed; the Uzbek tax advisor must confirm that Uzbekistan applies the convention and how it credits any US tax.

### Gaps
- No Treasury technical explanation or competent-authority guidance interpreting "representation" for a US LLC owned by an Uzbek resident (does a US-formed LLC, or a registered-agent address, count as an "office or other place of business established in the USA"?). CPA/attorney must confirm before claiming any treaty position (Form 8833 disclosure rules not researched).
- No Uzbek-government source (Ministry of Finance, lex.uz) confirming the 1973 convention's status from Uzbekistan's side.
- Status of any 2025–2026 negotiating rounds not found; news items are 2019–2023 only.

## 6. C-corp alternative: 21% federal tax, 30% dividend withholding, fundraising, Atlas

### Takeaway
A US C-corp pays 21% federal tax on its taxable income (worldwide, not only US-source) and dividends to the Uzbek shareholder bear 30% US withholding with no treaty relief, about 44.7% combined on distributed profit. It also files a full Form 1120 with Form 5472, pays Delaware franchise tax, and an agent. It makes sense mainly for venture/angel fundraising, equity compensation, or a real US presence. Foreign-use services income may get a reduced effective rate (about 14%) from the deduction for foreign-derived deduction eligible income (FDDEI) from 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **21% rate.** "The amount of the tax imposed by subsection (a) shall be 21 percent of taxable income"; subsection (a) taxes "the taxable income of every corporation". (P) — [26 U.S.C. §11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/11)
- **30% on dividends, no treaty reduction.** IRS Table 1 row for the CIS countries incl. Uzbekistan: dividends 30% and 30%, treaty article "None" (see section 5). (P) — [IRS Table 1](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-lbi/tax-treaty-table-1.pdf). Non-ECI US-source income of a foreign person is taxed at a flat 30% unless a treaty lowers it. (P) — [IRS Publication 519 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf). FDAP paid to foreign persons falls under Chapter 3 withholding (Forms 1042/1042-S) at 30%. (P) — [IRS partnership withholding page](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/partnership-withholding)
- **Form 5472 for a C-corp.** A 25% foreign-owned US corporation is a "reporting corporation": it files Form 5472 with its regular Form 1120 whenever it has a reportable transaction with a related party (dividends, loans, services, etc. between it and the foreign shareholder); the same $25,000 penalty regime applies. (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **FDDEI deduction.** For tax years beginning after 31 Dec 2025 the deduction is "33.34 percent of the foreign-derived deduction eligible income" (previously FDII); FDDEI is income from property sold for foreign use or "services ... provided to any person, or with respect to property, not located within the United States"; a taxable-income limitation applies. (P) — [26 U.S.C. §250](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/250). Secondary summaries put the effective federal rate on FDDEI at about 14% and note proposed regulations (REG-117130-25, 20 Aug 2026) on related exclusions (SS) — [HCVT](https://www.hcvt.com/alertarticle-Tax-Incentives-for-Exporters-After-OBBBA-IC-DISC-and-FDDEI); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20250714-international-tax-provisions-of-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-obbba).
- **Stripe Atlas.** $500 one-time setup fee (includes government fees and the first year of registered agent services); registered agent $100/yr thereafter; entity types offered: C corporation (listed first, "Often used by startups"), limited liability company ("Often used by small businesses"), and Subsidiary; "company tax ID"; "Startups in over 175 countries have chosen Atlas"; no country restrictions or eligibility criteria listed and Uzbekistan not mentioned; "within two business days, you'll be incorporated". The page does not name a default entity. (P, vendor's own page, fetched 8 Oct 2026) — [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas)
- **Atlas EIN timing (secondhand).** Rho says EIN timing depends on a US Social Security number: 1–3 business days with one, 15–45 days without. (SS) — [Rho Atlas review](https://www.rho.co/blog/stripe-atlas-review)
- **Fundraising.** Investors' fund documents and LP tax constraints make a Delaware C-corp the near-universal vehicle for venture rounds; an LLC is not legally barred but adds friction; foreign founders still raise on Delaware C-corps; converting an LLC later is possible but costs money and should precede fundraising. (SS, mixed commercial sources; one guide says a C-corp adds needless complexity if there is no outside capital, no equity compensation, no US employees and minimal US revenue) — [Stripe Atlas company-types doc](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/company-types); [Slidebean](https://slidebean.com/blog/startup-incorporation); [Terms.law](https://terms.law/DE-inc/delaware-corp-nonresidents.html)
- **Delaware costs.** Franchise tax rules above (min $175 / $400, $200,000 cap, due March 1); the 2026 fee law leaves the corporation franchise-tax minimum unchanged and raises the foreign-corporation annual report fee (domestic annual report fee not stated in the bill text). (P) — [Delaware franchise tax calculation](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/); [HB 400 engrossment](https://legis.delaware.gov/json/BillDetail/GenerateHtmlDocumentEngrossment?engrossmentId=37900&docTypeId=6)

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic (not sourced):** distributing after-tax profit: 1 − (1 − 0.21) × (1 − 0.30) = 44.7% combined US tax. If foreign-use services income qualifies for FDDEI at about 14%: 1 − (1 − 0.14) × 0.70 ≈ 39.8%. On $60,000 of annual profit (about $5,000 MRR level), 21% = $12,600 corporate tax and 30% of the remaining $47,400 = $14,220 dividend withholding, total about $26,820. A transparent LLC with no US trade or business pays $0 US income tax on the same profit (Uzbek tax separate).
- A C-corp is taxed on worldwide income even if there is no US trade or business, so it removes ECI uncertainty at the price of 21% on everything; the LLC approach pays 0% but carries the ECI-position risk (section 2).
- Salary or service fees paid by the C-corp to the founder for work performed in Uzbekistan would be deductible to the corporation and are foreign-source to the owner (services income sourced by place of performance, Pub 519), potentially reducing the dividend layer; Uzbek tax and transfer-pricing consequences are outside this research. CPA must confirm.
- When a C-corp makes sense: outside equity financing (VC/angel/accelerator), stock options for employees or partners, planned US employees or office (where ECI/US presence would arise anyway), or a goal of retaining earnings in the US entity at 21%. Atlas simplifies this path (C-corp is listed first; the Atlas page shows one $500 setup price and does not state a different price by entity type); the real cost difference between a C-corp and an LLC is the full Form 1120, Delaware franchise tax, and the tax layers above.
- An existing LLC can check the box to be a corporation (Form 8832) later; the SS-4 instructions describe the election.

### Gaps
- Delaware domestic-corporation annual report fee (believed $50), Atlas's default share structure and whether Atlas serves Uzbekistan-resident founders are not confirmed; Atlas's page lists no exclusions.
- FDDEI qualification for an Uzbek-owned US corporation selling SaaS (foreign-use proof, cloud sourcing) and its interaction with the proposed cloud-sourcing rules: CPA must confirm; secondary summaries only on the 14% figure.
- No source on Uzbek taxation of dividends or on Uzbek treatment of a US corporation.
- No verified Form 1042/1042-S due dates or penalties in this research.

## 7. Multi-member LLC consequences for foreign partners (Form 1065, 8804/8805, Section 1446)

### Takeaway
A two-member LLC is a partnership by default. A US-formed (domestic) partnership files Form 1065 every year, even if all partners are foreign (March 15, six-month extension; $255 per partner per month penalty, up to 12 months), and issues Schedules K-1 (and possibly K-2/K-3). Forms 8804/8805/8813 and Section 1446 withholding (37% for individuals, 21% for corporate partners) apply only if the partnership has effectively connected income allocable to a foreign partner; FDAP allocable to foreign partners goes through the Chapter 3 withholding regime. Form 5472 is replaced by the 1065 regime.

### Cited Findings
- **Default classification.** Domestic LLC with two or more members is a partnership by default; single-member is disregarded. (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Who must file Form 1065.** "every domestic partnership must file Form 1065, unless it neither receives income nor incurs any expenditures treated as deductions or credits for federal income tax purposes"; LLCs classified as partnerships have the same requirement; a foreign partnership is one "not created or organized in the United States", so a Delaware LLC is domestic regardless of partner nationality. (P) — [IRS Instructions for Form 1065 (2025)](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1065.pdf)
- **Due date and extension.** 15th day of the 3rd month after year end (March 15 for calendar-year; Form 7004 for an extension, filed by the regular due date). (P) — [IRS Form 1065 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1065.pdf); extension length six months per [Form 7004 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i7004.pdf)
- **Penalties.** Late/incomplete return: $255 per month or part of a month (maximum 12 months) multiplied by the number of persons who were partners during any part of the year, unless reasonable cause; failure to furnish or to include correct information on a Schedule K-1 (and K-3): $340 per schedule (intentional disregard: $680 or 10% of items). (P, instructions for tax year 2025) — [IRS Form 1065 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1065.pdf)
- **Foreign partner TIN.** Enter the partner's SSN or ITIN on the K-1 for individuals; "Foreign partners without a U.S. identifying number should be notified by the partnership of the necessity of obtaining a U.S. identifying number. Certain aliens who aren't eligible to obtain SSNs can apply for an ITIN on Form W-7." (P) — [IRS Form 1065 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1065.pdf). W-7 exception 1 lets a partner in a US or foreign partnership that invests in the US apply with a copy of the partnership agreement (excerpt incomplete). (P) — [IRS Form W-7 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7)
- **Partnership engaged in US trade or business.** A member of a partnership engaged in a US trade or business at any time in the year is treated as engaged in it. (P) — [IRS Publication 519](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/p519.pdf)
- **Section 1446(a) withholding.** Domestic or foreign partnerships with ECI must withhold on ECTI allocable to foreign partners: 37% for non-corporate foreign partners, 21% for corporate; Form 8813 payments, Form 8804 annual return, Form 8805 per partner (given to partners even if no tax paid); non-ECI income is FDAP under Forms 1042/1042-S at 30% (treaty may reduce). (P, page reviewed 21 Jul 2026) — [IRS partnership withholding](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/partnership-withholding)
- **Forms 8804/8805/8813 mechanics.** Required of "Every partnership ... that has effectively connected gross income allocable to a foreign partner"; due the 15th day of the 3rd month after year end (6th month if books and records are kept outside the US and Puerto Rico); Form 8813 installments on the 15th day of the 4th, 6th, 9th and 12th months; Form 7004 extends the filing but not the payment; late-filing penalty 5% of unpaid tax per month up to 25%; separate from Form 1065. (P, Rev. Jan 2026) — [IRS Instructions for Forms 8804, 8805, 8813](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8804.pdf)
- **Section 1446(f).** A transferee must withhold 10% of the amount realised on a transfer of a partnership interest if any gain would be ECI under section 864(c)(8). (P) — [IRS partnership withholding](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/partnership-withholding)
- **Form 5472 vs partnership.** Form 5472 reporting corporations are 25% foreign-owned US *corporations* (including DEs); a partnership is not a corporation unless it elects. (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf); a commentary guide also says multi-owner partnership-taxed LLCs are carved out of the 5472 requirement (SS) — [Terms.law](https://terms.law/DE-inc/delaware-corp-nonresidents.html)
- **State.** Delaware partnership return (PRT-RTN) only where there is Delaware-source income (section 3); New Mexico pass-through entities have a return trigger if "registered in the state" (section 3).

### Inferences
- Adding a partner to an existing single-member foreign-owned LLC changes the filing regime: the 5472/pro forma 1120 (April 15) is replaced by Form 1065/K-1 (March 15), with a per-partner late penalty (e.g., 2 partners × $255 × 12 = up to $6,120; 3 partners up to $9,180) and K-1 penalties of $340 each. Tax consequences of the conversion itself (deemed transactions) were not researched.
- If no partner has ECI, there is no 1446(a) withholding and no Forms 8804/8805/8813; if the no-US-trade-or-business position fails, the partnership must withhold 37%/21% quarterly and file 8804/8805, a significant administrative and cash-flow burden, and each partner is treated as engaged in the US business.
- K-1 identification friction: the instructions expect an SSN/ITIN; a partner with no US filing requirement may not qualify for an ITIN (W-7 requires a recognised reason). How a partnership with only foreign-source income and non-ITIN foreign partners handles the K-1 TIN field (and avoids the $340 penalty) needs a CPA answer.
- Per-product LLCs with different partners would multiply these filings; one holding LLC with product "divisions" would reduce filings but changes liability and partner economics.

### Gaps
- No primary text found on what to enter in the K-1 TIN field for a foreign partner who cannot obtain an ITIN (the common "FOREIGNUS" convention was not found in the 2025 instructions).
- K-2/K-3 requirements and exceptions for foreign partners were not read in detail (the instructions have a "domestic filing exception" and a "Schedule K-2 filing exception" section).
- Form 1042/1042-S due dates and penalties (for FDAP allocated to foreign partners) not researched.

## 8. EIN application for a foreign owner without SSN/ITIN; is an ITIN ever needed?

### Takeaway
A foreign owner applies for the LLC's EIN on Form SS-4 by fax (about 4 business days), phone (267-941-1099, only if no US residence or principal place of business), or mail (about 4 weeks); the IRS online application is not available. Enter "foreign" or N/A on line 7b. An ITIN is not needed to get the EIN or to file Form 5472 (a reference ID number substitutes); it is needed only if the owner has to file a US return, claims certain treaty benefits, or is a partner who must give a US ID on a K-1.

### Cited Findings
- **Line 7b.** If the responsible party has no SSN or ITIN and cannot obtain one, enter "foreign" or N/A; an entry is required. The responsible party must be an individual (the person who ultimately owns or controls the entity) unless the applicant is a government entity. (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Phone.** 267-941-1099 (not toll-free), 6:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m. Eastern, Monday–Friday; available only to applicants with "no legal residence, principal place of business, or principal office or agency in the U.S. or its territories"; if the representative asks, mail or fax the signed SS-4 within 24 hours; "The IRS no longer issues EINs by telephone for domestic taxpayers." (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Fax.** For applicants with no legal residence or principal place of business in the US: 855-215-1627 (within the US) or 304-707-9471 (outside the US); processing "generally within 4 business days"; Fax-TIN available 24/7; include the applicant's fax number for the return fax. (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Mail.** IRS, Attn: EIN International Operation, Cincinnati, OH 45999; submit at least 4–5 weeks ahead; EIN received "in approximately 4 weeks". (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Online.** Not available to applicants without a US legal residence, principal place of business or principal office. (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Third-party designee.** An authorised designee may answer questions and receive the EIN; the EIN is released by the method used to apply and the notice is mailed to the taxpayer; the designee's authority ends when the EIN is assigned. (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **Foreign-owned DE entries.** Line 8a/8b: LLC and number of members; line 9a: choose "Other" and enter "Foreign-owned U.S. disregarded entity-Form 5472" for a DE that files Form 5472. (P) — [IRS Form SS-4 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iss4)
- **International applicants.** Another IRS form instruction also points international applicants without a US place of business to 267-941-1099. (P) — [IRS Instructions for Forms 8804, 8805, 8813](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i8804.pdf)
- **ITIN not needed for the 5472.** The foreign owner's US ID is entered "if any"; otherwise a filer-assigned reference ID number (and FTIN if any). (P) — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i5472.pdf)
- **When an ITIN is needed (Form W-7).** Reasons include: (a) certain nonresident aliens must get an ITIN to claim certain tax treaty benefits; (b) a nonresident alien filing a US return to report ECI or to claim a refund; passive-income, partnership and other "exceptions" allow applying without a return. (P) — [IRS Form W-7 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/iw7)
- **Partner TIN.** Form 1065 instructions expect SSN/ITIN for individual partners (section 7). (P) — [IRS Form 1065 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/i1065.pdf)

### Inferences
- Timeline: phone gives the fastest route if the LLC's principal place of business is genuinely outside the US (a US registered-agent or virtual address may complicate eligibility); fax is "generally within 4 business days" per the IRS; mail is about 4 weeks; real-world delays and rejections are common for foreign-owned entities but are not documented by the IRS (anecdotal: Rho says 15–45 days without an SSN for Atlas EINs).
- A foreign-owned single-member LLC with no ECI and no treaty claim needing a US TIN has no ITIN requirement. An ITIN becomes necessary if a 1040-NR is required (ECI position fails), if certain treaty benefits are claimed, or if the partnership/K-1 route insists on a US ID.
- EIN is a prerequisite for the pro forma 1120/5472 filing (Loeb note) and for banking and Stripe onboarding.

## 9. Yearly obligation list and costs: single-member LLC vs multi-member LLC vs C-corp (synthesis)

### Takeaway
For a solo Uzbek owner with no US presence, the foreign-owned single-member LLC is the lowest-cost structure: no federal income tax if no US trade or business, one federal information filing (Form 5472 + pro forma 1120, $25,000 penalty if missed) and about $500–$650/yr in Delaware state fee plus agent (about $160–$310 in Wyoming; about $100–$250 in New Mexico if no LLC report applies), with no FinCEN BOI report. A multi-member LLC trades the 5472 for a Form 1065 regime and a partner-ID problem; a C-corp adds 21% on all profit and 30% on dividends and a heavier return.

**Single-member LLC yearly calendar (calendar-year entity)**
- 1st of the formation month: Wyoming annual report/license tax ($60 minimum) if the LLC is a Wyoming LLC.
- Apr 15: Form 5472 + pro forma 1120 (or Form 7004 by Apr 15, extending to Oct 15).
- Jun 1: Delaware LLC annual tax ($400) if the LLC is a Delaware LLC.
- Jun 15: Form 1040-NR (only if the owner has ECI or taxable US-source income and no US wages).
- Ongoing: keep books and records (the record-maintenance failure carries the same $25,000 penalty).

**Other entity types, for comparison:** multi-member LLC: Mar 15 Form 1065/K-1s (Form 7004 to Sep 15), Forms 8804/8805 by the same date and Form 8813 installments on the 15th of months 4, 6, 9 and 12 only if there is ECI; C-corp: Mar 1 Delaware franchise tax and annual report, Apr 15 Form 1120 + Form 5472 (Oct 15 if extended), plus Forms 1042/1042-S when dividends are paid.

### Inferences
- **Scale math (arithmetic from cited unit costs, not a quote):** per product LLC: Delaware $400 + $125 agent = $525 plus 5472 prep; Wyoming $60 + $125 = $185; New Mexico $0 + $125 = $125. For 25 LLCs: about $13,125 (DE), $4,625 (WY), $3,125 (NM) per year before federal filing prep, and 25 pro forma 1120/5472 sets (and 25 × $25,000 exposure if missed).
- At about $5K MRR across products, the filing/agent overhead of the LLC route is a small share of revenue; the C-corp's tax layers (about $26,800 on $60,000 of profit, if distributed) dominate.
- The choice of formation state is driven by recurring fees (Wyoming/New Mexico cheapest) and ecosystem/familiarity (Delaware, Atlas); none of the three adds US income tax for a no-activity LLC.
- The decisive facts are all "no US footprint": no US employees/agents/office, non-US hosting, clean separation of owner and LLC money flows, and timely 5472 filings.
