# Parent-company jurisdictions for an Uzbekistan-resident solo founder selling global SaaS through a direct Stripe account

*Researched 2026-10-08. Labels: (P) primary = regulator, statute, treaty text or the vendor's own help page; (S) secondary = formation provider, accountancy or press page, or a search-result summary. Via search summary means the page surfaced in a search result but was not opened in full. Every price carries the date or effective date of its source. Research only: no account opened, no form submitted, nobody contacted. Several vendor help pages return 403 to the fetch tool; where I read a help-centre JSON endpoint on the vendor's own domain instead, I say so. The earlier evidence file (2026-09-26, Payment rails section) predates the decision to keep Stripe; everything reused from it was re-checked and is flagged where it changed.*

## 1. Which candidates are on Stripe's supported-countries list, and what does Stripe require of the people behind the account?

### Takeaway
All eight candidate jurisdictions (US, UK, Estonia, UAE, Lithuania, Ireland, Singapore, Hong Kong) are on stripe.com/global today; Uzbekistan is not. Stripe's own help article for Estonian e-Residency companies says the individuals associated with an account must live in a Stripe-supported country and sends residents of other countries to Stripe Atlas (a US company), so a US entity formed through Atlas is the only structure Stripe explicitly offers a Tashkent resident; every other jurisdiction needs written confirmation from Stripe before money is spent.

### Cited Findings
- stripe.com/global lists 51 countries/regions (read 2026-10-08): the US, UK, Estonia, UAE, Lithuania, Ireland, Singapore and Hong Kong are all present, as are Latvia, Poland, Bulgaria, Cyprus and Malta; Uzbekistan is absent. India and Indonesia are marked as previews; Cote d'Ivoire, Ghana, Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa are an extended network run through Paystack; for businesses outside the list Stripe mentions only Treasury with stablecoins and says payments are not supported yet. — [Stripe global](https://stripe.com/global) (P, read through a summarizing fetch tool, not raw HTML)
- The 2026-09-26 evidence file reached the same conclusion (Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Georgia, Kyrgyzstan, Armenia missing; UAE present); confirmed again today. — [branch-c-founder-fit.md, Payment rails](/Users/max/Projects/10x-ws/workbench/career/engineering/wow-two/wow-two-ws/workbench/ventures/10x-ventures/ideation/runs/2026-09-26-one-job-ladder/branch-c-founder-fit.md) (local, reused and re-verified)
- Stripe's help article on Estonian e-Residency companies: Stripe supports them, but the place of residence of the individuals associated with the account must be in a country Stripe currently supports; a person living elsewhere may be able to form a US company through Stripe Atlas instead. No date shown on the article. — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/supporting-companies-that-registered-in-estonia-through-e-residency) (P)
- Atlas: $500 covers Delaware incorporation, state fees and the first year of registered-agent service; $100 per year afterwards; entity choices are C corporation, LLC or subsidiary; Stripe refunds the fee if it cannot support the business; the page offers a Stripe account and a Stripe Treasury business account (checking by Fifth Third Bank); it names no eligible or excluded countries and says founders in 175+ countries have used Atlas. — [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas) (P)
- Atlas docs: Delaware incorporation usually takes 1-2 business days; Atlas files the EIN application; without an SSN the EIN takes 10-30 business days; Atlas cannot guarantee that Stripe Payments will approve the business. — [Atlas signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup) (P)
- Atlas docs: after incorporating, check whether Stripe Treasury is available for the account; if not, apply to a third-party US bank or fintech. — [Atlas business bank accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/payments-business-bank) (P)
- Stripe Treasury (for platforms) is US-only; it asks for a US-based business and an account representative at a US address, while owners residing in certain other countries are permissible but may get enhanced review. This is the platform product; the Atlas Treasury eligibility terms for a Tashkent-resident owner were not found. — [Treasury requirements](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/requirements) (P)
- Stripe US verification: non-US-resident representatives supply a national ID or verification document instead of an SSN/ITIN (owners only above $500K payments volume); no country restriction is mentioned. — [Stripe support, US verification FAQs](https://support.stripe.com/questions/2023-24-updates-to-us-verification-requirements-faqs) (P)
- Stripe UAE: owners residing outside the UAE need only a passport; resident foreigners need passport, Emirates ID and visa. — [Stripe support, UAE ownership](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-ownership-verification-requirements) (P). Via search summary of Stripe's other UAE pages: free-zone establishments are supported when the trade licence was issued inside the UAE; the owner of a free-zone establishment must complete activation; payouts go only to banks on Stripe's supported UAE list, and a free-zone establishment may be paid to a personal account if the name matches the owner. — [UAE business verification](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-business-verification-requirements), [Supported banks in the UAE](https://support.stripe.com/questions/supported-banks-in-the-uae) (P, not opened in full)
- Stripe Services Agreement: the Stripe Account Country is the country of the business address; bank accounts must be held in a country Stripe approves; a High-Risk Jurisdiction clause exists; the first 100,000 characters I read contain no residency rule for representatives or owners. — [Stripe SSA](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa) (P, partly read)
- A third-party comparison says Stripe can reject some non-resident owners of US LLCs at verification based on where they live. — [businessanywhere.io](https://businessanywhere.io/stripe-paypal-non-resident-us-llcs-what-to-know/) (S, via search summary)
- A UK formation provider argues that where the business is registered matters more than where the founder lives, and also warns Stripe may probe where the business is really run; Stripe's own wording focuses on where the associated individuals live. — [IncorpUK](https://incorpuk.com/blog/opening-stripe-as-a-non-resident-a-practical-guide-to-setting-up-stripe-abroad/) (S, via search summary)

### Inferences
- The Estonia article is the only Stripe text found that states the individuals-must-reside-in-a-supported-country rule; until Stripe says otherwise in writing, treat it as a general rule that also covers UK, UAE, Lithuania and the rest. Under that reading Atlas is the only sanctioned route for a Tashkent resident.
- Atlas's silence on country eligibility means Uzbekistan's eligibility is unconfirmed; the refund clause covers Stripe being unable to support the business, which is not the same as no bank being willing to open an account.

### Gaps
- No Stripe page found lists Atlas-eligible countries, so Uzbekistan eligibility for Atlas, Stripe Payments on an Atlas LLC, and Atlas-linked Treasury is unconfirmed.
- Whether the Estonia residency rule is enforced for UK, UAE or other accounts is not stated on any page I found.
- stripe.com/global was read via a summarizing tool; a human should glance at the live page.

## 2. US LLC baseline (Delaware via Atlas, Wyoming, New Mexico)

### Takeaway
A foreign-owned single-member Delaware LLC formed through Atlas costs $500 up front (first-year registered agent included), then roughly $100 registered agent plus a Delaware LLC tax that rose from $300 to $400 under HB 400, with no resident director, no audit and no US federal income tax expected when there is no US trade or business, but a mandatory annual Form 5472 plus pro forma 1120 ($25,000 penalty). Wyoming ($100 plus $60 a year) and New Mexico ($50, registered agent only) are cheaper on paper but sit outside Atlas, and the real obstacle is banking: Mercury's official prohibited-countries list now includes Uzbekistan.

### Cited Findings
- Atlas cost and inclusions: $500 setup including first-year registered agent, then $100 a year; Delaware only. — [Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas), [Atlas signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup) (P)
- Delaware requires a registered agent; Atlas's agent renews at $100 a year; the Atlas address can be a home address or a partner virtual address. — [Atlas signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup) (P)
- Delaware LLC annual tax: $300 flat, due 1 June, late penalty $200 plus 1.5% monthly interest, no annual report for LLCs (Atlas page text predates HB 400). — [Atlas business taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes) (P)
- Delaware HB 400, signed 21 May 2026, raises the annual tax on LLCs, LPs and GPs from $300 to $400 effective 1 January 2026 (first shows on tax bills due in 2027); most filing-fee changes take effect 1 August 2026. — [Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/delaware-businesses-face-increased-annual-taxes-and-filing-costs/) (S, CPA firm)
- Delaware certificate of formation: $110 in post-August-2026 summaries; older guides say $90; not verified at corp.delaware.gov (its fee-schedule PDF was linked but unreadable to me). — [InCorp](https://www.incorp.com/resources/knowledge-base/delaware-business-filing-fee-increase) (S, via search summary); [corp.delaware.gov fee page](https://corp.delaware.gov/fee/) (P, no LLC figures shown)
- Wyoming: $100 for Articles of Organization; annual report licence tax is the greater of $60 or 0.0002 of Wyoming assets, due in the anniversary month. — [Rho](https://www.rho.co/blog/how-to-start-an-llc-in-wyoming), [StartBusinessByState](https://startbusinessbystate.com/wyoming/llc/) (S, via search summary)
- New Mexico: $50 for Articles of Organization; registered agent required; commercial agents about $50-$300 a year; most guides say no periodic report, while Topograph and Discern describe a $20 report every three years (HB 281) and another recap says HB 281 never passed - unresolved. — [Ezel](https://ezel.ai/surveys/llc-annual-report-requirements/new-mexico), [Topograph](https://www.topograph.co/guides/business-registers-in-new-mexico-united-states) (S, via search summary)
- A foreign-owned US disregarded entity has no income tax return of its own but must file a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 (fax or mail to Ogden, cannot e-file); the penalty is $25,000, plus $25,000 per related party for each 30 days it continues after 90 days from IRS notice; reportable items include formation contributions and distributions. — [IRS Form 5472 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472) (P)
- Practitioners: a foreign-owned LLC with no US office, staff or agent and work done abroad generally owes no US income tax, but SaaS income sourcing is unsettled; a January 2025 proposal would apportion cloud-service income by where staff, IP and equipment sit (finalisation status not confirmed). — [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/1/new-irs-regulations-address-cross-border-cloud-computing-and-digital-infrastructure-transactions), [terms.law](https://terms.law/2024/12/05/how-the-irs-taxes-foreign-owned-u-s-entities-selling-online/) (S, via search summary)
- A US C corporation pays a flat 21% of taxable income federally and Delaware C corporations owe franchise tax and an annual report. — [26 U.S.C. 11](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/11) (P), [Atlas business taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes) (P)
- Atlas lists a multi-member LLC as filing a partnership return (due 15 March). — [Atlas business taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes) (P)
- FinCEN: domestic US companies are exempt from Beneficial Ownership Information reporting (final rule, page updated 11 August 2026, effective 14 August 2026); only foreign-formed entities registered to do business in a US state remain covered. — [FinCEN BOI](https://www.fincen.gov/boi) (P)
- EIN: Stripe's bank guidance says the IRS takes 1-15 business days with a US address, phone and SSN and 15-50 business days without (Atlas's own docs say 10-30). — [Stripe support, US bank account](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-get-a-us-bank-account) (P)
- Mercury's official Prohibited countries article lists Uzbekistan among 48 prohibited countries (plus 4 sanctioned regions); the rule is by country of residence, not citizenship, and citizens living elsewhere may be eligible pending review; the article was last edited 2026-10-06 (read via the vendor's help-centre JSON endpoint because the HTML page returns 403). The company must be US-registered; non-US founders are otherwise accepted; registered-agent, PO box and UPS Store principal addresses are refused. — [Mercury prohibited countries](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580), [Mercury eligibility](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060) (P)
- Relay's Prohibited Countries page (31 countries) does not include Uzbekistan and ties the rule to citizenship or residency; page date not shown. — [Relay](https://relayfi.com/hc/en-us/articles/10239600121748-Prohibited-Countries/) (P)
- Wise: the list of countries where customers can hold money does not include Uzbekistan; a business needs a physical trading address in a Wise-supported country and trading addresses in unsupported countries are refused; the US business-verification page sets no owner-residence rule. — [Wise hold money](https://wise.com/help/articles/2813542/where-do-i-need-to-live-to-hold-money-with-wise), [Wise address rules](https://wise.com/help/articles/2969315/how-does-wise-verify-my-business-address), [Wise US verification](https://wise.com/help/articles/2953878/verify-your-us-business) (P)

### Inferences
- The relevant US entity is the LLC, not Atlas's default-listed C corporation: a C corporation carries 21% federal tax plus Delaware franchise tax and a second layer on dividends, while a foreign-owned disregarded LLC with no US presence is the structure practitioners describe as federally untaxed.
- Annual fixed cost for a Delaware Atlas LLC is about $100 registered agent plus $400 state tax (assuming the first bill falls due the June after formation, i.e. 1 June 2027 for a company formed in late 2026) plus a CPA for Form 5472/1120 (no price found); a New Mexico LLC could run on the registered-agent fee alone and Wyoming on $60 plus the agent.
- Within the US, Delaware-through-Atlas ranks first because it is the path Stripe itself names for unsupported-country founders; New Mexico second on cost; Wyoming third.
- Adding a partner later turns the single-member LLC into a partnership (Form 1065 route) and ends the foreign-owned disregarded-entity filing regime; per-product LLCs would multiply the $500-a-year fixed cost per entity.
- Formation is not the obstacle (fully online, no local director or secretary); the bank and Stripe-verification step is. Mercury (the usual choice) and Wise are unavailable to a Tashkent resident on current published rules; Relay and Atlas-linked Treasury are the unconfirmed remaining paths.

### Gaps
- Delaware formation fee unverified on the state site ($90 vs $110 conflict); Wyoming and New Mexico fees come from secondary sources; New Mexico's annual-report status is unresolved.
- No CPA price found for Form 5472/pro forma 1120 preparation.
- Federal tax treatment of SaaS income for a foreign-owned LLC rests on practitioner commentary, and the status of the January 2025 cloud-sourcing proposal is unknown.
- US-Uzbekistan income tax treaty status unverified (PwC's Uzbek treaty table does not list the US; the IRS treaty list page returned 404).
- Whether Relay, Payoneer, Airwallex or another provider onboards a Tashkent-resident owner of a US LLC is not verified; Stripe Treasury eligibility for such an owner is unknown.
- The 2024 press lists of Mercury countries did not include Uzbekistan; the October 2026 official list does, which supersedes the unverified note in the 2026-09-26 file.

## 3. UK private limited company (Companies House, ID verification, tax, place of management)

### Takeaway
Since 1 February 2026 Companies House charges GBP 100 to incorporate online and GBP 50 for the annual confirmation statement, and a non-UK director can verify identity online with any biometric passport; but a UK company pays 19-25% corporation tax on retained profit and, if run from Tashkent, risks dual residence because the 2018 UK-Uzbekistan protocol replaced the effective-management tie-breaker with a mutual-agreement clause that denies treaty benefits if the two tax authorities do not agree.

### Cited Findings
- Fees effective 1 February 2026: incorporation GBP 100 online (was GBP 50), confirmation statement GBP 50 online (was GBP 34), digital voluntary strike-off GBP 13. — [GOV.UK news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/companies-house-fees-are-changing-from-1-february-2026) (P)
- Fee table: paper incorporation GBP 124, paper confirmation statement GBP 110, same-day incorporation GBP 156; page updated 25 September 2026. — [Companies House fees](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/companies-house-fees/companies-house-fees) (P)
- Corporation tax: 19% small profits rate up to GBP 50,000, 25% main rate above GBP 250,000, marginal relief between. — [GOV.UK Corporation Tax rates](https://www.gov.uk/corporation-tax-rates) (P)
- Identity verification became a legal requirement on 18 November 2025, with a 12-month transition; a biometric passport from any country is accepted through GOV.UK One Login, expired passports are not, each email address can verify one identity, bank-detail and Post Office routes appear aimed at UK residents, and an authorised corporate service provider (ACSP) can verify a person who supplies suitable ID. — [GOV.UK verify your identity](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/verify-your-identity-for-companies-house) (P)
- Timing: a new incorporation requests each director's verification code during filing; existing directors give their code with the company's next confirmation statement. — [GOV.UK when to verify](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/when-you-need-to-verify-your-identity-for-companies-house) (P)
- Secondary reports say One Login can fail non-UK passport holders without UK financial history (a vendor claim) and give 17 or 18 November 2026 as the end of the transition; ACSP registration for filing agents has slipped to no earlier than November 2027. — [1Office](https://1office.co/blog/id-verification-non-resident-directors/), [Law Debenture](https://www.lawdebenture.com/news-insights/millions-of-directors-still-unverified-the-17-november-deadline-is-closer-than-it-looks), [DWF](https://dwfgroup.com/en-ie/news-and-insights/insights/2026/8/delays-to-implementation-of-compulsory-identity-verification) (S, via search summary)
- A UK-incorporated company is UK-resident for corporation tax (CTA 2009 s14); section 18 makes a company treaty non-resident, and so non-UK-resident, when a treaty tie-breaker places it abroad; HMRC guidance confines the central-management-and-control test for UK-incorporated companies to those within a statutory exception. — [CTA 2009 s14](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2009/4/section/14), [CTA 2009 s18](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2009/4/section/18), [HMRC INTM120040](https://gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/international-manual/intm120040) (P, via search summary)
- UK-Uzbekistan treaty (signed Tashkent 15 October 1993): the Protocol signed London 24 January 2018 (in force 1 June 2018) replaced Article 4(3), so a company resident in both states is placed by agreement of the competent authorities, and absent agreement it is treated as resident of neither for claiming treaty benefits (other than Articles 22, 25 and 26). The old effective-management tie-breaker no longer applies. — [Treaty Series No. 6 (2018)](https://treaties.fcdo.gov.uk/data/Library2/pdf/2018-TS0006.pdf) (P, read directly)
- Uzbek domestic law treats a foreign legal entity whose effective place of management is in Uzbekistan as an Uzbek tax resident; Uzbek corporate income tax is 15% generally. — [PwC residence](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/corporate-residence), [PwC corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income) (S)
- Public-facing rules: websites and business letters must show the company's registration number, registered office address, country of registration and that it is a limited company (normally Limited or Ltd in the name); the page does not discuss trading names. — [GOV.UK signs and stationery](https://www.gov.uk/running-a-limited-company/signs-stationery-and-promotional-material) (P)
- Running costs (vendor figures, 2026): registered office GBP 50-150 a year; accountant for micro-entity accounts and CT600 GBP 600-1,200 minimum or GBP 1,200-2,500 typical (Sleek), or a GBP 389 fixed offer (TaxFlow); first-year all-in GBP 1,500-3,000; living abroad does not bar a person from being a director; late accounts penalties start at GBP 150. — [Sleek](https://sleek.com/uk/resources/cost-of-running-a-limited-company/) (S, via search summary)
- Wise Business requires a trading address in a supported country; Revolut Business applicant-residence lists in help-centre versions seen exclude Uzbekistan (official page returned 403; via search summary). — [Wise address rules](https://wise.com/help/articles/2969315/how-does-wise-verify-my-business-address) (P), [Revolut Business eligibility](https://help.revolut.com/business/help/setting-up-an-account/is-my-business-eligible/what-country-of-residence-is-eligible-to-open-a-revolut-business-account/) (P, not opened)

### Inferences
- Formation and annual filing are cheap and remote-friendly (GBP 100 plus GBP 50, passport-chip ID), but the tax drag is the highest of the options: 19-25% on every pound of profit, with no 0%-on-retained regime.
- Managing from Tashkent creates a real dual-residence question: Uzbek law can claim the company, and the 2018 protocol leaves the outcome to negotiation, with treaty relief lost if there is no agreement.
- UK banking and Stripe-residence acceptability for a Tashkent resident are unverified, so the UK offers no banking advantage over the other options.

### Gaps
- UK dividend withholding tax for non-resident shareholders was not verified.
- No Stripe page found on UK accounts with a non-resident or unsupported-country director.
- UK high-street and challenger bank acceptance of a Tashkent-resident owner not verified; Revolut's current Business residence list could not be read directly.
- Accountant prices are vendor figures; no quote for non-resident-director filings (a possible hidden cost).
- Audit-exemption and micro-entity thresholds not verified; whether Uzbek authorities actually assert residence over foreign companies run from Uzbekistan was not found.

## 4. Estonia OÜ via e-Residency (fees, pickup, running costs, 2026 tax)

### Takeaway
Estonia has the best retained-profit tax (0%, with 22/78 charged only on distributions in 2026) and cheap formation (EUR 150 e-Residency plus EUR 265 company fee), but Stripe's explicit residence condition, the in-person card pickup (reachable points include Astana, Baku, Tbilisi, Ankara and Abu Dhabi), the mandatory Estonian address and contact person, the post-August-2025 VAT economic-connection test and in-person bank checks make it a hard fit for a Tashkent-based solo founder.

### Cited Findings
- e-Residency state fee is EUR 150, paid by card at application, non-refundable if refused; renewal and replacement also EUR 150; the card is valid 5 years with no annual fee. — [e-Residency costs and fees](https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-us/articles/360000625118) (P, read via the help-centre JSON endpoint)
- The official blog announces a flat EUR 165 fee for applications and renewals from 1 January 2027; physical cards continue until mobile e-Residency arrives, with mobile biometric capture planned for 2027. — [e-Residency blog, changes 2025 and beyond](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/en/blog/posts/changes-to-e-residency-in-2025-and-beyond) (P, published October 2024, updated April 2026)
- Process timing: online application about 30 minutes, identity check up to 30 days, card delivery 2-5 weeks, activation within 24 hours after pickup; the same ID used to apply is needed at pickup. — [e-Residency, become an e-resident](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/become-an-e-resident/) (P)
- Pickup points relevant to Tashkent: Astana (Kazakhstan), Baku (Azerbaijan), Tbilisi (Georgia), Ankara (Turkiye), Abu Dhabi and Dubai (Dubai marked a mobile point), Moscow, Beijing, plus Riga, Vilnius, Helsinki, Berlin, London and others; none in Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan or Armenia; fingerprints require collection in person. — [e-Residency pick-up locations](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/pick-up-locations/) (P)
- Visa-free entry for Uzbek citizens (secondary, details differ by source): UAE up to 30 days with a biometric passport, Kazakhstan 30 days per stay with a 90-in-180 cap, Georgia about a year, Azerbaijan up to 90 days, Turkiye 90 in 180 (one list says 30). — [kun.uz UAE](https://kun.uz/en/news/2024/02/16/citizens-of-uzbekistan-can-travel-to-the-uae-visa-free-starting-today), [yep.uz 2025 list](https://yep.uz/en/2025/08/visa-free-countries-uzbekistan-2025/), [zamin.uz](https://zamin.uz/en/society/222757-which-countries-can-be-visited-visa-free-with-an-uzbek-passport.html) (S)
- Nationality restrictions on e-Residency applications submitted after 1 October 2025: a ten-country list (Afghanistan, Burkina Faso, Iran, Yemen, DR Congo, North Korea, South Sudan, Mali, Nigeria, Syria) is reviewed only on narrow conditions, and Belarus and Russia face stricter rules; Uzbekistan is not mentioned. — [Police and Border Guard Board](https://www.politsei.ee/en/instructions/e-resident-s-digital-id/citizenship-based-application-restrictions) (P)
- A January 2025 official slide deck flagged possible future restrictions for citizens of countries without a bilateral security and law-enforcement cooperation framework, and ERR reported the ministry wanting to limit e-Residency access; neither was confirmed to cover Uzbekistan. — [e-Res live Q&A, Jan 2025](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/e-Res_liveQA_January_2025.pdf), [ERR](https://news.err.ee/1609490050/ministry-looking-to-limit-access-to-estonian-e-residency) (P/S, via search summary)
- Company registration: EUR 265 state fee fully online once the card is active; legal address and contact person needed when the board does not physically live in Estonia; minimum share capital is EUR 0.01 per shareholder; all board members need a digital ID supported by Estonian e-services. — [e-Residency start a company](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/start-a-company/), [e-Residency OU article](https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-us/articles/360000633557) (P)
- Address and contact-person cost: the start-a-company page says EUR 200-400 a year; the 9-things blog (5 August 2026) says about EUR 100-200; provider quotes include EUR 400 (Eestifirma bundle), about EUR 290 (1Office, billed yearly) and a 5-year EUR 545 deal on the official marketplace; permitted contact persons are notaries, attorneys, sworn auditors, tax representatives of non-residents and licensed service providers; over 1,200 companies were struck for lacking a compliant one. — [e-Residency start a company](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/start-a-company/), [e-Residency 9 things](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/9-things-you-should-know-about-running-an-estonian-company/) (P); [1Office](https://1office.co/estonia/services/virtual-address/), [Eestifirma](https://eestifirma.ee/en/service/contact-person/), [Dalanta on the e-Residency marketplace](https://marketplace.e-resident.gov.ee/service-providers/dalanta/) (S)
- Tax: 22/78 on distributed profit from 2025 (the 14/86 reduced rate abolished), no tax on profit reinvested or retained, distributions reported by form TSD by the 10th of the following month. — [EMTA income and social taxes](https://emta.ee/en/business-client/taxes-and-payment/income-and-social-taxes) (P)
- The official e-Residency blog says there are no planned changes to the corporate rate for 2026 or 2027 and that standard VAT rose to 24% on 1 July 2025. — [e-Residency blog](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/en/blog/posts/changes-to-e-residency-in-2025-and-beyond) (P). Conflicting wording: an official help article says the rate is a flat 22% yet "will increase to 20% from 1 January 2026", which reads as an error. — [e-Residency costs and fees](https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-us/articles/360000625118) (P)
- Since August 2025 EMTA wants proof of an economic connection to Estonia (local clients or suppliers, a physical office, or management decisions made in Estonia) before granting a VAT number, for new applicants and existing companies; economic activity may also be reviewed at e-Residency card renewal. — [e-Residency 9 things](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/9-things-you-should-know-about-running-an-estonian-company/) (P)
- VAT registration becomes mandatory above EUR 40,000 of taxable turnover with Estonia as place of supply; annual accounts are required even for a dormant company, in Estonian; dividends are allowed only after the annual report is approved and filed; invoices and statements must be kept 7 years. — [e-Residency 10 useful things](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/10-useful-things-to-know-after-you-register-an-estonian-company/) (P, published May 2021, possibly dated)
- Banking: traditional Estonian banks (LHV, Swedbank, SEB) generally require a foreign board member to attend in person; fintech accounts may reject some business models; e-Residency recommends an EEA neobank, quoting EUR 0-200 and 1 hour to 1 week. — [e-Residency 9 things](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/blog/posts/9-things-you-should-know-about-running-an-estonian-company/), [e-Residency start a company](https://www.e-resident.gov.ee/start-a-company/) (P)
- Stripe's e-Residency article requires associated individuals to live in a Stripe-supported country (section 1). — [Stripe support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/supporting-companies-that-registered-in-estonia-through-e-residency) (P)

### Inferences
- First-year hard fees are about EUR 415 (EUR 150 plus EUR 265) plus address and contact person (EUR 100-400) plus accounting; recurring fixed cost is address and contact person plus accounting plus EUR 150 e-Residency renewal every five years, with no state annual fee found.
- The 0% retained-profit regime only helps if Estonia remains the taxing state in practice; for an Uzbek-resident owner the Uzbek controlled-foreign-company rules (section 8) can tax retained profit in his hands, and the effective-management rule can make the OU Uzbek-resident.
- The Stripe residence condition, the VAT economic-connection test and in-person bank checks each cut against a Tashkent-run company; Estonia becomes workable mainly if a partner who lives in a Stripe-supported country is the account representative.
- Waiting until 2027 might remove the travel (mobile biometrics) but raises the fee to EUR 165; that depends on a plan, not a launch.

### Gaps

- No directly verified accounting price list; a search summary mentioned an official help-article estimate of about EUR 600 for a do-it-yourself first year and about EUR 1,300 with accounting support, but I could not locate that article, so the figure is unconfirmed.

- Which Estonian or EEA fintech accepts a Tashkent-resident board member is unverified.

## 5. UAE free-zone company (cheapest credible licence, annual cost, banking, 9% tax and QFZP rules)

### Takeaway
A no-visa free-zone licence in Ajman, RAKEZ or SHAMS runs roughly AED 5,500-7,000 (vendor figures; RAKEZ's own site shows an AED 6,000 starter package), and tax can be 0% on the first AED 375,000 of income or 0% on everything while revenue stays at or below AED 3 million (Small Business Relief now running to 2029); but SaaS cannot realistically qualify for the 0% Qualifying Free Zone Person regime, and a Tashkent-resident non-resident owner has no verified remote route to a UAE corporate bank account.

### Cited Findings
- Standard rates for a free-zone person that is not a Qualifying Free Zone Person (QFZP): 0% on taxable income up to AED 375,000 and 9% above. — [FTA Free Zone Persons guide CTGFZP1 (May 2024), section 5.6](https://tax.gov.ae/Datafolder/Files/Guides/CT/Free%20Zone%20Persons%20-%2020%2005%202024%20final%20for%20GCD.pdf) (P)
- QFZP conditions: adequate substance in a free zone, qualifying income, no election out, arm's-length pricing, transfer-pricing documents, audited financial statements regardless of revenue, and non-qualifying revenue within the lower of AED 5,000,000 or 5% of total revenue; failure ends QFZP status for that tax period and the next four; a QFZP pays 0% only on qualifying income, 9% on the rest, and cannot use the AED 375,000 band or Small Business Relief. — [FTA guide, sections 3.2-3.4 and 4.7](https://tax.gov.ae/Datafolder/Files/Guides/CT/Free%20Zone%20Persons%20-%2020%2005%202024%20final%20for%20GCD.pdf) (P)
- Software: the FTA guide says creating software or ERPs does not count as manufacturing goods, and the distribution activity excludes intangibles such as licences and software; copyrighted software counts as Qualifying Intellectual Property only for separately identifiable IP income (royalties, licence fees, gains) and only up to a nexus formula tied to the QFZP's own qualifying expenditure, with income above that taxed at 9% and income from products or services with no separate IP remuneration not counting. — [FTA guide, sections 9.3-9.4, 10.3.2, 10.14](https://tax.gov.ae/Datafolder/Files/Guides/CT/Free%20Zone%20Persons%20-%2020%2005%202024%20final%20for%20GCD.pdf) (P)
- Ministerial Decision 229 of 2025 sets 14 qualifying activities (manufacturing, qualifying commodities, securities holding, ships, reinsurance, fund and wealth management, headquarters, treasury, aircraft leasing, designated-zone distribution, logistics, ancillary), none described as software or SaaS. — [PwC alert](https://www.pwc.com/m1/en/services/tax/me-tax-legal-news/2025/new-ministerial-decisions-no-229-and-no-230-of-regarding-qualifying-free-zone-persons-regime.html) (S, via search summary)
- Small Business Relief (SBR): an eligible resident person (natural or juridical) with revenue at or below AED 3,000,000 in the period and all previous periods can elect to be treated as having no taxable income, file a simplified return and prepare cash-basis financial statements; exceeding AED 3,000,000 ends eligibility even if revenue later falls; the two excluded groups are QFZPs and members of multinational groups, so a free-zone company that is not a QFZP can elect; splitting a business across several entities to stay under the threshold is disqualifying and triggers repayment plus penalties; the guide's cut-off was periods ending on or before 31 December 2026. — [FTA Small Business Relief guide (Aug 2023)](https://tax.gov.ae/DataFolder/Files/Guides/CT/Small%20Business%20Relief%20Guide%20-%20EN%20-%2027%2008%202023.pdf) (P)
- Ministerial Decision 131 of 2026 (dated 29 July 2026, announced 7 August 2026) extends SBR to tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2029 with the AED 3 million cap unchanged; the official text on mof.gov.ae is in Arabic and I relied on press copies for the English wording. — [MoF decision (Arabic PDF)](https://mof.gov.ae/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Ministerial-Decision-No.-131-of-2026-Amending-Certain-Provisions-of-Ministerial-Decision-No.-73-of-2023-on-Small-Business-Relief-for-the-Purposes-of-Federal-Decree-Law-No.-47-of-2022-AR.pdf) (P); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/business/uae-extends-corporate-tax-relief-for-small-businesses-until-2029), [Entrepreneur Middle East](https://mena.entrepreneur.com/business-news/uae-extends-small-business-tax-relief-through-2029) (S)
- The FTA site news ticker shows a 30 September 2026 deadline for filing returns and paying corporate tax for the year ended 31 December 2025, i.e. returns are due nine months after year-end. — [FTA site](https://tax.gov.ae/en/taxes/corporate.tax/qualifying.free.zone.person.aspx) (P; this URL returned the FTA homepage when fetched on 2026-10-08)
- RAKEZ's own site shows an AED 6,000 business starter package and an AED 14,000 all-inclusive set-up package (scope not defined) and a fully online instant licence; no AED price for e-commerce, software or consultancy licences. — [RAKEZ](https://rakez.com/en/) (P)
- Vendor comparison (Avyanco, July 2026, government fees only): no-visa totals of about AED 5,565 (Ajman), AED 6,010 (RAKEZ), AED 6,875 (SHAMS), AED 12,900 (IFZA); with one visa AED 12,010 (RAKEZ), AED 13,141 (Ajman), AED 14,300 (SHAMS), AED 17,700 (IFZA). — [Avyanco RAKEZ](https://avyanco.com/rakez-free-zone-cost/), [Ajman](https://avyanco.com/ajman-free-zone-cost/), [SHAMS](https://avyanco.com/shams-free-zone-cost/), [IFZA](https://avyanco.com/ifza-free-zone-cost/) (S)
- Other vendor figures: SHAMS AED 5,750 entry licence covering up to five activities, process fully remote; RAKEZ e-commerce band AED 5,750-9,000 and service/consultancy from about AED 11,400; software/IT licences AED 12,000-18,000 (Flyingcolour), IFZA AED 13,900-18,900 and Meydan AED 11,500-18,900 (Takween); a no-visa RAKEZ first year including accounting about AED 14,535 (Osome); headline prices can roughly double once visas, desks and renewals are added; renewals sit near year-one prices. — [SafeLedger SHAMS](https://safeledger.ae/blog/shams-free-zone-license-cost), [BCL](https://bcl.ae/blogs/cheapest-free-zone-licence-dubai.md), [Flyingcolour](https://www.flyingcolour.net/blog/opening-a-software-company-in-dubai/), [Takween](https://takweenadvisory.ae/en/blog/it-software-license-cost-dubai), [Osome](https://osome.com/ae/blog/rakez-company-setup-cost/) (S, via search summary)
- Banking: Wio makes an Emirates ID and UAE visa a condition for the company's authorised individual; Mashreq's NEO BIZ key-facts document requires one UAE-resident partner or attorney-in-fact; RAKBANK's non-resident product is a personal account with a USD 100,000 minimum and HSBC adds non-resident signatories only in the branch (both per one banking marketplace); Meydan advertises remote setup with introductions to 26+ banks, and one 2026 formation-firm guide argues the company route usually works for non-resident founders because banks judge the business. — [Wio support](https://www.wio.io/support/) (P), [Mashreq NEO BIZ KFS](https://www.mashreq.com/-/jssmedia/pdfs/neobiz/NEOBiz_KFS.ashx) (P, via search summary), [Binderr](https://binderr.com/marketplace/ae/opening-a-business-bank-account-in-uae-for-non-residents), [Gulf News on Meydan](https://gulfnews.com/gn-focus/company-news/meydan-free-zone-launches-non-residency-business-setup-with-guaranteed-corporate-bank-account-1.500479010), [Business Dubai](https://www.businessdubai.ae/blogs/uae-bank-account-non-residents) (S, via search summary)
- Stripe UAE accepts non-resident owners on a passport alone and free-zone licences issued inside the UAE (section 1). — [Stripe UAE ownership](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-ownership-verification-requirements) (P)
- UAE-Uzbekistan treaty rates on Uzbek-source dividends: 0%, 5% or 15% depending on conditions. — [PwC withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/withholding-taxes) (S)

### Inferences
- SaaS subscription revenue is not on the qualifying-activities list and is not separately identifiable IP income, so a SaaS-only free-zone company should plan on non-QFZP status: 0% up to AED 375,000 (about US$102,000 at the 3.6725 peg, my arithmetic) then 9%, or 0% throughout via SBR while revenue stays at or below AED 3,000,000 (about US$817,000) through 2029; the founder must still register for corporate tax and file each year.
- The cheapest credible no-visa licence is Ajman, RAKEZ or SHAMS at about AED 5,500-7,000 (about US$1,500-1,900), but the licence category must cover software or e-commerce, and software/IT categories are quoted at AED 11,000-19,000; one visa plus Emirates ID brings the year-one figure to about AED 12,000-14,300 (about US$3,300-3,900), and a renewal is similar.
- Bank access, not formation, is the UAE blocker: the published bank rules need a UAE-resident signatory or a branch visit, so a UAE residence visa (and a trip; Uzbek citizens enter visa-free for up to 30 days per the secondary sources) is the likely price of a working account.
- Audit duty applies certainly to a QFZP; whether a free zone itself demands audited accounts from a non-QFZP varies and was not verified.

### Gaps
- No official free-zone price list read (RAKEZ and SHAMS pages show little; Ajman and IFZA not opened); all zone-by-zone prices are vendor figures dated July 2026.
- Free-zone audit rules for non-QFZP companies, UAE dividend withholding and the UAE-Uzbekistan treaty's company-residence tie-breaker not verified.
- Whether Stripe applies its supported-country residence condition to UAE accounts with an Uzbek-resident owner is unconfirmed.
- Remote UAE bank onboarding for a non-resident owner: only vendor claims (Meydan) in favour; official bank documents point the other way.
- Visa-free entry and 30-day limit come from secondary press; the English text of MD 131 of 2026 was not read from an official English source.

## 6. Other Stripe-supported options (Lithuania, Ireland, Singapore, Hong Kong)

### Takeaway
None beats the main four for this profile: Ireland needs an EEA-resident director or a bond, Singapore needs a resident director and secretary, Hong Kong needs a resident company secretary, an annual audit and is hard to bank, and Lithuania only duplicates Estonia's gates at a higher 17% rate. All four are on Stripe's list, so the same residence and banking checks apply.

### Cited Findings
- Lithuania 2026: standard corporate income tax rises from 16% to 17% from 1 January 2026; small entities 7% (was 6%); newly established small entities with revenue up to EUR 300,000 pay 0% for their first two tax periods subject to further conditions. — [KPMG Tax News Flash](https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2025/10/tnf-lithuania-amendments-to-corporate-income-taxation-effective-january-1-2026.html) (P/S)

- Ireland (via search summary): at least one director must be EEA-resident or the company must hold a section 137 bond (statute says EUR 25,000; some guides say EUR 25,394; premium about EUR 1,600 per two years); CRO online incorporation EUR 50; corporation tax 12.5% on trading income, 25% on passive income. — [Companies Act 2014 s137](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2014/act/38/section/137) (P, page returned 403), [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en-ie/locations/ireland/ireland-blog/eea-resident-director-requirement-ireland), [Open Forest cost guide](https://www.openforest.co/articles/cost-register-company-ireland), [PwC Ireland](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/ireland/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income) (S)
- Singapore: ACRA's page lists ordinary residence among director qualifications and requires a secretary within six months who is a resident individual and not the sole director; vendors state the rule as at least one locally resident director; ACRA fees S$315 (name S$15, registration S$300) per vendor figures; nominee director about S$1,200-5,000 a year; registered office S$250-500; annual return about S$60; corporate tax 17% with partial exemptions; recurring bill without a nominee about S$1,000-2,200. — [ACRA](https://acra.gov.sg/how-to-guides/setting-up-a-local-company/appointing-directors-company-secretary-and-other-key-personnel) (P); [Sleek](https://sleek.com/sg/resources/nominee-director-cost-singapore/), [Avyanco](https://avyanco.com/cost-of-company-formation-in-singapore/) (S, via search summary)

### Inferences
- Ireland, Singapore and Hong Kong each add a paid local person or a mandatory audit (EUR 1,600 bond per two years; S$1,200-5,000 nominee; HK audit) before any tax, which is out of proportion at under US$5K MRR.

## 8. Uzbekistan-side tax exposure (place of effective management, CFC, dividends)

### Takeaway
Whatever the foreign jurisdiction, the Uzbek side is not neutral: Uzbekistan taxes residents on worldwide income, treats a foreign company run from Uzbekistan as resident, and has had controlled-foreign-company rules for individuals since 2022, so the 0% retained-profit advantage of Estonia or the UAE is not safe for a Tashkent-run company. Local tax advice on these three points is a prerequisite for any structure.

### Cited Findings
- Uzbek corporate residence includes foreign legal entities whose effective place of management is in Uzbekistan (and those treated as resident under a treaty); residents are taxed on worldwide income. — [PwC residence](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/corporate-residence), [PwC corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income) (S)
- Uzbek corporate income tax is 15% generally; the 0% rate for export income was abolished from 1 January 2025; an optional turnover-tax regime exists up to 12,000 BCU of annual turnover. — [PwC corporate income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/taxes-on-corporate-income) (S)
- Resident individuals pay 12% on most income and 5% on dividends and interest (it is not stated whether this covers foreign-source dividends); the fixed individual-entrepreneur tax was abolished from 1 January 2026; dividends from joint-stock companies are PIT-exempt from April 2022 to December 2028. — [PwC personal income](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/individual/taxes-on-personal-income) (S)
- Controlled-foreign-company rules have applied since 1 January 2022 and aim at profits retained by foreign companies; a resident holding an interest in a foreign legal entity must notify the tax authority (Cabinet Decision 489 of 14 August 2020); individuals receiving foreign-entity income face an annual declaration due 1 April; resident individuals may hold accounts abroad. — [PwC group taxation](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/group-taxation), [Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Uzbekistan-Cabinet-Adopts-New--43375), [Zamin.uz](https://zamin.uz/en/iqtisodiyot/151367-horijdan-daromad-olganlar-uchun-muhim-habar-muddat-uzaytirildi.html), [Deloitte Uzbekistan highlights 2023](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/services/consulting/2024/dttl-tax-uzbekistanhighlights-2023.pdf) (S, via search summary)
- Uzbek treaty rates on Uzbek-source dividends: UK 5%/10%, UAE 0%/5%/15%, Estonia 5%/10%, Lithuania 10%, Singapore 5%, Ireland 5%/10%; the US and Hong Kong are not listed in PwC's table (the table is not a complete treaty list). — [PwC withholding taxes](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/withholding-taxes) (S)
- UK-Uzbekistan treaty: dual residence is resolved by mutual agreement, with treaty benefits denied if no agreement (section 3). — [Treaty Series No. 6 (2018)](https://treaties.fcdo.gov.uk/data/Library2/pdf/2018-TS0006.pdf) (P)

### Inferences
- A solo founder deciding everything in Tashkent is the textbook case for the effective-management claim; the choice of foreign jurisdiction does not remove it, and only the UK-Uzbekistan treaty text was checked.
- The Uzbek CFC rules could tax the founder annually on the retained profit of a wholly owned foreign company, which would erase the headline benefit of Estonia's 0% retained rate and weaken the UAE's 0% on a CFC reading; the details (thresholds, exemptions) were not found.
- Dividends or distributions to the founder are likely taxed in Uzbekistan whatever the foreign rate, so the foreign entity's tax should be compared on total burden, not headline rate.

### Gaps
- CFC definition, ownership threshold, low-tax test, exemptions and the notification forms were not found; the Tax Code text on lex.uz was not accessible.
- Whether foreign-source dividends are taxed at 5% and whether a foreign tax credit exists were not stated.
- No source on how Uzbek authorities enforce the effective-management rule against single-owner foreign companies.
- Treatment of a foreign disregarded LLC under Uzbek law not found.

## 9. Which jurisdictions let one company run many unrelated products under brand names?

### Takeaway
No jurisdiction researched forbids one company from selling many products under brand names; the practical limits are licence activity lists (UAE), mandatory company-detail disclosure on websites (UK), and Stripe's per-account review, while per-product partners push toward separate entities whose fixed costs multiply.

### Cited Findings
- UK: websites and business letters must show the company's registration number, registered office address, country and that it is a limited company; no rule on trading names appeared on the page. — [GOV.UK signs and stationery](https://www.gov.uk/running-a-limited-company/signs-stationery-and-promotional-material) (P)
- Estonia: the first annual report of an OU must show all business-activity areas by turnover, which presumes more than one activity. — [e-Residency OU article](https://learn.e-resident.gov.ee/hc/en-us/articles/360000633557) (P)
- UAE: a free-zone licence lists permitted activities; vendors say packages include up to 10 activities in Ajman, up to 5 at SHAMS, and 3 at IFZA with extras about AED 1,000 each; Stripe asks that the business type match the licence; and the FTA's Small Business Relief guide disqualifies a business artificially split across entities to stay under the AED 3 million cap. — [QASPRO via search summary](https://qasproglobal.com/?p=3172), [GrowAcross](https://growacross.com/insights/shams-free-zone-guide), [Uppersetup](https://uppersetup.com/en/blog/ifza-2026-the-complete-breakdown-the-uaes-most-registered) (S); [Stripe UAE pages](https://support.stripe.com/questions/uae-business-verification-requirements) (P)
- Stripe: one legal entity may hold several Stripe accounts, each with its own website, payout set-up and activation review (per a third-party reading); the Stripe agreement excerpt read states no numeric limit. — [CartDNA](https://cartdna.com/en/resources/guides/stripe/multiple-accounts) (S), [Stripe SSA](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa) (P)
- Atlas lists a multi-member LLC under partnership filing, and its US forms differ for LLCs and C corporations (section 2). — [Atlas business taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes) (P)

### Inferences
- A single entity with brand-name products is workable in all four main options; Delaware's fixed costs per entity ($100 agent plus $400 tax) and a UAE licence (about AED 5,500-7,000 a year) make per-product entities uneconomic for 20-30 small products.
- A partner on one product is better modelled by contract (revenue share or a separate account) than by a new entity until that product earns enough to carry the fixed costs.
- A UAE licence must cover software/e-commerce activities for every product type; the other jurisdictions need no activity matching beyond tax classification. Several UAE entities for different products would also risk the Small Business Relief anti-splitting rule if the products are really one business.

## 10. Ranking for this founder profile and the reasons

### Takeaway
Ranking: (1) Delaware single-member LLC through Stripe Atlas, (2) UAE free-zone company with a residence visa, (3) Estonia OU, (4) UK Ltd, (5) Lithuania, Ireland, Singapore and Hong Kong.
