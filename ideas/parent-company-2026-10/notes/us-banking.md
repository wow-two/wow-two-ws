## Key question 1: Mercury — is Uzbekistan on the prohibited list, and what about the 2026-05-01 closures?

### Takeaway
Yes. Uzbekistan is on Mercury's current "Prohibited countries" list (page last edited 2026-10-07). The test is country of residence, not citizenship, and it also covers "businesses that operate or do business in" a listed country. Mercury is not an option while the founder lives in or runs the business from Tashkent, and its cards also decline at Uzbek merchants.

### Cited Findings
- Mercury says it is "unable to open accounts for founders who live in – or businesses that operate or do business in – any of the countries and regions listed below. This is based on your country of residence, not your citizenship or nationality", and that a citizen who "currently reside[s] elsewhere" may still be eligible pending review. Uzbekistan is on the list. The article's JSON shows `updated_at` 2026-10-07T22:46:43Z. The HTML page returns HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so I read it through the help-centre API. — [Mercury: Prohibited countries](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580-Prohibited-countries), read via [help-centre JSON](https://support.mercury.com/api/v2/help_center/en-us/articles/28771710754580.json)
- Full "Prohibited countries" list (48): Afghanistan, Albania, Angola, Bangladesh, Belarus, Bhutan, Bosnia & Herzegovina, Burkina Faso, Burundi, Cambodia, Cameroon, Central African Republic, Congo (DR), Congo, Croatia, Eritrea, Gambia, Haiti, Indonesia, Iraq, Kyrgyzstan, Latvia, Lebanon, Lesotho, Liberia, Libya, Maldives, Mali, Mozambique, Myanmar, Nepal, Nicaragua, Nigeria, Pakistan, Palestine, Philippines, Russia, Somalia, South Sudan, Sudan, Syria, Ukraine, **Uzbekistan**, Vanuatu, Venezuela, Vietnam, Yemen, Zimbabwe. A separate "sanctioned" block adds Cuba, Iran, North Korea and the Russian-occupied regions of Ukraine. In Central Asia and the Caucasus only Kyrgyzstan and Uzbekistan are listed; Kazakhstan, Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Georgia, Armenia and Azerbaijan are not. — [Mercury: Prohibited countries](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580-Prohibited-countries)
- Mercury's card-restriction article says Mercury cards cannot be used "in or for merchants located in" a listed set of countries, and Uzbekistan is on it. Card transactions with Uzbek-based merchants are declined. Page updated 2026-09-22. — [Mercury: Countries where Mercury cards are restricted](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28778487704084-Countries-where-Mercury-cards-are-restricted)
- Outbound wires to Uzbekistan are not blocked by Mercury as a destination: Uzbekistan is on neither its "blocked" list nor its "documentation required" list (page updated 2026-09-11). This matters only for other account holders. — [Mercury: Sending international payments](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/40403130699028-Sending-international-payments)
- Mercury's eligibility rules: the company must be US-registered, with existing or planned US operations. International founders can apply with a passport and need no US citizenship or residency. Each 25%-plus beneficial owner gives a physical residential address, with no PO boxes or virtual addresses. The EIN document can be CP575, 147c or a returned SS-4. No SSN or ITIN is requested for the passport route. — [Mercury: Eligibility](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770467511060-Eligibility-and-requirements-for-opening-a-Mercury-account), [Mercury: Gathering your documents](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28770957425172-Gathering-your-documents)
- New US-operations check (article created 2026-09-02, updated 2026-10-06). If a business has no US workplace, no US-resident beneficial owner and no beneficial owner with a US-issued ID, Mercury may ask for proof of US operations. Accepted proofs include a record of a sale to a US customer, a US customer invoice or contract, or a signed SAFE. A business that does not operate in the US "may be locked or eventually closed". — [Mercury: Verifying your U.S. business operations](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/53171397356820-Verifying-your-U-S-business-operations)
- Mercury lists "You or your business is located in an unsupportable geography" among its closure reasons, and says "In most cases, our decision is final." — [Mercury: Understanding account closures](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/43095394066452-Understanding-account-closures-and-access-restrictions)
- The 2026-05-01 closures (secondary): a Hacker News post dated 2026-03-02 links to the same prohibited-countries article. The poster quotes a Mercury email: "Due to recent changes in how we determine account eligibility, we are no longer able to support accounts for businesses with physical or residential addresses in these countries. As a result, we will be closing the Mercury account for [name], LLC on May 1, 2026." The post names no countries. — [HN item 47224410](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47224410), date from the [HN API](https://hacker-news.firebaseio.com/v0/item/47224410.json)
- Prior evidence from 2026-09-26 could not read the list and called Mercury's UZ status unverified; it is now verified as listed. — [prior evidence, "Payment rails" section](/Users/max/Projects/10x-ws/workbench/career/engineering/wow-two/wow-two-ws/workbench/ventures/10x-ventures/ideation/runs/2026-09-26-one-job-ladder/branch-c-founder-fit.md)

### Inferences
- Mercury is closed to this founder while he lives in or runs the business from Uzbekistan. Adding a partner does not obviously fix this: the list targets "founders who live in" and businesses that "operate or do business in" listed countries, so a product built in Uzbekistan stays exposed.
- Mercury's own closure email wording is address-based and its eligibility article collects verified residential addresses for every 25%-plus owner, so the rule is enforced against where owners actually live.

### Gaps
- When Uzbekistan was added is not established. Mercury shows no revision history on the article, and I did not use archive services.
- The March 2026 email did not name the list, so I cannot confirm that Uzbek-resident LLCs were in the 2026-05-01 wave.
- A VaultLeap post about Indonesia and Vietnam closures turned up in search snippets only. I did not fetch it.

## Key question 2: Wise Business for a US LLC whose owner lives in Uzbekistan

### Takeaway
Not documented as allowed, and Wise's own rules point against it. Uzbekistan is missing from Wise's "hold money" country list, USD account details require an address in a country where Wise offers balances, and a business trading from an unsupported country gets only services available in both countries. Treat Wise Business as unavailable. Even a Wise USD account could not receive SWIFT from Uzbekistan.

### Cited Findings
- Wise's list of countries where residents can hold money has about 80 entries (including the US, except Nevada, and Georgia). Uzbekistan is not on it. The page says nothing about where owners or directors of a business must live. — [Wise: Where do I need to live to hold money](https://wise.com/help/articles/2813542/where-do-i-need-to-live-to-hold-money-with-wise)
- Uzbekistan is not on Wise's list of countries where it does not operate. That list covers Afghanistan, Belarus, Burundi, CAR, Chad, Congo, DRC, Cuba, Eritrea, Iran, Iraq, North Korea, Libya, Myanmar, Somalia, South Sudan, Russia, Sudan, Syria, Yemen, Venezuela and five Ukrainian regions. So registering and sending may work while holding balances does not. — [Wise: Which countries can I use Wise in](https://wise.com/help/articles/2978049/which-countries-can-i-use-wise-in)
- USD account details are available "for your personal or Wise Business account if your address is in a country where you can open a currency". The unavailable-address list does not name Uzbekistan, but Uzbekistan is not a country where currencies can be opened. — [Wise: Can I get USD account details](https://wise.com/help/articles/2810318/can-i-get-usd-account-details)
- Business verification: "If your business registered address and trading address are in different countries, we can only offer you services that are available in both countries." Verification asks for directors' and owners' details and may ask for ownership documents and proof of the trading address. — [Wise: How can I verify my business](https://wise.com/help/articles/2769792/how-can-i-verify-my-business)
- Wise US business requirements (last updated 31.07.26). The trading address must be the "primary place of business (where you work everyday, such as an office or co-working space)", a physical location. PO boxes, registration agencies and mail-forwarding addresses are refused. 25%-plus beneficial owners give a residential address, and non-US owners give a photo ID instead of an SSN. Directors are listed with country of residence. Sign-up is free and USD account details cost a one-time $31 (22 currencies). Nevada and US territories are not onboarded. — [Wise: US business account requirements](https://wise.com/us/blog/wise-business-account-requirements-us)
- A Wise account on CFSB details "can't receive USD from" Guinea-Bissau, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Serbia and Uzbekistan. This re-verifies the 2026-09-26 finding (checked 2026-10-08/09). — [Wise: Receiving international Swift payments](https://wise.com/help/articles/3MObHiWysjT2DzDNHTupa4/what-countries-can-i-receive-international-swift-payments-from)
- Wise's own send-to list does not include Uzbekistan. For the US-to-Uzbekistan page, Wise says it is working to allow UZS "from the US" but that is not available, and USD goes by international wire (SWIFT). The sample fee shows $9.03. — [Wise: send to Uzbekistan (US)](https://wise.com/us/send-money/send-money-to-uzbekistan), [Wise: countries/regions I can send to](https://wise.com/help/articles/2571942/what-countriesregions-can-i-send-to)
- Stripe payouts into Wise USD details work only for some account-detail types. Wise's page says "The setup only works with some account details" and points to Stripe's supported types. — [Wise: receive money from Stripe](https://wise.com/help/articles/2977935/how-do-i-receive-money-from-stripe-with-wise)

### Inferences
- A US LLC run from Tashkent has its trading address in Uzbekistan, where Wise offers no balances. Under the "services available in both countries" rule, onboarding is likely to fail, or the account could be shut later. No Wise page says this outcome outright.
- Wise is also unusable as the personal receiving leg for the founder. The founder cannot hold balances, so Wise cannot be both the US-side account and the Uzbek-side account.

### Gaps
- No Wise text covers "US-registered business, owner residing in an unsupported country". No community reports were gathered. Wise support was not contacted.

## Key question 3: Eligibility of the other candidates (Airwallex, Payoneer, Relay, Revolut Business US, Brex, Novo, Bluevine, Slash) and Stripe's own accounts

No candidate is confirmed for an Uzbekistan-resident owner. **Airwallex**
- Onboarding is by business registration country. The "Eligible Countries and Territories" table (updated 2026-10-05) includes the United States (onboarded to the US entity) and omits Uzbekistan, so a company registered in Uzbekistan cannot be onboarded. — [Airwallex: Eligible countries](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4408591334937-Eligible-Countries-and-Territories)
- General eligibility: registered and active business, eligible country, no unsupported industry, not on a sanctions list, not a shell bank or bearer-share entity. — [Airwallex: Eligibility to open an account](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/900001757026-Eligibility-to-open-an-account)
- US checklist (updated 2026-10-05): the SSN is needed only "if US person", otherwise a government photo ID such as a passport. It also requires the EIN letter, formation certificate and a beneficial-ownership attestation. For "foreign businesses and/or businesses largely run outside of the US" Airwallex may ask for a recent brick-and-mortar US bank statement (not a US neobank statement such as Mercury), contracts with US businesses, invoices to US customers, evidence of US-based employees, or a US coworking lease. — [Airwallex: Documents required for companies in the US](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/4419267227417-Documents-required-for-companies-in-the-US)
- The US Service Agreement §3 requires a "principal place of business located in the United States". §21(a)(i) requires you to be "located in an approved jurisdiction", which it does not define. A "Restricted Jurisdiction" is a country targeted by comprehensive sanctions, and a "Restricted Person" includes anyone 50% or more owned or controlled by persons in such jurisdictions. Uzbekistan is not mentioned. — [Airwallex US Service Agreement](https://www.airwallex.com/us/terms/airwallex-service-agreement), [Airwallex US terms index](https://www.airwallex.com/us/terms)
- The acceptable-use policy lists "Sanctioned Parties" only and names no countries. — [Airwallex US AUP](https://www.airwallex.com/us/terms/acceptable-use-policy)
- Incoming SWIFT into Airwallex Denmark-based Global Accounts is restricted from a list of countries that includes Uzbekistan. The Hong Kong (bank code 016) list does not include it. The article lists no US-based variant. — [Airwallex: Restricted Countries for SWIFT Deposits](https://help.airwallex.com/hc/en-gb/articles/12142689283343-Restricted-Countries-for-SWIFT-Deposits-via-Global-Accounts)
- Fees (page fetched 2026-10-08/09): Explore plan $0/user/month; sending USD by SWIFT $15–$25 per transfer (SHA and OUR); FX margin 0.5% on major currencies and 1.0% on others; domestic USD transfers free. No inbound fee is listed. — [Airwallex US pricing](https://www.airwallex.com/us/pricing)

## Key question 5: Path, cost and paperwork to bring USD to Uzbekistan

### Takeaway
Receiving foreign currency in Uzbekistan is legally unrestricted for resident individuals, and the real friction is on the US side (B2B-only wires at Relay, SWIFT fees of $15–25, correspondent deductions) and in each Uzbek bank's AML practice. The legal structure of the payment (owner distribution to a personal account, or invoice from the founder's Uzbek individual entrepreneur to the LLC) decides which paperwork and which rules apply. One rule needs local advice before setup: capital-movement operations through banks are registered with the central bank, and an individual's outbound equity investment is capped at $10,000 a year without a government decision.

**5e. Uzbek currency-control and bank-reporting rules**
- Law "On Currency Regulation" (ZRU-573 of 22.10.2019; lex.uz shows revisions through 25.07.2026). Art. 10: resident individuals may own foreign currency "transferred or sent into" Uzbekistan and "use it at their own discretion". Art. 12: resident individuals may open foreign-currency accounts and deposits in Uzbek banks and abroad. Art. 15: "current international operations", which include payments for services and "net income from other investments" (my reading: owner distributions), are carried out "without restrictions". Art. 20: banks must perform currency operations "on the condition that residents and non-residents present the relevant documents required by the banks". Art. 26: individuals do not file reports on buying and selling foreign currency. — [Law on Currency Regulation (lex.uz)](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4562846)
- Central bank rules (reg. 3281, current text lists amendments through 17.07.2026): "Currency funds arriving in the name of resident individuals are credited to their accounts without restrictions and used independently" (¶16, in Uzbek). Resident legal entities and IEs hold foreign currency in accounts at Uzbek banks (¶13, ¶15). — [CBU rules, annex (Uzbek)](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4975566), [CBU resolution 18/5](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4975572)
- Capital-movement rule (reg. 2536, amended by CBU resolution of 2 June 2026 No. 15/1, in force from 17 September 2026): capital movement operations are registered in the central bank through banks. Residents may make certain operations abroad within caps; for individuals, "investment activity abroad — forming charter capital or equity participation in foreign enterprises" up to USD 10,000 equivalent per calendar year; above that a Presidential or Cabinet decision is needed. Documents must be in Uzbek or Russian, or legalised or apostilled, translated and notarised. After registration, residents file quarterly reports (by the 25th of the month after quarter-end) to their servicing bank. A further clause refers to investment in "foreign enterprises in the USA" funded from residents' foreign-currency account balances; its exact meaning is unclear. — [CBU capital-movement regulation (Uzbek)](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/2296324), [CBU resolution wrapper](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/2296326)
- If the founder acts as an individual entrepreneur or legal entity exporting services to the LLC: business entities, except registered self-employed persons, must enter foreign-trade contracts, acts and invoices into the E-kontrakt system with a digital signature (Resolution 283, as amended 30 January 2026 No. 40). Settlements under a foreign-trade contract "may be carried out ... only through" the entity's account in an Uzbek commercial bank. IT Park residents may take payments from abroad for subscriptions to their own software on a public offer without entering data. — [Cabinet Resolution 283 (lex.uz)](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4812424)
- Repatriation: exceeding the repatriation deadline by 180 days turns an export operation into a capital-movement operation (Art. 11), and Art. 11-1 sets fines of 5% (to 360 days), a further 10% (360–545 days) and a further 35% (over 545 days) of the unrepatriated amount. Decree UP-176 of 27.08.2026 (in force 28.08.2026) sets a one-time amnesty to 1 January 2027 allowing exporters to clear overdue receivables with their own funds, and from 1 January 2027 lets a court halve the fine in listed cases (good-faith collection efforts, over 50% repatriated, or sanctions or payment-system restrictions). — [Law on Currency Regulation (lex.uz)](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4562846), [Decree UP-176 (lex.uz)](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/8444982)
- 2026 AML amendments (secondary, citing the Ministry of Justice; effective 9 August 2026): domestic transfers of 25 BRV or more (UZS 10.3 million, about $870) must carry sender and recipient details; banks must make card payments traceable; transactions of 1,000 BRV (UZS 412 million) or more with FATF-monitored countries within 30 days are suspicious; the no-ID cash foreign-currency purchase limit rises from $100 to $500. For cross-border transfers to individuals, banks must pass on sender and recipient identifiers (name, residential address, birth date, ID details), keep the payment traceable end to end, and answer foreign institutions' requests within three working days. The rules were registered 8 May 2026 and took effect 9 August 2026. — [Spot.uz, 10 Aug 2026](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/08/10/currency-exchange/), [yep.uz, May 2026](https://yep.uz/en/2026/05/uzbekistan-tightens-control-bank-transfers-card-transactions/)
- Draft only (secondary, June 2026): banks would report to the tax service when a card receives 500 BRV or more (UZS 206.1 million) in a month from other individuals, excluding close relatives. The article does not say whether transfers from abroad are covered. — [Anhor.uz](https://anhor.uz/news/banki-mogut-objazat-peredavat-nalogovoj-dannye-o-krupnyh-perevodah-mezhdu-grazhdanami/)
- A 2022 bank notice shows the practice for individual senders: KDB Bank holds incoming transfers over USD 1,000 from individual clients of other banks until sender details (name, account, address or birth data, passport number) arrive, and may return funds after 10 days. — [KDB Bank notice](https://kdb.uz/en/press-center/news/kdb-bank-uzbekistan-jsc-informs-about-the-implementation-of-changes-in-the-practice-of-crediting-the-amounts-of-incoming-funds)

### Inferences
- Two structures are workable, with different paperwork:
  - Owner distribution or fee to a personal USD account at an Uzbek bank. The law credits resident individuals "without restrictions", but banks may still ask for the basis under Art. 20. Relay cannot make this wire at all (no business-to-person international wires). Slash's and Airwallex's pages name no business-only limit, though I did not find an explicit statement that they pay individuals; Stripe Global Payouts explicitly supports individual recipients in Uzbekistan.
  - The founder's Uzbek IE (or company) invoices the LLC for services, and the LLC pays that business account (B2B, which Relay allows). This brings E-kontrakt registration, the repatriation clock and the "settle through an Uzbek bank account" rule, but gives each payment a clean contract-and-invoice basis. Self-employed status removes the E-kontrakt duty (Resolution 283) but its other limits were not researched.
- Expected paperwork for either route, based on law and press rather than any bank-published list: the LLC's formation certificate, EIN letter and operating agreement (to prove ownership), and a stated payment basis (invoice or service contract, or a written owner-distribution record). Documents not in Uzbek or Russian are likely to need translation; for capital operations the text requires notarised translation. No bank-published list of documents for incoming LLC-to-individual SWIFT was found.
- Likely all-in cost per $1,000 sent, from sourced pieces only: $15–$25 outbound SWIFT (1.5%–2.5% at that size), possible intermediary deductions (unquantified), and 0% to about 0.5% on the Uzbek side, depending on bank and on whether the founder withdraws cash. Stripe Global Payouts would add a 1% FX fee and, if the wire fee applies, $25, but its Uzbekistan cross-border fee is not published.
- The founder's first funding of the LLC is the part most likely to trigger the capital-movement rule. If the LLC is funded only from its own Stripe revenue and the $500 Atlas fee is paid outside a bank transfer, there may be no registrable transfer, but that needs local advice.

- A search-engine summary attributed to a Kapitalbank FAQ a rule of "no cap on incoming SWIFT, documents for legal-entity senders, non-resident threshold of USD 10,000 a month". I could not find that text in the live Russian or English FAQ, so treat it as unverified.
