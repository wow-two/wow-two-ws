## 1. Brand names under one LLC: DBA / trade-name rules in Delaware, Wyoming and New Mexico, and whether a DBA is needed for Stripe statements, websites and app stores

### Takeaway
None of the three states requires a state DBA for a no-in-state-operations LLC that sells under brand names online: Delaware registration is optional for LLCs (and costs about $100 for the first name for a Delaware LLC operating elsewhere), Wyoming costs $100 per name and requires the name to be in use in Wyoming, and New Mexico has no general DBA filing. No platform needs a DBA either: Stripe wants a recognizable descriptor, Apple shows only the legal entity name and rejects DBAs at enrollment, and Google's developer name is a free, changeable field.

### Cited Findings
**Delaware**
- Since 2 Feb 2026 the Division of Revenue (DOR), not county prothonotaries, runs a statewide DBA registry; the DBA fee "remains $25" statewide; "DBAs in Delaware do not expire and do not require renewal." (P) — [Delaware DOR, Trade Names FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/trade-names-faqs/)
- A Delaware LLC or corporation that wants a DBA but does not intend to do business in Delaware needs a "Trade Name Only" license first: "It costs $25 and shares the same renewal and maintenance requirements as a standard Delaware business license"; "providing a Certificate of Good Standing is a requirement." The FAQ does not state an annual $25 figure or the renewal cycle. (P) — [Delaware DOR, Trade Names FAQs](https://revenue.delaware.gov/trade-names-faqs/)
- Statute text (6 Del. C. ch. 31, as summarised by the fetch tool; page shows no 2026 effective dates): §3101 requires a person doing business in Delaware under a non-disclosing trade name to file with the Division of Revenue; §3107 exempts "legally incorporated companies"; §3108(a) lets a corporation or LLC doing business in Delaware register a non-disclosing trade name but "registration is optional"; §3108(b) lets a Delaware corporation or LLC that does no business in Delaware register a trade name with a Secretary of State good-standing certificate after first acquiring a special trade name license under 30 Del. C. §2306. (P) — [6 Del. C. ch. 31](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c031/index.html)
- A short-form good standing certificate costs $50 (fee schedule revised 1 Aug 2026). (P) — [Delaware Division of Corporations fee schedule](https://corpfiles.delaware.gov/Fee_Schedule/AugustFee2026.pdf)
- Search-engine summary of Delaware One Stop/DOR guidance: the Trade Name-Only license is an annual $25 charge, several DBAs can ride on one license, a valid DOR license must stay attached to every DBA, and existing DOR accounts on the same FEIN can block a new Trade Name-Only registration; a Superior Court opinion reads §3108 (effective 2 Feb 2026) as relieving LLCs of fictitious-name certificate filings (conditions cut off); pages disagree on the bill number (HB 40 vs HB 177). (SS) — [Delaware One Stop, Trade Names](https://onestop.delaware.gov/Operate_TradeNames)
- A law-firm alert dated 13 Feb 2025 (before the effective date slipped to Feb 2026) said registration "will need to be completed by all entities seeking a trade name in Delaware", the Trade Name license costs $25 for one year and renews for three years at $75. (S, pre-implementation; conflicts with the statute's "optional" wording for LLCs doing business in Delaware) — [Saul Ewing alert](https://www.saul.com/insights/alert/delawares-updated-trade-name-registration-process)
- Delaware LLC state costs relevant here: no annual report required for LLCs; annual tax $400 from tax year 2026 (see section 2). (P in sibling notes, S here) — see `us-compliance.md` section 3; [Withum, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.withum.com/resources/delaware-businesses-face-increased-annual-taxes-and-filing-costs/)

**Wyoming**
- Trade name registration (Secretary of State, Business Division): $100 to register, $50 to renew, $25 to assign, $10 to cancel, $30 to reserve for 120 days; valid 10 years; notarized application mailed with a check; entity applicants must be in good standing; up to 15 business days; "You need to already be using the name in Wyoming before you can register it." (SS of the SOS form and fee-schedule PDFs; my direct fetches of sos.wyo.gov failed with connection refused) — [SOS trade name registration form](https://sos.wyo.gov/forms/business/tn/tn-registrationapplication.pdf); [SOS business fee schedule](https://sos.wyo.gov/Business/docs/BusinessFees.pdf)
- A formation-provider page (updated 4 Sep 2025) gives the same: $100, renewal every ten years at $50 by mail, and "your trade name must be in use before you register it". It does not say whether registration is mandatory for an LLC. (S) — [TRUiC, Wyoming DBA](https://howtostartanllc.com/dba/dba-wyoming)
- The 2021 SOS fee study proposed raising the trade name fee to $200; the schedule reviewed still lists $100/$50, suggesting the increase was not adopted. (SS) — [Wyoming SOS fee study, Feb 2021](https://wyoleg.gov/InterimCommittee/2021/02-202102222021FeeStudy.pdf)

**New Mexico**
- The NM Secretary of State's "Trademarks" page covers trademarks and service marks only; it says "The registration fee is a minimum of $50, which includes the class code requirement" (each additional class $25) while its instruction 11 says $25 per class plus $25 registration (the page contradicts itself). It does not address trade names, assumed names or LLC DBAs. (P) — [NM SOS, Trademarks](https://www.sos.nm.gov/commercial-services/trademarks/)
- A statute-based survey (statute checked 25 Jul 2026) says "No ordinary business must file a general DBA", "No general DBA fee", "No fixed DBA term or renewal"; its analysis cites NMSA Chapter 53 and concerns corporations, not the LLC Act. (S) — [Ezel, NM assumed-name survey](https://ezel.ai/surveys/assumed-name-registration-requirements/new-mexico)
- FindLaw and Incfile say New Mexico imposes no trade-name registration duty; the Trademark Act (NMSA 57-3-1 to 57-3-12) lets a person apply to the SOS to register a trade name, with a $25 fee per application according to an AG opinion; one filing-service blog describes a five-year renewal (not confirmed). (SS) — [FindLaw](https://www.findlaw.com/smallbusiness/starting-a-business/how-to-get-a-dba-in-new-mexico-in-2-steps.html); [Incfile](https://www.incfile.com/new-mexico-llc/fees-filing-requirements); [NM AG opinion](https://nmonesource.com/nmos/ag/en/14918/1/document.do)
- Ezel's survey also says an NM LLC files no periodic report and pays no recurring charge to the state. (SS) — [Ezel, NM LLC annual report survey](https://ezel.ai/surveys/llc-annual-report-requirements/new-mexico)

**Platforms: is a DBA needed to show a brand?**
- Stripe statement descriptors must contain 5–22 Latin characters, at least one letter, no `< > \ ' " *`, "Reflect your Doing Business As (DBA) name", and not be a single common term or bare URL; the docs use "DBA name" for the brand name customers recognise and do not mention a state DBA filing or certificate. For subscriptions the descriptor can be set per Product or per Invoice. (P) — [Stripe, Statement descriptors](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/statement-descriptors)
- Customers see the Stripe account's business name, website URL, support details and statement descriptor on statements and receipts; "If your customer can't recognize one of your payments, they might dispute it." (P) — [Stripe, Set up your account](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/set-up)
- Stripe: "You must use separate Stripe accounts for projects, websites, or businesses that operate independently from one another"; using one account for separate businesses "can cause confusion" because public business information is shared; each additional account has its own public info; "When you have multiple projects or businesses that operate under the same legal entity, you can use the same tax ID and business information across multiple accounts." (P) — [Stripe, Multiple separate accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/multiple-accounts)
- Apple: organization enrollment requires "a legal entity that can enter into contracts with Apple. We do not accept DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, or branches", and "Your organization's name will be displayed as the seller name of your apps on the App Store." The organization's website must be public, functional and on a domain "associated with your organization." (P) — [Apple, Enrolling as an organization](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)
- Google Play: "Your developer name will appear on Google Play and can be changed any time"; the organization name and address come from the linked Google Payments profile and must match the Dun & Bradstreet profile. (P) — [Play Console Help, required information](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13628312)

### Inferences
- For an LLC run from Uzbekistan that sells software online, no state DBA is a prerequisite for brand names in any of the three states, and none of Stripe, Apple or Google requires one. DBA filings are therefore optional paperwork (a bank or counterparty may still ask for one; LegalZoom says registrants will want the certificate "for banking and contracts" per a search summary (SS) — [LegalZoom, Delaware DBA](https://usaa.legalzoom.com/articles/file-a-dba-in-delaware)).
- Indicative cost if the founder chose to register brand names anyway (arithmetic on the sourced fees): Delaware about $50 (good standing) + $25 (license) + $25 per name = $100 for the first brand and about $25 per further brand if one license can be shared (SS) — roughly $700 for 25 brands; Wyoming $100 per name plus notarization and a mailed filing — about $2,500 for 25 names, and only available if the name is "in use in Wyoming", which an LLC with no Wyoming operations may not satisfy (unclear); New Mexico $0 (no DBA) or about $25 per name under the optional Trademark Act route.
- Brand separation on a platform is partial under one LLC. Stripe allows several Stripe accounts under one legal entity and tax ID (own descriptor, own public info, own payout bank account if wanted). Apple shows the LLC legal name as seller on every app; Google shows one developer name per account. Because Apple will not accept a DBA, the only way to show a different seller name on the App Store is a different legal entity, so the parent LLC's name should be neutral (not one product's brand).

### Gaps
- Official Wyoming and New Mexico trade-name pages could not be fetched (sos.wyo.gov refused connections; NM SOS page does not address trade names); Wyoming and New Mexico facts above are secondary or search-summary only.
- No source states whether Wyoming trade-name registration is mandatory for LLCs, or what "in use in Wyoming" means for online-only use.
- Stripe's docs do not say whether Stripe ever asks for DBA documentation when the public business name differs from the legal name; I found no Stripe page on this.
- Legal duties to disclose the operating entity on websites (consumer-protection, EU/UK trader-identity rules) were not researched.
- Delaware: renewal cadence and annual amount of the Trade Name-Only license are unresolved (FAQ vs search summary vs 2025 law-firm alert).

## 2. Holding LLC + per-product LLCs: per-entity yearly cost, Form 5472 per entity, liability-isolation value, banking and Stripe implications, indie-hacker practice

### Takeaway
Per-product LLCs scale fixed cost, verification burden and filing exposure linearly: at mid-case inputs (state fee + registered agent + Form 5472/pro forma 1120 preparation) a Wyoming or New Mexico LLC costs roughly $325–$385 a year and a Delaware LLC about $725, i.e. about $8k–$18k a year at 25 LLCs versus under $1k for one, before bank, Stripe, Apple and Google onboarding. A holding LLC adds one more entity without any tax consolidation for a foreign-owned disregarded structure, and the (thin, anecdotal) indie-hacker evidence favours one LLC until risk, ownership or exit paths diverge.

- The evidence for "common indie-hacker practice" is anecdotal and points the same way: one LLC (or none) while revenue is small; separate entities when risk, a partner or an exit diverges. No source quantifies how many multi-product indie founders use per-product LLCs.

## 3. Series LLC (Delaware and Wyoming): states, protected-series formalities, EIN and bank per series, treatment by Stripe, banks and app stores, legal uncertainty

### Takeaway
Series LLC statutes exist in more than 20 jurisdictions (Delaware 1996, Wyoming 2018) and can lower per-product state cost (one registered agent, Delaware tax $400 once plus $100 only for optional registered series, Wyoming $10 per series at formation), but I found no Stripe, Apple or Google statement that a series can be a separate account holder, the IRS rule treating each series as a separate entity is still only proposed (2010), and recognition outside series states is untested. In practice the platform accounts would sit with the master LLC, so a series buys internal liability partitioning rather than platform or abuse isolation.

### Cited Findings
**States**
- Wikipedia's list of series LLC statutes: Delaware (1996, first), Wisconsin (2001; "does not specifically provide for a liability shield between the different series"), Iowa and Oklahoma (before April 2005), Illinois and Nevada (2005), Tennessee and Utah (2006), Texas (2009), Montana (2011), Missouri (2013), Indiana (2016), Ohio (2022), Florida (listed "2026"), and Alabama, Arkansas, District of Columbia, Kansas, Minnesota, North Dakota, South Dakota, Virginia, Wyoming, Puerto Rico (years not given); California does not permit series LLCs; Arkansas, Virginia, Nebraska and Iowa have passed the Uniform Protected Series Act. (S, Wikipedia; not a current authoritative list) — [Wikipedia, Series LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_LLC)
- West Virginia SB 670 (2026) adds a Uniform Protected Series Act article to Chapter 31B; the enrolled text is in the signed-bills folder (signing and effective dates not confirmed). An older WealthCounsel list counted 15 series states (AL, DE, IL, IA, KS, MN, MO, MT, ND, NV, OK, TN, TX, UT, WI). **Delaware formalities and fees**

(S) — [Wikipedia, Series LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Series_LLC)

### Inferences
- A series is not, on the evidence found, a separately enrollable legal entity for Apple (DBA/branch language), a separate Stripe account holder (one legal entity and tax ID per account), or a separate D&B legal entity unless it is a Delaware registered series (and even then no source says Apple, Google or Stripe accept it). The practical design is: master LLC holds all platform accounts and bank accounts; series are an internal partition. That gives no platform isolation (section 7).
- Cost comparison (arithmetic on sourced fees): Delaware, 25 unregistered series ≈ one $400 annual tax + one agent ($125 mid) + prep ≈ $725/year (no per-series state fee found for unregistered series); 25 registered series ≈ $400 + 25 x $100 + agent = $3,025/year plus $2,750 formation (25 x $110). Wyoming ≈ $60 + agent + $10 per series at formation ($250 one-time for 25). Compare section 2 (25 separate LLCs: $8k–$18k/year mid case). Form 5472 count under a series is unclear (one filing for the master today; potentially one per series if the proposed rules are finalised) — CPA must confirm.
- The legal-uncertainty evidence argues against putting the highest-risk product (ForeverPin's redirect/phishing exposure) inside a series; it is the case where a real separate LLC (and separate platform accounts) matters most.
- A planned friend/partner venture is awkward in a series: a second member makes that series partnership-classified under the proposed rules, banks and Stripe are unfamiliar with series, and the sale/transfer mechanics (section 4) are untested for a series.

## 4. Selling one product: asset sale from a multi-product LLC vs selling a per-product entity; what marketplaces such as Acquire.com see; how Stripe subscriptions, customer data and app-store listings move

### Takeaway
Per Acquire.com's founder (secondary AMA recap), most deals on the marketplace are asset purchase agreements, which buyers prefer because a stock/entity purchase imports unknown liabilities, and Acquire.com facilitates asset deals by default while still hosting a stock deal the parties agree on. A per-product entity keeps the Stripe account, app-store accounts and contracts in place if the buyer takes the entity, whereas an asset sale needs a buyer-owned Stripe account (customer/card copy plus subscription re-creation), Apple/Google app transfers, and a migration plan.

### Cited Findings
- Acquire.com founder Andrew Gazdecki (FounderPath AMA recap): most deals he sees are asset purchase agreements; "When you buy a whole business with a SPA, you're also acquiring all the liabilities you might not know about"; buyers "are always going to push for an asset purchase agreement"; Acquire.com "works to facilitate asset acquisitions versus stock acquisitions" and "can't, for regulatory reasons, encourage" stock purchase agreements, but can still facilitate one the parties agree; sellers should consider an SPA "from a tax standpoint" (QSBS, which takes five years to qualify). The article does not discuss Stripe or customer transfer. (S, speaker is Acquire.com's founder; Acquire.com's own documentation not found) — [FounderPath AMA recap](https://founderpath.com/blog/saas-acquisitions-lessons-from-500m-in-closed-deals)
- Buyer-side checklist (Venture Lab, 2026): "A buyer often needs a buyer-controlled account, not borrowed access to the seller's legal entity"; "even a Stripe administrator does not automatically become the account owner"; "Subscription migration is operational work, not a single button click"; expect "mapping work, product and price recreation, webhook changes, and a communication plan"; check reserves, payout delays, open refunds and disputes, and who owns pre-close customer claims; app-store listings need formal ownership transfer. It does not cover entity-vs-asset structure or multi-product sellers. (S) — [Venture Lab, buy micro-SaaS questions](https://venture-lab.org/2026/buy-micro-saas-questions/)
- Stripe, entity/ownership change: contact Stripe Support first; the owner/representative information is edited by Stripe enabling editing in the account (not by emailing new owner details); settings include payout bank account, statement descriptor and phone, receipt business info, owner email, legal business name and tax ID; "If your company has been acquired by a legal entity in a different country, the process is different." (P) — [Stripe, Transfer a Stripe account to a different entity](https://support.stripe.com/questions/company-ownership-change)
- Stripe, cross-border acquisition sequence: ownership moves first, the buyer opens a new account in the new business country, then Stripe Support is asked to move the data; Stripe docs say that after activating a service "you can't change the business origin country." (SS for the sequence; P for the origin-country rule) — [Stripe support, acquired by an entity in a different country](https://support.stripe.com/questions/company-acquired-by-a-legal-entity-in-a-different-country); [Stripe, Set up your account](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/set-up)
- Customer/card data copy between Stripe accounts (third-party help docs, not Stripe's own): both sides request Stripe to move payment tokens (source and destination account IDs; usually confirmed within a business day); Stripe Standard accounts have dashboard copy options (all, partial, CSV) with an authorization step on the receiving side; saved cards stay in the old account until moved; subscriptions are re-created separately (Stripe documents migrating subscriptions into Stripe Billing); third-party tools exist (SubPorter from $99 for up to 100 subscriptions; MoveMRR) and are unvetted. (SS) — [Fundraise Up, Stripe migration](https://fundraiseup.com/docs/migration-legacy-stripe/); [Union, Stripe-to-Stripe migration](https://unionfit.helpscoutdocs.com/article/659-stripe-to-stripe-migration-process); [Stripe, migrate subscriptions](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/migrate-subscriptions); [TrustMRR, SubPorter](https://trustmrr.com/startup/subporter)
- Apple app transfer: the Account Holder initiates and the receiving Account Holder accepts; the app stays on sale; "the app retains its reviews and ratings" and keeps its Bundle ID; auto-renewable-subscription apps must use an app-specific shared secret that the initiator hands to the recipient; the transferor keeps pre-transfer payment/sales information and the recipient only gets post-transfer transactions; not transferred or restricted: nominations, matchmaking configuration, Apple Pay merchant ID, Xcode Cloud data (must be removed first), TestFlight (must be off), promo codes after transfer; Sign in with Apple Service ID transfers unless its association is removed first. (P) — [Apple, Overview of app transfer](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/transfer-an-app/overview-of-app-transfer/)
- Apple Small Business Program: "when an app is transferred, the proceeds for the calendar year will be associated with all accounts that initiate or accept the transfer of that app." (P) — [Apple, Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- Google Play transfer: moves users, statistics, comments, ratings, subscriptions, app content declarations and package names; orders created before the transfer, earnings reports and test groups do not move; the target account must be registered and active (new target account costs the $25 fee) and needs an active payments profile for paid apps or in-app products; the original account and apps must comply with policy; Support replies within 2 business days; the target developer must approve. (P) — [Play Console Help, transfer an app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247)
- Founder-community comment that a separate account per SaaS simplifies a later handover, and that in a shared LLC a sale means carving the product's assets out first (the second clause is a summariser's inference). (SS) — [Indie Hackers: multiple SaaS legal structure](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/multiple-saas-legal-business-structure-e3eb1d4a89)
- The IRS says a single-member LLC is "treated as an entity disregarded as separate from its owner" and a multi-member LLC is a partnership by default (relevant to selling a membership interest versus assets, and to a buyer joining a product LLC). (P) — [IRS, Limited liability company](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc)

### Inferences
- Because buyers on the marketplace usually push for an asset deal, a per-product LLC does not guarantee an entity sale; its practical advantages are (a) a clean product-only P&L, bank and Stripe history for diligence, (b) the option of a membership-interest sale in which the Stripe account, Apple/Google accounts, domains and contracts stay where they are, and (c) no carve-out from a shared LLC. Its cost is the section 2 fixed cost from day one.
- In an asset sale out of a shared LLC the seller's LLC and its other products remain with all liabilities; the buyer needs its own Stripe account, so card/customer data copy plus subscription re-creation (renewal timing, double-charge risk, buyer communication) is the main execution work; Apple/Google transfers are documented and preserve ratings.
- Whether a foreign buyer of a US LLC's membership interests triggers Stripe's "different country" path is unclear; if it does, even an entity sale needs a new Stripe account and data migration.
- Design for sellability inside one LLC: give every product its own Stripe account (allowed under one legal entity), domain, repository, email/support address and app-store listing so any product can be transferred (Stripe data copy, app transfer) without untangling shared accounts.

- No source on customer notice, consent, privacy-law or payment-card-data obligations when moving customers to a buyer.

## 5. Apple Developer Program organization enrollment for a US LLC owned by an Uzbek national: D-U-N-S, seller name, cost, eligibility, App Store Connect banking and tax forms (W-9 vs W-8), Small Business Program

### Takeaway
Apple's published rules let a US LLC enroll as an organization ($99/year, D-U-N-S under the exact legal name, organization legal name shown as seller, DBAs rejected) and contain no nationality or residence rule for the Account Holder, but as of September 2026 a widely reported, unresolved App Store Connect defect shows foreign-owned single-member US LLCs only a W-9, which the IRS says a foreign person cannot give, and banking is not processed until tax forms are accepted. Each extra LLC would need its own D-U-N-S, $99 membership and tax form, and all of the founder's LLCs would be "Associated Developer Accounts" sharing one $1M Small Business Program threshold.

### Cited Findings

You must be the organization's owner/founder, executive team member, senior project lead, or an employee with legal authority granted to you by a senior employee"; "a legal entity that can enter into contracts with Apple. We do not accept DBAs, fictitious business names, trade names, or branches"; a D-U-N-S Number; a work email on the organization's domain; a public, functional website on an organization-associated domain (social media pages or minimal sites "won't be accepted"). (P) — [Apple, Enrolling as an organization](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)

(P) — [Apple, enrollment FAQ](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/)

- Region: "Enrollment may not be supported in certain regions, for example due to sanctions or other restrictions." Uzbekistan is not mentioned either way in the pages retrieved; no nationality or residency rule for the Account Holder appears. ### Inferences

Pilot the path with one LLC before creating more.
- One membership holds many apps (Apple's Small Business and transfer pages assume an account holds multiple apps), and the legal-entity name is the seller on all of them. Splitting accounts gives no Small Business Program benefit because every account under common ownership is an Associated Developer Account whose proceeds are summed.
- Choose a neutral legal name for the LLC that will hold the App Store account; a DBA cannot be used, and an app's brand appears only in its title/icon.

- Testing: "personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023" must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for at least 14 days; the article is scoped to personal accounts and does not mention organization accounts. (P) — [Play Console Help, app testing requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)
- Tax information: Google "may be required to collect tax information"; W-9 for US persons, companies and partnerships, W-8BEN/W-8BEN-E for foreign beneficial owners; the page defines a disregarded entity but does not say how a US LLC with a foreign owner should answer; the entity name on the form must match the payments profile; without a valid form "the default rate is generally 30% for Chapter 3 withholding or 24% for backup withholding"; non-US forms refresh every 3 years or on a change of circumstances. (P) — [Google Payments Center, Submit US tax information](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/10349995)
- Service fee (official page is the 2021 version): "the service fee for each developer is 15% for the first $1M (USD) of earnings you make each year"; with multiple Associated Developer Accounts "the 15% service fee tier is applied as long as the total earnings of all ADAs in the group is under $1M"; above $1M the 2021 page says 30% for all ADAs for the rest of the year. (P, 2021 text) — [Play Console Help, 15% service fee](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10632485)

(SS) — [Foxdata](https://foxdata.com/en/blogs/google-play-store-opens-external-billing-fees-drop-to-10-starting-june-30-2026/); [ProPakistani, 27 Jun 2026](https://propakistani.pk/2026/06/27/google-play-store-cuts-fees-and-allows-third-party-billing/)

(P) — [Play Console Help, why accounts get terminated](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2491922)
- App transfers between developer accounts are supported and move ratings, subscriptions and users (see section 4). (P) — [Play Console Help, transfer an app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6230247)

### Inferences
- A US LLC's Google Payments profile is a US profile; the D-U-N-S for that LLC (legal name matching the profile) is the gating item and the same D-U-N-S under the LLC's legal name serves Apple too, so one free D&B record per LLC covers both stores.
- One organization account shows one developer name at a time (changeable), so separate store-facing developer identities per brand require separate accounts, each with its own D-U-N-S and $25 fee; the 15%/10% fee tier is pooled across related accounts, so splitting accounts gives no fee advantage.
- Organization accounts are not named in Google's 12-tester requirement page, so an organization account appears to avoid it (the page is silent; confirm in Play Console before relying on it).
- I found no Google-side equivalent of Apple's reported W-9/W-8 deadlock, but Google's page leaves the same disregarded-entity question to the user's tax adviser; default withholding is 30% or 24% if a valid form is missing.

### Gaps
- Google's current service-fee page (2026 structure) was not retrieved; the 2026 figures are trade-press summaries.
- How a foreign-owned US LLC is routed in Google's tax interview was not found.
- Whether a contact person resident in Uzbekistan is accepted for a US-LLC organization account was not found (no country rule on the page).
- Google's separate 2026 Android developer-verification programme was not researched.

## 7. Risk isolation: how far abuse (for example phishing through a QR redirect service) spreads to liability, Stripe, Apple, Google and domain registrars under each structure

### Takeaway
Separate entities isolate creditor liability better than they isolate platform enforcement: Stripe's agreement allows closure at any time and set-off across affiliates under common control, Google terminates related accounts, Apple expels developers for fraud and dishonesty and tracks control relationships, while domain-level blocklists (Safe Browsing) and registrar actions follow the domain rather than the entity. For a QR/link-redirect product the cheapest effective isolation is separate domains (and registrar/hosting accounts) for that product; a separate LLC mainly protects the other products' assets from that product's creditors.

### Cited Findings
**Stripe**
- Stripe Services Agreement (US): "Unless otherwise agreed in writing, Stripe may terminate this Agreement or close User's Stripe Account at any time" (§10.1(b)(ii)(1)); suspension is allowed if the user "breaches this Agreement or any other agreement between the parties" (§10.1(b)(i)(3)); "termination of this Agreement will not affect any other agreement between the parties or their Affiliates" (§10.2). (P; I read the first 100,000 of 143,000 characters) — [Stripe Services Agreement](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa)
- Cross-account set-off: if a "User Entity" owes a "Stripe Entity", "Stripe or its Affiliate may, to the extent Law permits, deduct, recoup or setoff these amounts from" including "the Stripe Account balance of a User Entity" and "each User Bank Account" (§7.2(c)); the user "authorizes Stripe to debit and credit each User Bank Account without separate notice" to collect amounts owed by "User or another User Entity" (§7.5); "Affiliate" = an entity that directly or indirectly Controls, is Controlled by, or is under common Control with another (Control = more than 50% voting power or equity); "User Entity" = "an individual or entity that is part of the User Group (including you)", and the User Group includes the user's Affiliates that have agreements with a Stripe Entity (§12). (P) — [Stripe Services Agreement](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa)
- A user must not create a Stripe account for "a user whose use of the Stripe services was suspended or terminated by Stripe, unless Stripe approves otherwise" (§1.2(a)(vii)). (P) — [Stripe Services Agreement](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa)
- Stripe docs: additional accounts are subject to standard policies and "[don't] inherit any special status"; Stripe Organizations access can lapse if a linked agreement ends or an account is suspended or closed; closing one account does not close others (each must be closed separately). (P for the first; SS for the others) — [Stripe, Multiple separate accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/multiple-accounts); [Stripe, Organizations terms](https://stripe.com/mx/legal/organizations); [Stripe, Close an account](https://support.stripe.com/questions/close-a-stripe-account)

**Apple**
- App Review Guidelines: "If you attempt to cheat the system (for example, by trying to trick the review process, steal user data, copy another developer's work, manipulate ratings or App Store discovery) your apps will be removed from the store and you will be expelled from the Apple Developer Program"; repeated 4.3(b) "indistinguishable"/low-effort submissions "may lead to removal from the Apple Developer Program"; guideline 4.2.6 rejects apps created from a commercialized template or app-generation service unless submitted directly by the content provider; 4.3(a) forbids multiple Bundle IDs of the same app. (P) — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Developer Program License Agreement §11.2: termination "effective immediately upon notice" if the developer "engage[s], or encourage[s] others to engage, in any misleading, fraudulent, improper, unlawful or dishonest act relating to this Agreement"; and if the developer "or any entity or person that directly or indirectly controls You, is under common control with You... [is] or become[s] subject to sanctions". The only common-control language found concerns sanctions. §3.2 also bars "unlawful, unfair, misleading, fraudulent, improper, or dishonest acts or business practices relating to Your Covered Products." (P; I read only part of the agreement) — [Apple Developer Program License Agreement](https://developer.apple.com/support/terms/apple-developer-program-license-agreement/)
- Apple tracks control: the Small Business Program defines Associated Developer Accounts by >50% ownership or ultimate decision-making authority (section 5). (P) — [Apple, Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)
- Forum reports: an organization account terminated 23 Aug 2026 "under Section 3.2(f)" with "All of our apps... removed from the App Store the same day", no specific trigger named, reinstatement request unanswered after two weeks (A); search summaries of other threads say notices flag accounts tied to other terminated accounts, halt payouts and block app transfers, and warn that replacement accounts may be terminated, with appeal windows of 14 or 30 days (SS/A; threads not opened). — [thread 845120](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/845120); [thread 699218](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/699218); [thread 705020](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/705020)

**Google**
- "If termination is for violating the Distribution Agreement, any related Google Play Developer accounts are also terminated"; new accounts opened after termination are terminated "without a refund." Official pages do not say how relatedness is determined; blogs claim IP, device and payment/contact overlap (unverified). (P for the policy quoted from answer 2491922; SS for the enforcement-process page, which I did not open, and for the heuristics) — [Play Console Help, why accounts get terminated](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/2491922); [Play Console Help, enforcement process](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234)

**Domains, blocklists and registrars**
- Safe Browsing precedents (operator incident reports, search summaries): a listing covered all bit.ly pages for close to 12 hours while branded short domains were spared; statichost.eu customers saw warnings for about six hours on 25 Sep 2025 after phishing on customer subdomains; the code.run wildcard was flagged after one tenant's phishing site; immich.cloud preview environments were repeatedly flagged. Robtex notes Safe Browsing can target specific URLs, and a helpdesk article says Google often flags the subdomain rather than the top-level domain; dedicated domains "generally kept tenants out of the blast radius." (SS/S) — [SecurityWeek, bit.ly](https://www.securityweek.com/google-temporarily-flags-bitly-links-malicious/); [statichost.eu](https://sh.statichost.eu/blog/google-safe-browsing); [Northflank status](https://status.northflank.com/en/clhj2hrjr1525823symzpj0ntbei); [Immich](https://immich.app/blog/google-flags-immich-as-dangerous); [Robtex](https://robtex.com/en/sources/google-safe-browsing)
- Phishers use QR codes to route victims around controls that block suspicious domains (Cofense research as reported). (SS) — [Cyware](https://cyware.com/news/new-phishing-campaign-bypasses-security-controls-by-abusing-qr-codes-to-redirect-victims-795ad0e6)
- Registrars: I found no public Namecheap or Cloudflare Registrar text saying one phishing complaint suspends every domain in an account; terms reserve broad suspension powers (Namecheap may suspend with no warning for severe issues; Cloudflare's registrar terms put liability for misused licensed names on the registrant). A March 2026 ICANN GNSO "straw proposal" would have registrars review other domains of the same customer after confirming abuse, an attorney on that list argued account-wide suspension clauses raise no obvious legal problem, and a poster described one notice disabling 73 domains (unverified). (SS/S/U) — [ICANN GNSO DNS-abuse list, 9 Mar 2026](https://lists.icann.org/hyperkitty/list/gnso-dnsabuse-pdp@icann.org/message/Q2IWJIMRALWYMGJLMWUTPZVRXK5L5KWR/); [Namecheap, why a domain is suspended](https://www.namecheap.com/support/knowledgebase/article.aspx/10626/46/why-has-my-domain-been-suspended/); [Cloudflare Registrar terms](https://www.cloudflare.com/registrar-terms/)

**Legal liability**
- See section 2: the shield between separate LLCs holds only if each is run as a real separate business (own books, bank, contracts); software-specific risk is often handled by contract caps and insurance. (SS) — [Zenind](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/multiple-llcs-explained-when-to-use-separate-llcs-for-different-businesses); [Aaron Hall](https://aaronhall.com/using-multiple-llcs-for-business-line-separation/)

### Inferences
- **One LLC, many brands:** one legal entity means one Apple membership, one Google account and several Stripe accounts under one tax ID (one "User Group"). A phishing incident at ForeverPin can therefore reach (a) all LLC assets via creditors, (b) every Stripe account of the entity (account closure at will plus cross-account set-off), (c) the Apple and Google accounts that hold Pose Coach and Hijinx (Apple expulsion removes all apps; Google terminates related accounts), and (d) only ForeverPin's domains unless the registrar acts account-wide.
- **Holding + per-product LLCs:** creditor liability is confined to the affected LLC if the veil holds; platform actions can still spread because the signals Stripe, Google and Apple state or imply are control and shared identity (common control for Stripe affiliates; "related accounts" for Google; Associated Developer Accounts and reported linkage notices for Apple). A solo founder using the same person, address, bank, payment cards, devices and support email across the LLCs would likely look related; no source says how the platforms detect it.
- **Series LLC:** same platform exposure as one LLC (all accounts under the master LLC) plus the untested statutory partition for creditors.
- Domain/registrar/hosting separation is the lever with documented evidence (blocklists and abuse handling work per domain or subdomain): put ForeverPin's redirect domains on a separate registrar account and hosting account, give customers branded domains where possible, screen destinations at link creation, monitor Search Console, and keep an appeal path (the incident reports' own mitigations).
- Entity separation is therefore mainly justified for creditor/asset protection and sale/partner reasons (section 8), not as a defence against platform contagion.

### Gaps
- No platform document describes how it detects "related" accounts; no documented case found of Stripe closing sibling accounts because one was terminated (only contract text on set-off and affiliates; the SSA excerpt read was incomplete).
- Apple's linkage-termination wording is known only from user-posted notices and search summaries.
- No source on registrar account-wide suspension practice that is current and primary; ICANN-list material is advocacy and proposal text.
- No case law on veil-piercing or tort liability for software LLCs was gathered; insurance (cyber/E&O) costs were not researched.
- Effect on personal liability of the founder if he personally directs the abusive product was not researched (generally personal tort liability is not shielded; no source gathered).

## 8. When to split a product out of the shared LLC (decision triggers; synthesis of sections 1–7)

### Takeaway
The evidence supports one LLC as the default (with per-product Stripe accounts, domains, repositories and app-store listings so any product stays separable) and splitting only on a concrete trigger: a partner joining one product, a planned sale, a materially higher-risk product such as the QR/link redirect service, a need for a distinct App Store seller identity, or product revenue high enough that roughly $325–$725 a year of fixed cost per LLC is small (about $270–$600 MRR for a 10% overhead). A holding LLC and a series LLC do not remove the platform-level spillover that is the main abuse risk.

### Cited Findings
- Practitioner sources list splitting triggers: materially different risk, one product generating far more claim exposure, a co-owner joining only one product, a product that may be sold separately, and ring-fencing an unproven launch; one LLC is reasonable for closely related low-risk products; each extra entity adds filing fees, administration and bookkeeping. (SS) — [Zenind, multiple LLCs](https://www.zenind.com/help/post/multiple-llcs-explained-when-to-use-separate-llcs-for-different-businesses); [LLC University, multiple businesses](https://www.llcuniversity.com/multiple-businesses-under-one-llc/); [Startups for the Rest of Us 609](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/609-building-your-mvp-the-bug-fix-hamster-wheel-and-more-listener-questions)
- A single-member LLC is a disregarded entity; an LLC with two or more members is a partnership by default; an LLC can elect corporate treatment on Form 8832. (P) — [IRS, Limited liability company](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/limited-liability-company-llc)
- Apple shows only the legal-entity name as seller and refuses DBAs (section 5); Stripe allows several accounts under one legal entity (section 1); Apple and Google 15% fee tiers pool related accounts (sections 5–6). (P) — see those sections.
- Sale mechanics and platform contagion evidence: sections 4 and 7.

### Inferences
- **Trigger 1, partner joins one product:** the product needs its own LLC (a co-owner of only that product cannot be a member of the shared LLC without owning part of everything); a second member also changes the default tax classification from disregarded to partnership (IRS), with foreign-partner reporting consequences (see `us-compliance.md` section 7) and a new Apple/Google tax-form question. This is the clearest structural trigger and matches the gate at $2K MRR in the founder's plan.
- **Trigger 2, planned sale:** split before sale only if a membership-interest sale is wanted; marketplace buyers usually prefer asset deals, so a product-level Stripe account, domain and app listing may be enough (section 4). Keep products separable instead of splitting early.
- **Trigger 3, risk class:** the QR/link redirect product (ForeverPin) carries phishing/abuse exposure that can hit domains, Stripe and store accounts (section 7); it is the first candidate for its own LLC and its own domains/registrar/hosting accounts, and should not sit in a series or share Apple/Google accounts with consumer apps.
- **Trigger 4, store identity:** a separate LLC is the only way to show a different seller name on the App Store or a different developer identity on Google Play.
- **Trigger 5, economics:** with mid-case fixed cost per LLC of about $325 (New Mexico), $385 (Wyoming) or $725 (Delaware), a 10% overhead needs about $3,250–$7,250 revenue per year, i.e. about $270–$600 MRR per product (arithmetic on section 2 inputs); at an average of about $200 MRR per product across 25 products (the $5K MRR target) per-product LLCs do not pay for themselves on cost alone.
- **Comparison of the three structures on the questions asked (evidence-weighted):**

| Question | One LLC + brands | Holding + per-product LLCs | Series LLC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entity count / fixed cost | 1 | N+1; $8k–$18k/yr at N=25 (mid) | 1 (+$100/yr per registered series in DE) |
| Stripe | 1 entity, many accounts allowed | 1 account per entity | master LLC (series not documented) |
| Apple / Google accounts | 1 each; one seller name | N each; N D-U-N-S, N tax-form attempts | master LLC (series not documented) |
| Creditor isolation | none between products | strong if veil respected | statutory but untested outside series states |
| Platform/abuse isolation | none (one User Group, one seller) | partial; linkage by common control/identity | none (master LLC) |
| Selling one product | asset sale (buyer opens own accounts) | asset or membership-interest sale | asset sale (series transfer mechanics untested) |
| Partner joins one product | awkward (partner would join the whole LLC) | clean (partner joins that LLC) | awkward/untested |

### Gaps
- Revenue thresholds above are arithmetic on dated third-party prices, not sourced benchmarks; Form 5472 preparation prices vary 15x between vendors.
- No source quantifies how often Apple, Google or Stripe actually propagate enforcement across related entities.
- Uzbek-side tax and legal treatment (CFC rules, reporting of a US LLC) and US tax consequences of a sale for a nonresident alien were out of scope and not researched.
- A US attorney and CPA should confirm entity choice (state, series vs separate LLCs), the Form 5472 count per entity, and the Apple/Google tax-form approach before the first LLC is used for platform enrollment.
