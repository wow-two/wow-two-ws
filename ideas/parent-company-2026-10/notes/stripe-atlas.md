# Stripe Atlas and Stripe account mechanics for a Uzbekistan-national founder running many products under one US company

*Researched 2026-10-08; every "current" claim is as fetched that day. Read-only: no accounts created, no forms submitted, nobody contacted. Evidence labels: **P** = primary (Stripe, IRS, Delaware, Mercury official pages); **S** = secondary; **U** = unverified or my inference. Stripe legal text (Services Agreement, Service Terms, Restricted Businesses list, Unacceptable Risk Policy) and the docs.stripe.com pages (Atlas, payouts, Global Payouts, Organizations, multiple accounts) were read as raw or verbatim page text; support.stripe.com articles, the Stripe Atlas tax guide, and the Delaware and IRS pages were read through a summarising fetcher, so exact figures there are worth a spot-check before they carry weight. Stripe revised its user terms on 2026-09-28 (new users from that date, existing users from 2027-01-06 for General Terms changes); a new Atlas applicant is under the new terms ([Stripe: user terms update, 2026-09-28](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-user-terms-update-september-28-2026)).*

## 1. Atlas: price, entity types, what is included, timeline, and eligibility for a Uzbek national

### Takeaway
Atlas costs $500 once (Delaware state fees and the first year of registered agent included), then $100 a year, for a Delaware LLC, C corp or C-corp subsidiary; Delaware formation takes 1-2 business days and the EIN takes 1-2 business days with an SSN but 10-30 without one. Stripe publishes no country allow-list; the only exclusions I found are sanctions-style (Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, Crimea, Donetsk, Luhansk) and Uzbekistan is not named. The real friction for a Tashkent resident is downstream of Atlas: Mercury, a named Atlas banking partner, lists Uzbekistan as a prohibited residence country (its page was updated 2026-10-07), and whether Stripe Treasury will bank an Uzbek-resident owner is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings

**Price and inclusions (P, fetched 2026-10-08)**
- The Atlas page prices incorporation at $500 one-time, covering government fees and the first year of registered agent services, then $100 per year, renewing automatically and cancellable — [stripe.com/atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas); same figures in [Atlas docs: how to incorporate](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup).
- Included: Delaware incorporation with next-day expedited processing and state fees, a company tax ID (EIN), founder equity issuance and share purchase, 83(b) filing, document templates built with Cooley LLP, $2,500 of Stripe product credits for the first year, and over $50,000 of partner discounts — [stripe.com/atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas).
- The docs add: the EIN comes from IRS Form SS-4 plus Form 8821 so Atlas can call the IRS; a registered agent for year one; a Stripe account created by the sign-up flow; pre-EIN banking and payments; perks with providers such as AWS, Mercury and Carta — [Atlas docs overview](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas), [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup).
- A promotion refunds the $500 fee when you fund a Stripe Treasury bank account with $5,000 within 90 days; the checking accounts are provided by Fifth Third Bank, N.A. — [stripe.com/atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas). Conditions beyond the page footnote were not read.
- Stripe says it refunds the Atlas fee if it cannot support the business, and that Atlas cannot guarantee approval for Stripe payments — [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup). The (pre-2026-09-28) Atlas Terms say Stripe may decline any Atlas service at its sole discretion with no recourse to the founder, and contain no general fee-refund clause — [Atlas Terms, last updated 2025-07-22](https://stripe.com/atlas/legal).
- Under the Atlas service terms effective 2026-09-28, each founder and the company are jointly and severally liable for acts and losses arising from Atlas use; registered agent and filings run through a third-party provider (LegalZoom terms) — [Stripe Service Terms: Stripe Atlas Services](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).

**Entity types and annual costs**
- Atlas forms a Delaware C corp, a Delaware LLC (one founder or several) or a C-corp subsidiary — [company types](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/company-types). The $500 price is quoted once for incorporation, with no separate LLC price — [stripe.com/atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas).
- Stripe's own framing: LLC profits pass through to founders, which is simple for a single US founder, but "tax filings can become complex" with a non-US founder — [company types](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/company-types).
- LLC equity does not vest, so LLC founders need no 83(b) election; Atlas files 83(b) for every C-corp founder. If a founder lacks both an SSN and an ITIN, Stripe tells the IRS the founder is a non-US taxpayer who will seek an ITIN only if they become a US taxpayer — [83(b) for non-US founders](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/83b-elections-non-us-founders).
- Stripe's tax guide: a single-member LLC is a disregarded entity; an LLC owned solely by a non-US individual must file Form 5472; any company incorporated in the US is a US person whoever owns it; most Atlas owners will not need an ITIN — [Stripe guide: business taxes](https://stripe.com/guides/atlas/business-taxes).
- IRS: a foreign-owned US disregarded entity files a pro forma Form 1120 with Form 5472 attached (paper filing, not e-file); the penalty for failing to file is $25,000 per reporting corporation, with a further $25,000 for each 30-day period that the failure continues more than 90 days after IRS notice; contributions to and distributions from the entity are reportable — [IRS Form 5472 instructions (rev. 12/2024, page updated 2026-04-30)](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i5472).
- Recurring Delaware charges, LLC: Atlas docs say a flat $300 annual LLC tax due June 1 (late: $200 penalty plus 1.5% per month), no annual report — [Atlas docs: business taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes). A secondary legal-services summary says HB 400 raised the LLC tax to $400, effective for tax year 2026 and first payable in 2027 — [Capitol Services (S)](https://www.capitolservices.com/insights/legislative-articles/delaware-fee-and-annual-tax-changes/). The enrolled bill was not read.
- Recurring Delaware charges, C corp: Delaware shows a $50 annual report fee (non-exempt domestic), a minimum franchise tax of $175 on the authorized-shares method or $400 on the assumed-par-value-capital method, due March 1, with a $200 late penalty plus 1.5% per month — [Delaware: pay taxes](https://corp.delaware.gov/paytaxes/), [Delaware: calculation methods](https://corp.delaware.gov/frtaxcalc/). Atlas says most Atlas companies pay less on the assumed-par method — [Atlas docs: business taxes](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/business-taxes).
- Delaware annual tax is not prorated: forming before December 31 incurs that year's tax; waiting until January 1 skips a full year — [company types](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/company-types).

**Timeline and application inputs**
- Atlas promises incorporation, banking access and payment activation "within two business days" — [stripe.com/atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas). Delaware filing usually takes 1-2 business days — [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup).
- EIN: 1-2 business days after incorporation if the founder has an SSN, a US address and a US phone number; 10-30 business days without an SSN — [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup). Conflicting figures: Stripe's banking support article says 1-15 business days with those three items and 15-50 without — [Stripe support: how to get a US bank account](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-get-a-us-bank-account); Mercury's 2023 partnership post says non-US founders can wait up to five weeks — [Mercury blog](https://mercury.com/blog/mercury-stripe-atlas-partnership).
- Before the EIN arrives an Atlas company can activate US card payments on Stripe and apply for Treasury; payouts are allowed up to $100,000 after the first-payout waiting period; other payment methods wait for the EIN — [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup).
- Application asks for: entity type; legal name; a company description (used only to screen restricted categories); a website or social profile (used to check you are a real person); a company address (a home address or partner virtual address is allowed); a US phone number (a personal or partner virtual number is allowed, used on the EIN application and needed to activate Stripe payments); each founder's name as on government ID, email and home address (identity verification); the applicant's date of birth; SSN or ITIN (see the 83(b) bullet above for founders with neither) — [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup).
- Atlas address rules: for incorporation any address works, US or non-US, except the registered agent's; for Stripe payments a physical or virtual US address works; most banks need a physical address; Stripe Treasury "and some banking partners" accept non-US physical addresses; a virtual US address can come from Atlas partner Stable — [Stripe support: which address for Atlas](https://support.stripe.com/questions/which-address-should-i-use-for-atlas-incorporation).

**Eligibility, restricted countries, KYC limits**
- The Atlas Terms (2025-07-22 version) define Prohibited Jurisdictions as Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria and the Crimean region, require founders to be 18 or older, and require the company to have no activity, customers, employees or contractors there — [Atlas Terms](https://stripe.com/atlas/legal). The 2026-09-28 Atlas service terms I read do not repeat that clause; sanctions language now sits in the Services Agreement and restricted-businesses list — [Service Terms](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).
- Stripe's Prohibited and Restricted Businesses list (updated 2026-09-22) bars dealings with persons located in, resident in, or citizens of Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, and the Crimea, Donetsk and Luhansk regions — [Stripe: Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses). Uzbekistan is not named.
- Atlas-restricted categories (cannot use Atlas): illegal drugs; marketing certain products to minors; unauthorised or unregulated financial services; hazardous or restricted materials; offensive or discriminatory products; unregulated health services; intellectual property infringement; weapons and violence; deceptive or unethical practices. Atlas-supported but possibly restricted for Stripe Payments: adult content, debt and credit services, cryptocurrency, gambling and betting, NFTs, identity services and insurance, certain financial legal services, "digital promotion products", travel organisations — [Stripe support: Atlas restricted categories](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-atlas-restricted-business-categories).
- Stripe says startups in over 175 countries have used Atlas — [stripe.com/atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas). Stripe's India FAQ (updated Aug 2025) shows Stripe publishes country-specific handling where it matters (India: subsidiary structure for ODI compliance) and says Indian founders can use Atlas — [Stripe support: Atlas in India](https://support.stripe.com/questions/can-i-use-stripe-atlas-if-i-live-in-india). I found no equivalent Stripe page for Uzbekistan.
- Stripe does not list Uzbekistan among countries where a Stripe account can be opened; the United Arab Emirates is listed; Kazakhstan, Georgia, Kyrgyzstan and Armenia are not — [stripe.com/global](https://stripe.com/global). A Uzbekistan-registered business cannot open a direct Stripe account, so the US-entity route is the only direct one.

**Atlas bank partners and what they accept**
- After incorporation, Atlas says to check whether Stripe Treasury is available; "if eligible" apply from the Atlas Dashboard before the EIN arrives; otherwise apply to a third-party US bank — [Atlas docs: business bank accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/payments-business-bank). Stripe's bank-account guide says eligibility varies by provider and founder location and that a passport suffices as personal ID — [Stripe support: how to get a US bank account](https://support.stripe.com/questions/how-to-get-a-us-bank-account).
- Mercury (Atlas perk partner, first pre-EIN banking partner in 2023): its prohibited-countries article, last updated 2026-10-07, lists Uzbekistan (alongside Kyrgyzstan, Pakistan, Vietnam, Indonesia, the Philippines, Nigeria, Ukraine, Russia, Belarus and others). Kazakhstan, Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan, Turkey and the UAE are not on the list. Mercury says it cannot open accounts for founders who live in, or businesses that operate or do business in, listed countries; the test is country of residence, not citizenship, and a citizen of a listed country who lives elsewhere may still qualify after review — [Mercury: prohibited countries (retrieved via the help-center API)](https://support.mercury.com/api/v2/help_center/en-us/articles/28771710754580.json); public article URL [support.mercury.com](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/28771710754580-Prohibited-countries) returned 403 to my fetcher.
- Mercury says it can close accounts for an "unsupportable geography" and often cannot explain closures — [Mercury: account closures](https://support.mercury.com/hc/en-us/articles/43095394066452-Understanding-account-closures-and-access-restrictions). A Hacker News poster quoted a Mercury email giving 2026-05-01 as a closure date for businesses with physical or residential addresses in listed countries (S, user-posted, list not in the thread) — [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47224410).
- Stripe Treasury terms (updated 2026-09-28) require the user to reside in the User Territory, defined as the United States and its territories (excluding some) — [Service Terms: Stripe Treasury](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).

### Inferences
- Nothing published blocks a Uzbek national or Uzbekistan resident from forming an Atlas company; Stripe keeps discretion to decline or terminate (General Terms 10.1(b)(ii)(1)) — [Stripe Services Agreement](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa). The blocking risk is at banking and Stripe-payments underwriting, not at formation.
- LLC and C corp cost the same to form. Delaware's recurring charge is $300 for an LLC today (reportedly $400 from tax year 2026) versus $225 for a C corp only if it has 5,000 or fewer authorised shares ($175 plus the $50 report); Atlas's own note that the default method produces large bills above 5,000 shares suggests Atlas C corps usually use the $400-minimum method, about $450 a year with the report fee (U; Atlas's default authorised-share count was not checked). For a sole non-US owner the LLC carries the Form 5472 and pro forma 1120 duty with the $25,000 penalty. The tax-structure choice belongs to the tax researcher.
- Timing: forming in October to December 2026 starts Delaware's 2026 annual tax (reportedly $400 for an LLC, payable June 2027); forming in January 2027 defers the first Delaware annual tax by a year (derived from the company-types statement that the tax is not prorated).
- Year-one cash cost is roughly $500 plus any virtual US address or US phone number from partners (partner pricing not read); from year two it is $100 plus the Delaware annual tax.
- The 2026-09-26 prior evidence said Mercury's UZ status was unverified; it is now verified as prohibited. Marketing pages from formation companies that suggest Mercury works for Uzbekistan residents conflict with Mercury's own list (these pages were not fetched; only seen as search snippets).

### Gaps
- No Stripe statement on Uzbek nationals or residents using Atlas; I could not test the Atlas application (research-only).
- Stripe Treasury eligibility for an Uzbek-resident owner is unresolved (see section 5, address-rule conflict).
- Conditions on the $5,000 Treasury fee-refund offer; partner pricing for a virtual US address and phone number.
- Other US banks and fintechs (Relay, Brex, Wise, Payoneer, Airwallex and similar) were not researched; the 2026-09-26 evidence that Wise bars Uzbekistan residents from holding balances was not re-verified.
- Delaware HB 400 enrolled text not read; Atlas docs still show the $300 LLC figure.
- Atlas answers on EIN timing conflict across Stripe pages (10-30, 15-50 business days, up to five weeks).

## 2. Opening a Stripe US account for a US LLC whose only owner is a non-US person

### Takeaway
Stripe requires a registered business to be registered in the US even if it operates elsewhere, and needs a verifiable EIN; a non-US-resident representative submits a national ID or verification document at onboarding and no SSN or ITIN is demanded of a non-US person, who instead provides a local tax ID plus proof of non-US-taxpayer status (acceptable documents not listed on the page I read). Atlas companies can start taking card payments before the EIN, and a US phone number is required. A US Stripe account pays out in USD to a bank account in the LLC's own name, or settles into a Stripe financial account where eligible; Stripe's payout documentation offers a Uzbekistan bank account only to "cross-border payouts" accounts, not to a standard US account.

### Cited Findings

**Identity, tax ID, address, phone**
- Registered businesses must be registered in the US "even if you operate in another country"; every business must give the address of the physical location where most business activity happens; PO boxes are refused; Stripe verifies legal name, entity type, EIN, SSN or ITIN and the business address and may ask for official documents — [Stripe support: requirements for a US Stripe account](https://support.stripe.com/questions/requirements-for-having-a-us-stripe-account).
- The 2024 US verification update: US-resident representatives give the last four digits of SSN or ITIN at onboarding; non-US-resident representatives give a national ID or verification document at onboarding; owners follow the same split once payments volume passes $500K; sole proprietors and single-member LLCs must give a business address; all entity types must give an email for the representative — [Stripe support: 2024 verification updates FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/2024-updates-to-us-verification-requirements-faq).
- A representative who is not a US individual and has no SSN or ITIN provides a local non-US tax ID and documentation confirming they are not a US taxpayer; the page links to Stripe Docs for acceptable documents without listing them — [Stripe support: representative and owner tax ID rules](https://support.stripe.com/questions/business-rep-owner-tax-id-requirements-for-us-companies). That page says owners at $500K give the last four digits of SSN or ITIN and is silent on a separate rule for non-US owners.
- A US account needs a verifiable TIN (EIN, or SSN, or ITIN if no EIN); an Atlas company can accept payments before its EIN arrives and Atlas links the EIN afterwards. The same page also says the SSN of individuals associated with every US account is still required, which conflicts with the 2024 FAQ's non-US branch above (the page does not discuss foreign owners and may predate the 2024 change) — [Stripe support: signing up without a tax ID](https://support.stripe.com/questions/signing-up-for-a-us-stripe-account-without-a-tax-id-or-employer-id-number).
- If Stripe cannot verify an address automatically it may ask for a lease, utility bill, tax return, or articles of incorporation or other government business document naming the business or the account owner — [Stripe support: verifying your business address](https://support.stripe.com/questions/verifying-your-business-address).
- Atlas separately says a physical or virtual US address works for Stripe payments, and that activating Stripe Payments needs a US phone number — [Stripe support: which address for Atlas](https://support.stripe.com/questions/which-address-should-i-use-for-atlas-incorporation), [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup). Applying to Stripe payments triggers extra review of the Atlas information — [signup docs](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/signup).
- Stripe's physical-presence rule applies from 2025-07-01 to Treasury, Issuing and Opal on US accounts: both the business address and the account representative's address must be a physical US address; registered-agent, mailbox and virtual addresses do not count; payment processing is explicitly unaffected; beneficial owners who are not the representative may use such addresses — [Stripe support: physical presence FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/physical-presence-requirement-faq).
- The Services Agreement fixes the Stripe entity and regional terms by "Stripe Account Country", the country of the business address in the account details; the US terms use California law and arbitration in San Francisco — [Stripe Services Agreement: General Terms](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).

**Payouts and the US bank-account requirement**
- Under the Services Agreement a Stripe-required User Bank Account must be held in the user's own name (the LLC) in a country Stripe approves — [General Terms 7.4](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).
- Bank accounts generally must be in a country whose official currency is the settlement currency, and the account currency must match payout settings; virtual accounts (N26, Revolut, Wise) are accepted but may fail more often; for eligible US businesses proceeds settle into a Stripe financial account and an external bank account is needed only for external payouts; first payouts typically take 7-14 days after the first live payment — [Stripe docs: payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts).
- The same payouts page shows the Uzbekistan (UZ) bank format (bank code, branch code, account number) and states that Uzbekistan is available only to "cross-border payouts" accounts; the US entry shows only a US routing and account number — [Stripe docs: payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts).
- Connect cross-border payouts are self-serve only between platforms and connected accounts in the US, UK, EEA, Canada and Switzerland; for other countries Stripe points to sales or Global Payouts — [Connect cross-border payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts).
- Multi-currency settlement is available to US accounts, with a separate bank account per currency; the per-currency table of supported bank countries is rendered dynamically and I could not read it — [Stripe docs: multi-currency settlement](https://docs.stripe.com/payouts/multi-currency-settlement).
- Stripe's country list for opening accounts omits Uzbekistan — [stripe.com/global](https://stripe.com/global).

### Inferences
- A Uzbek owner can in principle satisfy Stripe's KYC without an SSN or ITIN: passport-type ID at onboarding plus a non-US tax ID and non-US-taxpayer evidence if Stripe asks. Which tax-status document Stripe accepts is the open item (U).
- The business-address instruction ("where most business activity happens", no PO box) and Atlas's "virtual US address is fine for payments" can both be right only if Stripe's payments flow accepts either; which one the Dashboard enforces for a foreign-operated US LLC is not documented (U). Because the Payments terms let the card networks make the final determination of the user's location (see section 4), a Tashkent-run business with a virtual US address carries a location-mismatch risk (U).
- Payout path in practice: Stripe settles USD to a US bank account in the LLC's name or to a Stripe financial account. A personal Uzbek bank account cannot be the payout account on a standard US Stripe account (General Terms 7.4 plus the payouts page). Mercury is not available to a Tashkent resident, so the realistic Stripe-native option is Stripe Treasury if it approves the owner.
- The only documented Stripe route for USD-to-UZS money movement is Global Payouts out of a financial account (section 5).

### Gaps
- No Stripe page lists the "documentation that confirms you are not a US taxpayer" that a non-US representative may need.
- Unknown whether the Dashboard accepts a non-US business address on a US account, and how Stripe resolves the Atlas "virtual address works" statement against the support page's "physical location of majority of activity".
- Stripe's own pages disagree on whether non-US individuals need an SSN (the 2024 FAQ and tax-ID article say a national ID or non-US tax ID is enough; the no-tax-ID article says SSN is still required for individuals on every US account); no dated resolution was found.
- Unknown which business type (Company versus sole proprietor) a foreign-owned single-member LLC should select.
- The multi-currency settlement table (supported bank countries for a US account) was not retrievable.
- No firsthand reports from Uzbek or Central Asian founders about Stripe approving an Atlas LLC were found; the search surfaced only formation-company marketing (not cited).

## 3. One legal entity, several Stripe accounts: multiple accounts, Organizations, descriptors, shared verification

### Takeaway
Stripe's documentation expects separate accounts for projects, sites or businesses that operate independently, and explicitly lets one legal entity reuse the same tax ID and business information across several accounts, each with its own public business information and statement descriptor. Organizations are an optional container (up to 75 accounts, all affiliates, jointly and severally liable) for consolidated reporting and team access; nothing in the docs says verification status is shared, and new accounts get no inherited standing.

### Cited Findings
- Stripe says to use separate accounts for projects, websites or businesses that operate independently; a new account follows standard policies and pricing and inherits no special status from an existing account — [Stripe docs: multiple separate accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/multiple-accounts).
- Each account can carry the tax ID and legal entity of one business; where several projects sit under one legal entity the same tax ID and business information can be reused across accounts, with suitable public business information to avoid customer confusion; each additional account can use the same or a different payout bank account — [Stripe docs: multiple separate accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/multiple-accounts).
- Extra accounts are created from the Dashboard account menu ("New account") under the same email; for large numbers of accounts created automatically Stripe recommends a Connect platform — [Stripe support: create and manage multiple accounts](https://support.stripe.com/questions/create-and-manage-multiple-stripe-accounts). Stripe also documents running separate business lines as connected accounts under one platform even if the business is not a marketplace — [Organizations setups](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/orgs/setup).
- The support article is silent on whether each account needs its own identity verification and on whether risk actions spread between accounts — [Stripe support: create and manage multiple accounts](https://support.stripe.com/questions/create-and-manage-multiple-stripe-accounts).
- Organizations: a Dashboard container for reporting, team management, SSO and Sigma queries across accounts; use cases listed include separate business units and global expansion; shared customer and payment-method records are in public preview — [Stripe docs: Organizations](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/orgs).
- Building one: creator must be Super Administrator on each account; up to 75 accounts; each account in only one organization; a new account created inside an organization can optionally copy a legal entity, business details or payout bank account from existing accounts; removing every account permanently closes the organization — [Stripe docs: build an organization](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/orgs/build).
- Organizations terms (last modified 2025-11-18): all accounts in an organization must be Affiliates; Stripe may remove an account at its discretion; account data is shared across the organization; while in an organization the legal entities are jointly and severally liable for activity related to Organizations Services — [Stripe Service Terms: Stripe Organizations](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).
- Statement descriptor rules per account: 5-22 Latin characters, at least one letter, none of `< > \ ' " *`, must reflect the DBA name, and not be a single common term or bare URL; a card prefix of 2-10 characters plus a dynamic suffix is supported; subscription and invoice charges can take a descriptor set on the Product, so products inside one account can show different descriptors — [Stripe docs: statement descriptors](https://docs.stripe.com/get-started/account/statement-descriptors).
- A prohibited use under Stripe's list is processing for products or services that were not disclosed in the account application, or on behalf of an undisclosed merchant; and a change of business model without Stripe's consent is a listed remedy trigger — [Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses), [Service Terms: Stripe Payments 5.3(l)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).

### Inferences
- Thirty products under one LLC can map to thirty Stripe accounts with the same EIN and legal entity, each with its own public name, website, descriptor and (optionally) bank account; the 75-account organization cap covers that scale (U on whether Stripe throttles creation).
- Each account is underwritten on its own declared product and website, so a product's account should describe that product only; a second product's revenue running through the first product's account would be an undisclosed-product breach (inference from the prohibited-use and business-model clauses).
- If a partner later owns a product through a separate LLC, that LLC needs its own account (one legal entity per account); placing it in the same Organization requires affiliate status and creates joint and several liability under the Organizations terms, so keeping it outside the Organization avoids that clause (U; the Services Agreement "User Group" definition in section 4 may link it anyway).

### Gaps
- No Stripe statement that verification, KYC results or risk standing are shared or inherited between accounts of one entity; the only sharing documented is optional copying of entity, business details and bank details when creating an account in an organization.
- No documented cap on accounts per entity or per email other than the 75-account organization limit.
- Connect-platform-as-container (platform bears responsibilities for connected accounts) was only noted, not examined.

## 4. Risk linkage between accounts, and restricted or prohibited business categories

### Takeaway
Stripe's contract links accounts by "User Group": the user, any individual or entity Stripe reasonably decides is associated with the user, and affiliates with Stripe agreements. Remedies (reversals, delayed or cancelled payouts, reserves, suspension) can be triggered by what any User Entity does, setoff and reserve funding reach across User Entities, and Stripe may share User Group information with card networks including terminated-merchant lists. None of the Stripe lists I read names link shorteners, QR or redirect services, transcription services, or party-game content packs; the adjacent risks are intellectual-property infringement, harmful or facilitated abuse, adult or offensive content, and unclear subscription pricing.

### Cited Findings

**Linkage in the contract (Stripe legal text, raw)**
- "User Group" means the user, any entity or individual Stripe reasonably determines is associated with the user, and each affiliate of those with a Stripe services agreement; "User Entity" means anyone in the User Group — [Services Agreement 12 (Definitions)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).
- If a Stripe entity cannot collect what a User Entity owes, Stripe may recoup from a Reserve of any User Entity, funds payable to a User Entity, any User Entity's Stripe balance, bank accounts and backup payment methods — [General Terms 7.2(c)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).
- Payments Terms 5.3: Stripe may apply its remedies if it reasonably determines that a User Entity has excessive disputes, refunds or reversals, a negative balance, a material deterioration, a breach of this or any other Stripe services agreement, a violation of law, a change of control, suspicious or fraudulent activity, an unconsented business-model change, or conduct presenting an unacceptable risk; 5.5 lists the remedies: reversals, changed or delayed or cancelled payouts (to bank accounts or financial accounts), a Reserve, suspending or terminating processing, and refusing refunds — [Service Terms: Stripe Payments](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).
- A Reserve may be changed when the risk of the User Group's use changes, may be funded from money any Stripe entity owes any User Entity for transactions the User Group accepts, and sits under a security interest held for all Stripe entities — [Service Terms: Stripe Financial Services Terms 3.3-3.4](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).
- Stripe may share account and User Information, including information about the User Group, with card-network and acquirer partners, and the user waives claims arising from that sharing including inclusion on a terminated-merchant list — [Service Terms: Stripe Payments 3.6](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).
- A user may not try to open an account for the benefit of a user whose Stripe use was suspended or terminated, unless Stripe approves — [General Terms 1.2(a)(vii)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa). Stripe may suspend immediately for reasonably believed unlawful or harmful activity, fraud-rate increases, breaches, or failure to supply requested information, and may close an account at any time — [General Terms 10.1(b)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).
- Counter-signal: termination of the Services Agreement does not by itself end any other agreement between the parties or their affiliates, so no clause I read says one account's closure automatically closes another — [General Terms 10.2](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).
- Unacceptable Risk Policy (updated 2026-07-29), applying to all Payments users: Stripe may pause charges, pause payouts, reverse payments and begin closing the account; payouts are typically paused for 120 days from the first notice, followed by a formal review that may release, keep holding, or cancel the remaining balance; appeals are available in some cases — [Stripe: Unacceptable Risk Policy](https://stripe.com/legal/unacceptable-risk-policy).
- Terminated-merchant files (Mastercard MATCH, Visa VMSS): listings hold the business and the principal owner's name, address, phone and tax ID, last 5 years, and most processors, Stripe included, automatically reject listed businesses or principal owners; only the listing acquirer can correct a listing; qualifying reasons include fraud, illegal transactions, excessive chargebacks (Mastercard thresholds 1% and $5,000 monthly) and excessive fraud — [Stripe docs: terminated merchant files](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/match).
- The Payments Terms say card networks may ask for the user's location and make the final determination of it — [Service Terms: Stripe Payments 3.4(b)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms). A listed prohibited use is cross-border acquiring where the merchant's business address is outside the acquiring Stripe entity's jurisdiction unless card-network rules allow it — [Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).
- I found no founder report (Indie Hackers, Hacker News or Reddit results) showing one account's closure causing closure of a sibling account; the searches returned generic single-account closure stories only (S, negative finding).

**Category screen against the three product types (Stripe list updated 2026-09-22)**
- Not named anywhere on the list: link shorteners, URL redirects, QR code services, transcription or speech-to-text, scraping or downloading video, party games — [Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).
- Prohibited, adjacent: products or services that infringe or facilitate infringement of copyright, trademark or privacy rights; unfair or deceptive practices including no-value-added resale of a service and negative-option or reduced-price-trial offers with unclear pricing; adult content including pornography and mature content for sexual gratification; games of chance and games of skill with a monetary or material prize — [Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).
- Restricted, adjacent: sale of in-game currency or items unless the seller operates the virtual world; content-creation platforms that let third parties sell digital goods; cyberlocker and file-sharing services — [Prohibited and Restricted Businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses).
- The Services Agreement lets Stripe suspend where it reasonably believes a business may be unlawful, enables or facilitates illegal or prohibited transactions, may be harmful to a third party, or presents unacceptable risk — [General Terms 10.1(b)(i)(5)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa).
- The Atlas restricted-categories page lists "digital promotion products" as supported by Atlas but possibly restricted for Stripe payments; it does not define the term — [Stripe support: Atlas restricted categories](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-atlas-restricted-business-categories).

### Inferences
- Contractually, several Stripe accounts held by the same LLC and owner are one User Group: abuse or a dispute spike on one product can justify holds, reserves or setoff against the others, and a termination can put the business and its principal owner (the founder personally) on a terminated-merchant file for five years. Separate accounts reduce operational blast radius but do not separate legal exposure (inference from the definitions and 5.3, 7.2(c), 3.6, MATCH).
- Because the principal owner's personal details go on MATCH, a separate legal entity per product does not insulate the founder if he controls it (inference).
- ForeverPin: no category rule applies on its face; the live risk is abuse of redirects (phishing or malware) falling under harm or facilitation clauses, plus file hosting for PDF or menu targets brushing against the cyberlocker restriction (U). Clear subscription pricing and cancel terms matter given the negative-option clause.
- Transcript Forge: transcription of user-supplied audio is not a listed category; a tool built to fetch and transcribe third-party video could attract the copyright-facilitation clause (U; depends on the product's actual flow).
- Hijinx: selling the operator's own content packs is outside the "in-game items" restriction (that restriction exempts the operator); risks are adult or discriminatory content in packs, prize-linked play, and any third-party sale of packs, which would turn it into a content-creation platform (U).
- A business run from Uzbekistan but registered with a virtual US address combines two flagged themes: card-network location determination and the cross-border-acquiring prohibition (U).

### Gaps
- Stripe does not publish how it decides that an entity or individual is "associated" with a user, nor whether closure of one account automatically triggers review of others; no primary or credible secondary report was found.
- No Stripe statement on whether QR or redirect SaaS, or YouTube-adjacent transcription, is treated as restricted; only absence from the published list.
- Appeals and reopening: the Unacceptable Risk Policy describes appeals; the support page on closing accounts (not fetched) is described only in third-party snippets, so reopening odds are unknown.
- Whether a given Stripe termination leads to a MATCH or VMSS listing turns on the reason code: Stripe's page says acquirers must list a terminated merchant that meets the criteria, that closing an account does not prevent a later listing, and that Stripe does not disclose listing information to users — [Stripe docs: terminated merchant files](https://docs.stripe.com/disputes/match). Which of an owner's accounts would be listed is not stated.

## 5. `uz_bank_account` and moving money from a US company to a Uzbekistan bank account

### Takeaway
The 2026-02-25 changelog added `uz_bank_account` (with 11 other countries) as a value of `default_outbound_destination` on Accounts v2 recipient configuration, so a Uzbek bank account can be a Global Payouts destination. Global Payouts is available to businesses located in the US and UK, runs out of a funded Stripe Treasury financial account, and lists Uzbekistan as UZS by wire for individual and company recipients. It is a payer-side tool for a business paying third parties; Stripe's documents neither permit nor forbid paying the sender's own owner or a related Uzbek company. The unresolved gate is whether a Tashkent-resident owner can obtain a Treasury financial account at all.

### Cited Findings

**What the changelog says**
- The 2026-02-25 entry adds 12 cross-border payout destinations, including `uz_bank_account` for Uzbekistan, to the read-only `default_outbound_destination` field on the Accounts v2 recipient configuration; recipients can create bank accounts via Outbound Setup Intents and receive payouts from Outbound Payments; it is an API-version `2026-02-25.preview` change — [Stripe changelog: cross-border payout destinations](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/clover/2026-02-25/cross-border-payouts-new-countries). The other additions are Bhutan, Costa Rica, Dominican Republic, Guatemala, Moldova, North Macedonia, Macao, Mozambique, Peru, Pakistan and Taiwan.
- The Global Payouts docs: available to businesses located in the US and UK; private preview for several EU countries; it needs Treasury, a funded financial account, recipients created via API or Stripe-hosted forms, then sends to more than 160 countries; Stripe describes it as best for businesses that already hold a money transmitter licence and as decoupled from payments — [Stripe docs: Global Payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts).
- The send-money guide says to make sure you have Treasury access first, and sends via Dashboard (Treasury, Payouts, Send money) or the v2 Outbound Payments API with a restricted key; its examples use an API version labelled `2026-09-30.preview`; it also carries an early-access sign-up for cross-border payouts — [Stripe docs: send money](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts/send-money).

**Uzbekistan specifics (US sender)**
- Recipient requirements: individuals need email, name, a non-PO-box address and phone number; companies need email, phone, company name and a non-PO-box address; the payout is in UZS by wire — [Stripe docs: recipient requirements](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts/recipient-requirements).
- Minimum payout to Uzbekistan is 343,000 UZS (source and destination minimums both apply); typical arrival for payouts outside the US is 1-7 days; the US sender's wire maximum is 10,000,000 USD, standard 1,000,000 USD; no Uzbekistan-specific maximum was listed — [Stripe docs: payout methods](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts/payout-methods).
- Fees (US sender, fetched 2026-10-08): funding by ACH or wire is free; local-bank payouts cost $1.50; wire payouts cost $25 per wire (US only); cross-border payouts add 0.25-1.25% depending on the destination country plus an FX fee of 1% (US sender, conversions other than among USD, EUR and GBP, which cost 0.50%); Uzbekistan does not appear in the published per-country cross-border fee table — [Stripe docs: Global Payouts pricing](https://docs.stripe.com/global-payouts/pricing).
- Treasury terms (updated 2026-09-28): the user must reside in the "User Territory" (the United States and most of its territories); Stripe may require source-of-funds information on a transaction and may limit use until satisfied — [Service Terms: Stripe Treasury 1.2, 2.2(a)](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms). Global Payouts Fiat terms (last modified 2026-04-24): payouts go from the user's Financial Account to Recipients, there may be limits on number and value shown in the Dashboard, and the user must link Stripe's privacy policy in contracts with Recipients — [Service Terms: Stripe Global Payouts Fiat](https://stripe.com/legal/ssa-service-terms).
- Treasury access: Atlas says Treasury is for those "eligible"; Stripe's physical-presence FAQ (from 2025-07-01) requires a physical US address for the business and for the account representative on Treasury, and rejects registered-agent, mailbox and virtual addresses — [Atlas docs: bank accounts](https://docs.stripe.com/atlas/payments-business-bank), [physical presence FAQ](https://support.stripe.com/questions/physical-presence-requirement-faq). Stripe's Atlas address article says Treasury "and some banking partners" accept non-US physical addresses — [Stripe support: which address for Atlas](https://support.stripe.com/questions/which-address-should-i-use-for-atlas-incorporation). The legacy Treasury-for-platforms page lets owners reside in over 150 countries with enhanced review for some, bars sending or receiving funds outside the US from a platform-provided financial account, and bans registered-agent addresses — [Treasury for platforms requirements](https://docs.stripe.com/treasury/requirements); that page covers a different product and is cited only as a contradictory signal.
- The Connect route (cross-border payouts to connected accounts) is self-serve only within the US, UK, EEA, Canada and Switzerland, so it does not reach a Uzbek bank account — [Connect cross-border payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts).
- Stripe's Outbound Payments `purpose` field exists only to flag payroll for NACHA rules on US ACH; no purpose value for owner distributions or intercompany payments is documented — [Stripe changelog: outbound payment purpose](https://docs.stripe.com/changelog/clover/2026-02-25/outbound-payments-purpose).

### Inferences
- Technically the flow Stripe payments, then financial account, then Global Payouts wire to a named Uzbek bank account in UZS appears supported for a US company, and the recipient can be an individual or a company. Whether the owner or a related Uzbek company is an acceptable recipient is unaddressed in Stripe's documents (U); the Treasury terms' source-of-funds and limit provisions mean Stripe can ask questions or restrict the flow.
- Fee arithmetic (my calculation from the published table; assumes Stripe converts USD to UZS, since the recipient table lists UZS, and the stacking order is unverified): lower bound is the $25 wire fee plus 1% FX; upper bound adds the top 1.25% cross-border fee and the $1.50 standard fee. A $200 payout costs about $27-$31 (13.5-15.5%); $1,000 about $35-$49 (3.5-4.9%); $5,000 about $75-$139 (1.5-2.8%) (U). The flat $25 dominates small payouts, which favours batching.
- Uzbek-side currency-control and tax treatment of the receipts, and the receiving bank's own inbound fees, are outside Stripe's documents and outside this note.
- The prior evidence entry ("cross-border payouts: you as recipient, Global Payouts and Accounts v2 preview; not a way to charge customers") is confirmed on the destination point; what is new is that Global Payouts lists the US as an available sender location and publishes UZS wire requirements, minimums and fees.

### Gaps
- Treasury eligibility for an Uzbek-resident owner is unconfirmed, and Stripe's own pages conflict: the Atlas address article says Treasury accepts non-US physical addresses, the physical-presence FAQ requires US physical addresses for business and representative. Ask Stripe, or test through the Atlas Dashboard (not done).
- The send-money page carries a "request early access to cross-border payouts" banner while the pricing and recipient pages treat cross-border sends as live for US senders; whether a new Atlas company can send to Uzbekistan self-serve today is unconfirmed.
- No per-country cross-border fee for Uzbekistan is published, and the pricing page does not state whether the $25 wire fee replaces or adds to the $1.50 standard fee.
- Per-account Dashboard limits for Outbound Payments are not published; Stripe says they appear in the Dashboard.
- Exchange-rate margin on USD to UZS conversion is not documented in the pages read.
- No Stripe statement permits or forbids paying the sender's own owner or a related company; no Stripe-listed purpose code for it.
- Prior-evidence items not re-verified (outside scope): Paddle, Polar, Dodo, Lemon Squeezy, Creem, FastSpring, Wise and Payoneer statements, UZ-side tax figures.
