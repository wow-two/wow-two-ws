## 1. CFC rules in the Tax Code: articles, effective dates, thresholds, exemptions, taxation of undistributed profit, notices, penalties

### Takeaway
Uzbekistan has full CFC rules (Tax Code Arts. 39-40, 203-209, penalty Art. 227) in force since 1 Jan 2022, and they expressly cover resident individuals: a foreign company controlled by a resident (more than 25% from 1 Jan 2023) is a CFC, and its undistributed profit is added to the owner's personal income tax base - at the general 12% rate - only if it exceeds UZS 300 million (about $25.4k) for the period and no narrow exemption applies. Notices are due within one month of acquiring a participation and by 20 March after any year in which CFC profit is counted; omitted CFC profit is fined 20% of the unpaid tax, minimum UZS 10 million.

### Cited Findings
**Where the rules sit and when they apply**
- [Official] The Tax Code table of contents places Art. 39 (controlled foreign companies) and Art. 40 (control over foreign companies, controlling persons) in Chapter 4; Part VII "controlled foreign companies: tax conditions and general rules" is Chapter 26, Arts. 203-209 (Art. 209 = notices on participation in foreign entities and on CFCs); Art. 331 (Chapter 45, Part XII, profit tax) holds specifics for taxing CFC profit of legal entities - [lex.uz Tax Code](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/4674902).
- [Code] Part VII carries the note that it took effect 1 Jan 2022 - [mirror p16](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/16/1600000); [Big4] PwC: "CFC rules came into force on 1 January 2022", last reviewed 27 Aug 2026, and the page gives no thresholds - [PwC Uzbekistan group taxation](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/group-taxation).
- [Big4] No 2026 change to the CFC rules was found. EY's summary of the 25 Dec 2025 Law ZRU-1108 (effective 1 Jan 2026 unless stated) covers only transfer-pricing changes (a transfer-pricing audit type and an interest-free-loan carve-out) and does not mention CFCs - [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uz/technical/tax-alerts/2026/01/uzbekistan-tax-code-amendments-2026). The Code mirror read (current to 19.07.2026) still shows the 25% / 10% thresholds, so no later change to Art. 40 is visible.

**Definition and control test (Arts. 38-40)**
- [Code] Art. 39: a foreign legal entity is a CFC if (1) it is not an Uzbek tax resident and (2) its controlling persons are Uzbek tax-resident legal and/or natural persons; the same test applies to foreign unincorporated structures - [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000).
- [Code] Art. 40(1): controlling person = an Uzbek tax resident holding more than 25% of the company, or more than 10% if all Uzbek tax residents together hold more than 50%; item 1 is marked "in force from 1 January 2023" - [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000). Before 2023 the test was more than 50% (exactly 50% = not controlling) - [buxgalter.uz 2022 expert comment](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text180563_esli_rezident_vladeet_doley_v_inostrannoy_kompanii).
- [Code] Art. 38: share = direct + indirect (multiply along each chain); Art. 40(3): a natural person's share is aggregated with listed relatives (spouse, parents, children, siblings, etc. - Art. 37(3)(11)); Art. 40(5): a person below the thresholds is still a controlling person if it controls the company for its own or its relatives' benefit; Art. 40(6): "control" = power to determine decisions on distributing net profit, through holdings, a management agreement or other relations; Art. 40(13): a resident may self-identify as a controlling person and must notify the tax authority - [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000).
- [Big4] PwC adds that the first return including CFC profit of individuals was for 2023, filed in early 2024, that the first CFC notices were due by 20 March 2024, and that Uzbekistan joined the OECD Global Forum in 2022 so CFCs of Uzbek citizens are expected to surface through information exchange - [PwC Uzbekistan via spot.uz, Apr 2023](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/). PwC's own CFC page confirms residents (individuals and entities) owning or controlling foreign entities or structures are in scope - [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/corporate/group-taxation).

**How undistributed profit is taxed (Arts. 203, 208, 366, 367, 375)**
- [Code] Art. 203: Uzbek tax residents pay tax on CFC undistributed profit; legal entities add it to the profit-tax base, individuals add it to the personal income tax (PIT) base; it is applied per CFC and per controlling person - [mirror p16](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/16/1600000).
- [Code] Art. 366 includes CFC profit in an individual's PIT base; Art. 375(10) classes it as property income; no separate CFC rate was found, so the resident rate applies - [mirror p28](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/28/2800000); PwC confirms "no special rate ... standard PIT rate of 12%" - [spot.uz/PwC](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/).
- [Code] Art. 367: CFC profit is deemed received on the last day of the tax period (calendar year) following the calendar year in which the CFC's financial year ends - [mirror p28](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/28/2800000). Example by that rule: FY2025 profit is treated as received 31 Dec 2026, declared in the 2026 return.
- [Code] Art. 208: CFC profit is computed under the Code's profit rules (the text says "Section XI"; the profit-tax part appears as Section XII in the current numbering - cross-reference looks stale); it is reduced by dividends paid in the calendar year after the financial year, including interim dividends paid during the financial year; the controller's share = its holding on the date of the distribution decision, or at period end if no decision by 31 December; profit that foreign law requires to go into charter capital is not counted; a zero amount is not shown in the return; "profit is counted only if it exceeds 300 million sums" for the tax period; the controller files the CFC's financial statements (translated into Uzbek) and, where an audit is mandatory under home law or the charter or is done voluntarily, the audit opinion - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000). Same 300M and 31 Dec points confirmed by a State Tax Committee expert answer - [buxgalter.uz Jan 2023](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text187507_kak_uchest_pribyl_kik_v_nalogooblojenii_predpriyatiya).
- [Code] Art. 208(5): Uzbek-entity dividends are excluded from CFC profit if the controller actually receives them and is exempt or has paid tax on them - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000). PwC: reinvesting CFC profit does not reduce the base; dividends in the following year do - [spot.uz/PwC](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/).
- [Code] Art. 207: effective tax rate (ETR) = foreign income tax of the company and its separate units plus tax withheld at source, divided by total profit; if there is no income or total income is zero/negative, no ETR is computed and the CFC is deemed to meet Art. 204(1) - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000). The rate benchmark in Art. 204(1)(3) is the profit-tax rate in Art. 337 para. 12, which is the general 15% rate - [mirror p16](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/16/1600000), [mirror p26](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/26/2600000).

**Exemptions (Arts. 204-206)**
- [Code] Art. 204(1): CFC profit is exempt if at least one applies: (1) non-commercial organisation that by home law does not distribute profit; (2) active foreign company or active foreign holding company; (3) ETR not below the Art. 337 para. 12 rate (15%); (4) bank or insurer licensed under foreign law - [mirror p16](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/16/1600000), [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000).
- [Code] Art. 204(2): ground (2) is denied if the CFC's place of permanent location is on the offshore list (Art. 181 parts 3-4); Art. 204(3): grounds (3) and (4) require that the location state has a tax treaty with Uzbekistan; Art. 204(6): the ground must be documented and filed within the Art. 209 deadlines, translated into Uzbek as needed - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000). PwC summarises the same list and calls the exemptions "extremely limited" - [spot.uz/PwC](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/).
- [Code] Art. 205: active company = passive income no more than 20% of total income for the financial year (unconsolidated statements under foreign law); holding company = Uzbek controller holds at least 75% directly for 365 consecutive days, passive income (excluding dividends from active subsidiaries) no more than 5%, and at least 50% direct stake in each active subsidiary for 365 days - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000).
- [Code] Art. 206: passive income = items 1-12: dividends; distributions on liquidation; interest; royalties; gains on shares/rights; derivatives; sale of immovables; most leasing; fund units; "consulting, legal, accounting, audit, engineering, advertising, marketing and information-processing services" plus R&D and design-engineering; personnel-provision services; and "similar" income. Item 13 "other income" is active - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000). PwC reads the broad list as narrowing "active" to supply of goods and works/services not on the list (construction as example) and says nothing on software - [spot.uz/PwC](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/).
- [Code] Art. 44 (royalties) excludes from "royalty": use of a computer program limited to its functional purpose for end consumption, software development/adaptation/configuration services, newly generated information under a customer contract, and transfer of a distribution right without reproduction rights; copyright "including software" and know-how otherwise are royalties - [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000).
- [Press/Pro] State Tax Committee explanation: an active company's profit is exempt; if the active share is 80% or more the whole profit (active and passive parts) is exempt; otherwise the whole CFC profit enters the base - read via search excerpt of [buxgalter.uz](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text173750_kak_i_dlya_chego_opredelyat_aktivnost_kontroliruemyh_inostrannyh_kompaniy) (page truncated when opened).

**Notices, procedure, penalties**
- [Code] Art. 209: Uzbek tax residents (individuals included) must notify the tax authority of participation in foreign legal entities or foreign unincorporated structures within one month of the participation arising or changing (and of ending it); no repeat notice if grounds are unchanged; the excerpt sets no minimum percentage; the CFC notice is due "no later than 20 March of the year following the tax period" in which the CFC profit share is to be counted under Art. 208; electronic filing, individuals may file on paper; form set by the Cabinet; corrected notices allowed - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000).
- [Code] Art. 209 demand procedure: if the tax authority has information that a person controls a CFC and no notice was filed, it demands explanations (20 calendar days to respond, evidence may be attached); if control is confirmed it issues a controller notice, challengeable in court within three months; no challenge in three months = deemed acceptance - [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000).
- [Code] Art. 22 (taxpayer duties) separately mentions notifying the tax office about holdings above 10% of the charter capital of Uzbek or foreign legal entities - [mirror p2](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/2/120000). Forms are in Annexes 5 (participation in foreign legal entities) and 6 (CFC notices) to Cabinet Resolution No. 489 of 14.08.2020, applicable from 1 Jan 2022 - [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/-4948595).
- [Code] Art. 227: not including a CFC profit share in the base - fine of 20% of the unpaid tax, minimum UZS 10 million (about $847), for legal-entity or individual controlling persons; Art. 224 general underpayment fine is also 20% - [mirror p18](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/18/1800000).
- [Big4] PwC reports a fine for a late notice of 3 BRV for citizens and 10 BRV for officials (about UZS 1.32M / 4.4M at BRV 440,000, about $112 / $373), and that filing the CFC notice is also needed to carry a CFC loss forward - [spot.uz/PwC](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/). The Code article carrying that fine was not found (Art. 220 on late reporting states no amount in the mirror - [mirror p18](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/18/1800000)).
- [Press/Pro] Dividends on shares of individuals were exempt from PIT 1 Apr 2022 - 31 Dec 2024 (expired unless extended) - [yuz.uz](https://yuz.uz/ru/news/kakie-izmeneniya-v-zakonodatelstve-uzbekistana-vstupyat-v-silu-s-1-aprelya-2022-goda) [Excerpt].

**Interaction with residency of the foreign company itself**
- [Code] Art. 33: a foreign entity is an Uzbek tax resident if its place of effective management (POEM) is in Uzbekistan unless a treaty provides otherwise; Art. 34: POEM is Uzbekistan if (1) the executive body regularly runs the entity from Uzbekistan (not "regular" if substantially smaller than activity in another state) or (2) the principal managing officers mainly lead it from Uzbekistan, including day-to-day decisions within the executive body's competence; strategic planning, budgeting, consolidated reporting, internal audit and preparing shareholder/board decisions do not by themselves count; the carve-out (Art. 34(3)) needs both a business run in the state of location with its own qualified staff and assets AND a treaty in force; compliance must be documented (Art. 34(4)); tie-breaker if both states qualify: accounting, document flow or day-to-day personnel management in Uzbekistan = Uzbekistan - [mirror p3](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/3/240000). A CFC must be non-resident (Art. 39), so a POEM-resident foreign entity is taxed as an Uzbek resident entity (general profit tax 15%, Art. 337 para. 12) instead.
- [Code] Art. 36 (permanent establishment, PE): a fixed place of business through which a foreign entity carries on business in Uzbekistan; services including consulting by the entity's own personnel for more than 183 days in a 12-month period on the same or related project; the PE starts at the earliest of contract signing, first employment contract for Uzbek activity, or arrival of a non-resident individual to perform it; registration with the tax authority is required; the text does not say a home office automatically creates a PE - [mirror p3](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/3/240000), [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000). Operating an unregistered foreign PE is fined (Art. 219 area) - [mirror p18](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/18/1800000).

### Inferences
- A solo founder who owns 100% is a controlling person under every limb (Art. 40(1)), so the foreign company is a CFC unless it is POEM-resident or covered by an Art. 204 ground.
- Because of the UZS 300M cliff (the whole profit is counted once the threshold is exceeded - a reading of Art. 208(16), whose wording does not say that only the excess is taxed; the fetch tool flagged this as an interpretation), a first-year company with profit below about $25.4k pays no CFC tax and, on the wording of Art. 209, probably owes no CFC notice - but still owes the one-month participation notice. At the target $5K MRR ($60k/yr, about UZS 709M), retained profit would exceed the threshold unless it is distributed, paid out as fees to an Uzbek contractor, or otherwise reduced.
- Timing works as a planning window: FY N profit is deemed received 31 Dec of N+1, and dividends paid in N+1 (or interim in N) reduce it, so distribution converts a 12% CFC charge into a 5% dividend charge (Art. 381(2) rate; see Q3).
- Art. 204 outcome by structure (inference from the cited text): a UK Ltd paying real UK corporation tax at or above 15% effective could qualify under ground (3) (UK has a treaty) and ground (2); a US entity cannot use ground (3) or (4) (no US treaty found - see Q2/Q3) and can use ground (2) only if its state is not on the offshore list (Delaware and Wyoming are both on the current Annex 1 list published on lex.uz - see Q2/Q7); the bank/insurer and non-profit grounds are irrelevant.
- SaaS and app revenue sold to end users is helped by Art. 44 (end-use software is not a royalty; development services are not royalties), which pushes it toward Art. 206 item 13 (active). The residual risk is Art. 206 item 10 ("information-processing services", R&D, design-engineering): no source says whether a SaaS subscription is "information processing". Mixed revenue (subscriptions plus any licence or consulting income) must stay at or under 20% passive.
- If the founder runs the foreign company alone from Tashkent, Art. 34(1) conditions are met on their face and Art. 34(3) cannot be met (no staff or assets abroad, and no US treaty), so deemed Uzbek residency of the company (15% profit tax on worldwide profit, registration, no CFC treatment) is a live risk in any structure where he directs it from Tashkent. A UK Ltd gets no relief from the treaty either (Art. 4(3) of the UK treaty - see Q2).

### Gaps
- The official lex.uz text of Arts. 204-209 could not be read directly (fetch cap of about 478k characters on the lex.uz page); everything above comes from the prg.kz mirror via a paraphrasing tool. Exact wording, especially the 300M cliff, the structure of Art. 204 (items 1-3 were visible on one chunk, item 4 and Parts 2-6 on another) and Art. 209 thresholds, must be confirmed.
- Whether any notice is due in a year when profit is at or below UZS 300M (Art. 209 is silent; PwC advises filing for loss-making CFCs to preserve losses). Whether Art. 22's ">10% holding" notice and Art. 209's participation notice (no stated percentage) are the same duty.
- The Code article that fixes the 3 BRV / 10 BRV late-notice fine was not found; only PwC states it.
- Whether foreign income tax paid by the CFC reduces CFC profit under the profit rules, and how Art. 331 (legal entities) interacts with Art. 208.
- No Uzbek guidance found on SaaS as "active" under Art. 206. The State Tax Committee article on activity tests was only partly readable (paywall/truncation).
- Items 1-3 of the PwC exemption list are from a 2023 article; the Code text read here matches it except that the 300M figure is a counting threshold in Art. 208, not an Art. 204 ground.

## 2. Treatment of a US LLC disregarded for US tax (and UK Ltd comparison): foreign legal entity? profit taxed to owner or only distributions?

### Takeaway
No Uzbek statute, ruling or practitioner source found addresses US check-the-box or disregarded status. The Code keys classification to the foreign entity's civil-law status (legal entity vs. structure without legal personality), so a US LLC - a separate legal person under state law - is most likely a "foreign legal entity" and therefore a CFC of its resident owner. Owner-level tax then arises on distributions (as dividends, 5%) and on undistributed profit once it exceeds UZS 300M and no exemption applies (12%) - not on all profit every year, and not only on cash distributions. The bigger Uzbek-side issues are POEM/PE exposure, the fact that Delaware and Wyoming are both on the current offshore list (Annex 1 of the FX-monitoring regulation, which the Code's Art. 181 offshore concept appears to use), and the lack of a US-Uzbekistan income tax treaty.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Offshore list, current text: Annex 1 "Перечень оффшорных зон" to the Regulation on monitoring the validity of currency operations (Reg. No. 2467 of 12.06.2013, lex.uz doc 2186012) lists 68 jurisdictions (numbers 101-169, number 152 vacant after Panama was removed) including State of Delaware (120), State of Wyoming (112), Cyprus (127), Hong Kong (118), US Virgin Islands (114), Puerto Rico (154), American Samoa (101), Malta (139), Liechtenstein (134), Monaco (141), Seychelles (157), Mauritius (135) and the UK overseas territories and Crown dependencies (Anguilla, Bermuda, BVI, Cayman, Gibraltar, Guernsey, Jersey, Alderney, Sark, Isle of Man, Turks and Caicos, Montserrat); Nevada, New Mexico, Panama, the USA as a country, the UK, the UAE, Singapore, Estonia and Georgia do not appear - [lex.uz Annex 1 page](https://lex.uz/ru/docs/2186012) (list read through a summarising fetch; the page also carries the regulation body). The latest amendment, Resolution No. 2467-7 (registered 06.12.2025, in force 09.12.2025; signed by the Tax Committee, the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the CBU), "excludes position 52" following a revision of the OECD Global Forum membership list - [lex.uz 2467-7](https://lex.uz/docs/7889232).
- [Code] Art. 181(3)-(4): an offshore jurisdiction is a state or territory with a preferential tax regime and/or no disclosure of financial-transaction information; the list is set by the State Tax Committee jointly with the State Customs Committee and the CBU - [mirror p14](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/14/1400000). Press linked the May 2020 draft of this list to Art. 181 and the CFC rules - [spot.uz, 25 May 2020](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2020/05/25/offshore/). That the Art. 181 list is the same document as Annex 1 above is likely (same names, same 2020 history) but not confirmed by any text read.
- [Code] Art. 35 defines "foreign structures without legal-entity status" as organisational structures formed under foreign law without forming a legal entity (examples: funds, partnerships, trusts, collective investment vehicles); Art. 39 applies the CFC test to both foreign legal entities and such structures; Art. 40(14) extends the structure rules to foreign legal entities that have no capital participation under home law - [mirror p3](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/3/240000), [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000).
- [Code] For structures without legal personality, Art. 208(6) reduces profit by amounts distributed to controllers/beneficiaries, and Art. 40(8)-(12) make founders or economic owners controlling persons - [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000), [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000).
- [Official] Treaties: PwC's Uzbekistan treaty table (reviewed 27 Aug 2026) lists the United Kingdom and does not list the United States - [PwC treaties](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties). US-side search excerpts say the US applies only a legacy 1973 USSR-era treaty to Uzbekistan, and that Uzbekistan-US talks on a new treaty were reported in 2019 - [Excerpt: Orbitax](https://orbitax.com/news/country/article/Tax-Treaty-between-the-US-an-36522), [Excerpt: anhor.uz](https://anhor.uz/news/taxation/).
- [Official] UK-Uzbekistan convention (1993, in force; 2018 protocol per HMRC): Art. 4(3) says a non-individual that is resident of both states is resolved by mutual agreement of the competent authorities and, absent agreement, gets no treaty benefits (except Arts. 22, 25, 26) - [HMRC-hosted convention text](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/729828/1993-uzbekistan-DTC.pdf) (text extracted locally from the PDF).
- [Press/Pro] List history: the State Tax Committee's May 2020 draft listed 68 jurisdictions including Delaware and Wyoming - [Kun.uz, 23 May 2020](https://kun.uz/news/2020/05/23/ozbekistonda-ofshor-yurisdiksiyalarning-royxati-tasdiqlanadi), [spot.uz, 25 May 2020](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2020/05/25/offshore/); the list was reported as 69 zones after 12 jurisdictions were added in Oct 2020 (American Samoa, Aruba, Guatemala, Dominica, Kiribati, Monaco, Alderney, Pitcairn, San Marino, Sint Maarten, Uruguay, Philippines) - [Kursiv, 16 Oct 2020](https://uz.kursiv.media/2020-10-16/v-uzbekistane-rasshirili-spisok-ofshornykh-zon/), [Mondaq](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/financial-services/1073166/the-list-of-offshore-zones-has-been-updated-in-uzbekistan); Panama was removed in Dec 2025 leaving 68 - [Kun.uz, 12 Dec 2025](https://kun.uz/en/news/2025/12/12/uzbekistan-removes-panama-from-offshore-monitoring-list).
- [Code] The list is used in Art. 181 (controlled foreign-trade deals with offshore counterparties) and in Art. 204(2) (denies the "active company" CFC exemption) - [mirror p14](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/14/1400000), [mirror p17](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/17/1700000).
- [Code] Art. 33-34 and Art. 36 (POEM and PE) apply to any foreign legal entity, including a US LLC; the Art. 34(3) carve-out needs a treaty in force - [mirror p3](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/3/240000).

### Inferences
- Disregarded status for US tax is a US classification; the Code does not look to it. The most natural reading is: US LLC = foreign legal entity = CFC when owned more than 25% by an Uzbek resident (and not POEM-resident). Owner-level tax then arises (a) on distributions as dividends (5%, Q3) and (b) on undistributed profit above UZS 300M per year at 12% unless exempt. It is neither "only distributions" nor "all profit annually".
- Even if Uzbek authorities instead read an LLC as a transparent "structure" (Art. 35), Art. 208(6) lets distributions reduce profit, so a single owner who can freely distribute is in a similar position; the exemption analysis (Art. 204/205) would still be needed.
- Delaware and Wyoming are on the current offshore list. If the Art. 181 / Art. 204(2) list is that Annex 1 (likely), a Stripe-Atlas-type Delaware entity (LLC or C-corp) would (i) lose the "active company" CFC exemption, (ii) have no ETR/bank route either (no US treaty), and (iii) make every deal with it a controlled transaction (Q7), plus enhanced bank monitoring (Q4). A state not on the list (for example New Mexico or Nevada, which are absent) would be an Uzbek-side advantage; the accountant must confirm the list used by Arts. 181 and 204.
- The UK Ltd is the cleaner entity on Uzbek CFC mechanics (not on the offshore list; treaty exists; ETR ground available if UK tax is actually paid at 15%+), but it is not cleaner on POEM: Art. 4(3) gives no tie-breaker, so a UK Ltd directed from Tashkent is exposed to dual residence with no automatic relief.
- Alternative "setups common among Uzbek founders" (UAE free zone, Georgia, Estonia, Singapore and similar): only the offshore-list facts above are sourced (UAE, Singapore, Estonia and Georgia-as-a-country are absent from the current Annex 1 list; Cyprus and Hong Kong are on it). Any jurisdiction whose effective rate is below 15% or that has no treaty with Uzbekistan cannot use ground (3) (Art. 204(1)(3), 204(3)); treaty status and tax rates for each candidate were not verified.

### Gaps
- No Uzbek authority on LLC/disregarded-entity classification, nor any written State Tax Committee position on payouts from a US LLC (dividends vs. owner draws vs. business income). Several Russian-language searches for Uzbek practitioner guidance on a US/Delaware LLC owned by a Tashkent resident returned only generic CFC commentary and US Form 5472 pages (US-side, out of scope).
- Whether the list used by Tax Code Arts. 181 and 204(2) is exactly Annex 1 of the FX-monitoring regulation (Art. 181 names the Tax Committee, Customs Committee and CBU; the Dec 2025 amendment was signed by the Tax Committee, the Ministry of Economy and Finance and the CBU). Annex 1 was read through a summarising tool; confirm Delaware (120) and Wyoming (112) on lex.uz directly.
- Whether Uzbek authorities treat the 1973 US-USSR treaty as in force (PwC's list says no).
- Whether a home-office founder creates a PE of a foreign entity under Art. 36 (text silent on homes).

## 3. Rates for residents (foreign dividends, foreign business income), foreign tax credit, declaration duties, reporting of foreign accounts and shareholdings

### Takeaway
A resident individual is taxed on worldwide income at 12% (Art. 381(1)); dividends and interest at 5% (Art. 381(2), text not limited by payer or source); foreign-source income must be declared on the annual aggregate-income return by 1 April and tax paid by 1 June, payable even from a foreign bank account. Foreign tax credit exists only where a tax treaty applies (none with the US; UK yes). No duty for individuals to notify foreign bank accounts was found in the Currency Law or the Tax Code; the foreign-shareholding notice (Art. 209) is the reporting duty that applies.

### Cited Findings
- [Code] Art. 30: individual tax resident = physically in Uzbekistan more than 183 calendar days in any consecutive 12-month period starting or ending in the tax year (plus fallback, centre-of-vital-interests and early-status rules); citizenship alone is not a basis - [mirror p3](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/3/240000). gov.uz states the same 183-day test - [gov.uz](https://gov.uz/en/advice/70/document/1320).
- [Code] Art. 364/368: residents are taxed on total income from sources in Uzbekistan and abroad; non-residents only on Uzbek-source income; foreign-currency income converted at the CBU rate on the receipt date (Art. 366) - [mirror p28](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/28/2800000).
- [Code] Art. 381: residents 12% general rate; dividends and interest 5% - "does not limit this rate by payer or source"; Art. 382 non-residents (Uzbek-source): dividends/interest 10%, international freight 6%, other 12% - [mirror p29](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/29/2900000). PwC and GSL both report 12% general / 5% dividends and interest for residents - [PwC via spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/), [GSL](https://gsl.org/en/taxes/uzbekistan/).
- [Code] Art. 343(2) (corporate recipients): dividends from a non-resident legal entity are taxed at the 5% rate and foreign tax withheld cannot be offset unless a treaty allows - [mirror p26](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/26/2600000).
- [Code] Art. 393: resident income to be declared includes "income received from sources outside the Republic of Uzbekistan" and income from non-agent sources; Art. 397: file the aggregate annual income declaration at the place of permanent residence not later than 1 April of the year after the reporting year - [mirror p29](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/29/2900000). Same 1 April deadline at gov.uz guidance via [advice.uz](https://gov.uz/en/advice/70/document/1336) (page listed, not opened - [Excerpt]).
- [Code] Art. 397/398: tax due on the declaration is payable by 1 June of the following year; residents may pay from a bank account held in a foreign state, in foreign currency, converted at the CBU rate on the payment date; Art. 397 also lets the tax authority assess tax itself if a resident fails to file - [mirror p30](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/30/3000000).
- [Code] Art. 399: PIT paid abroad by a resident is credited against Uzbek tax using the Art. 342 procedure; Art. 342 requires the income to be taxable in Uzbekistan, an international treaty with Uzbekistan, and proof of payment (certificate of the foreign competent authority or other document, translated), and caps the credit at the Uzbek tax due - [mirror p30](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/30/3000000), [mirror p26](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/26/2600000). PwC: relief for individuals only where a double tax treaty exists - [PwC](https://taxsummaries.pwc.com/republic-of-uzbekistan/individual/foreign-tax-relief-and-tax-treaties).
- [Code] Art. 22 (taxpayer duties): no duty to notify the tax authority of opening or closing bank accounts, including abroad, and no individual duty to report foreign accounts or foreign property was found; the one holdings duty is notifying holdings above 10% of the charter capital of Uzbek or foreign legal entities - [mirror p2](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/2/120000).
- [Official] Currency Law (LRU-573 of 22.10.2019) places no notification duty on resident individuals who open accounts abroad (legal entities do have duties - Q4) - [lex.uz Currency Law](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/5515844).
- [Official] Uzbekistan signed the OECD/Council of Europe Multilateral Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters on 15 Jul 2026 (153rd signatory); it enters into force after ratification and deposit; no CRS exchange timeline for Uzbekistan was given - [RegFollower summary of the OECD release](https://regfollower.com/uzbekistan-signs-multilateral-convention-to-tackle-tax-evasion-and-avoidance/). Uzbekistan joined the Global Forum in 2022 - [PwC via spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2023/04/10/taxes/).
- [Code] Foreign persons selling services in electronic form have their own VAT chapter (Chapter 39, Arts. 278-282) - [mirror p1 table of contents](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/1/1) (content not read).

### Inferences
- Foreign dividends: the safest reading is 5% PIT (Art. 381(2) has no source limit; Art. 343(2) applies 5% to non-resident-company dividends for corporate recipients). No foreign credit is available for a US LLC (no treaty; also no US tax at entity level for a non-US-owned disregarded LLC with no US-source ECI - US point for the other researchers). A UK Ltd's UK corporate tax is the company's tax, not the owner's, so Art. 399 does not credit it; the UK benefit is through the CFC ETR ground (Q1), not a credit.
- Foreign business income of an individual who is not an IE (for example fees paid by his foreign company as contractor or salary) falls under the 12% general rate and the annual declaration; if the business is an IE under the 1% turnover tax, income from that business is outside PIT (Art. 365; Q6).
- Practical annual calendar for structure (a): participation notice within one month of formation; by 20 March - CFC notice (only for years when CFC profit is counted); by 1 April - declaration (dividends at 5%, CFC profit at 12%); by 1 June - pay.
- No account-reporting obligation for the founder personally on the evidence found, but data may still flow to the tax authority through bank reporting and, once ratified and implemented, CRS - the timing is unknown.

### Gaps
- Whether 5% applies to a distribution from a US LLC that the US treats as an owner draw (classification of the payout under Art. 375(2) "dividends").
- Whether Art. 381(2) treats foreign-source dividends differently: the mirror shows no restriction but I could not verify the full article.
- Whether CRS reporting to Uzbekistan has started; ratification status of the MAC.
- Whether individuals owe any bank-level declaration for foreign cards/accounts (not in Code or Law as read), and the VAT-on-electronic-services chapter (Arts. 278-282) content (relevant if the foreign company sells to Uzbek end users).
- The PwC-cited dividend exemption for individuals' shares to 31 Dec 2024 is expired; confirm no 2025-26 successor.

## 4. Currency control: foreign accounts, foreign transfers, repatriation, capital outflow

### Takeaway
Under the 2019 Currency Law a resident individual (which includes Uzbek citizens living abroad) may hold accounts abroad without a permit or notification, move money back without restriction, and is under no duty to repatriate or sell foreign-currency income; payments for services are free "current international operations". Resident legal entities need a presidential/Cabinet decision or treaty to hold accounts abroad and must report them. New in 2026: CBU rules on capital outflow (individuals up to $10k per year to fund foreign companies without approval; US companies carved out), with registration in the FERUz system and quarterly reporting, effective 16-17 Sep 2026.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Law "On currency regulation" (LRU-573, 22.10.2019, in force 23.10.2019): Art. 6 residents include Uzbek citizens living abroad, residence-permit holders and Uzbek-registered legal entities and their branches abroad; individual entrepreneurs are not mentioned. Art. 12: resident individuals may open foreign-currency accounts and deposits in Uzbek banks and abroad with no notification duty; resident legal entities may open accounts abroad only under a decision of the President or Cabinet or under a treaty, must notify the tax office and the CBU on opening/closing/changes and file quarterly balance/turnover reports within a month. Art. 10: individuals may hold currency valuables at their discretion; Art. 12: individuals may move currency from foreign accounts to Uzbek accounts without restriction; Art. 26: individuals do not report currency purchases/sales; Art. 15: payments for foreign trade and services are current international operations carried out without restrictions; Art. 9: domestic payments in soums; Art. 19: FX purchases/sales via Uzbek banks; the Law sets no repatriation duty for dividends or profits from abroad - [lex.uz Currency Law](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/5515844) (page reflects the 2019 text; amendments may exist).
- [Official] Art. 11/11¹ (repatriation of foreign-trade assets): residents must ensure repatriation; export proceeds delayed more than 180 days are treated as capital movement; liability applies if not repatriated within 45 days (90 days for small business) after the 180 days: 5% fine up to 360 days, +10% to 545 days, +35% beyond; exemptions include overdue receivables not above 10% of 36-month currency revenue; text does not name individuals or IEs - [lex.uz Currency Law](https://www.lex.uz/uz/docs/5515844). A bill to restructure these fines passed first reading on 29 Sep (year not shown in the excerpt) - [Excerpt: UzDaily](https://www.uzdaily.uz/en/uzbek-mps-propose-halving-fines-for-failure-to-repatriate-assets/).
- [Official] Capital outflow, 2026: CBU Board Resolution No. 151 of 02.06.2026 (registered by the Ministry of Justice as No. 2536-3 on 15.06.2026) amends the Regulation on FX operations related to capital movement (Res. No. 222 of 23.11.2013); in force 16 Sep 2026 per spot.uz ("three months after publication") and 17 Sep 2026 per buxgalter.uz. Individuals: up to $10,000 per calendar year to form or acquire equity in foreign companies without government approval; above that, President/Cabinet approval; companies: up to $200,000 per year for private, $100,000 for state-owned. All capital movements are registered in the FERUz system; residents file quarterly "information on investment activity abroad" via their bank (by the 25th of the month after the quarter per buxgalter.uz; unclear whether individuals must file); late filing suspends further capital operations; supplementary agreements to the bank within 10 days - [spot.uz 15 Jun 2026](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/06/15/investment-outbound/), [buxgalter.uz](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text216259_kak_izmenyatsya_pravila_investirovaniya_za_rubej_dlya_yurlic_i_fizlic), draft stage: [spot.uz 26 May 2026](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/05/26/investments-simplify/).
- [Press/Pro] US carve-out: from 1 Jan 2026 (CBU amendment of 16 Dec 2025 following a presidential decree on the US-Uzbek Business and Investment Council) transfers for charter capital, equity and working capital of US companies are not subject to the $10,000 approval threshold; spot.uz says capital may be sent "within the funds on the account" with no fixed cap; the article adds the funds must be held in accounts with Uzbek banks (ambiguous wording) - [Fergana, Dec 2025](https://en.fergana.agency/news/144083/), [spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/06/15/investment-outbound/).
- [Press/Pro] Offshore-list monitoring: banks must report to the tax authorities foreign-currency transfers above UZS 100 million; listed jurisdictions get "enhanced oversight of transactions" - [Mondaq, Oct 2020](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/financial-services/1073166/the-list-of-offshore-zones-has-been-updated-in-uzbekistan), [Kun.uz, Dec 2025](https://kun.uz/en/news/2025/12/12/uzbekistan-removes-panama-from-offshore-monitoring-list).
- [Excerpt] 2026 AML tightening for bank transfers and card flows (payments of UZS 10.3M or more must carry sender and recipient data; app transfers or card credits above 500 / 1,000 BRV in 30 days flagged) from search excerpts of [Kun.uz, 10 Aug 2026](https://kun.uz/en/news/2026/08/10/govt-tightens-bank-checks-on-transactions-above-uzs-103-million) and [Yep.uz](https://yep.uz/en/2026/05/uzbekistan-tightens-control-bank-transfers-card-transactions/) - pages not opened.

### Inferences
- Holding a foreign-company bank account (Mercury, Wise, etc.) is the company's account, not the founder's; the founder personally has no filing duty for it on the evidence found. An Uzbek legal entity (IT Park LLC) should not hold the foreign company's accounts itself without a presidential/Cabinet decision - keep accounts in the foreign company's name.
- Receiving USD from the foreign company into an Uzbek account is a current operation for services (free) and is a dividend/draw if it is a profit distribution; no repatriation or conversion obligation falls on an individual; large flows trigger bank AML questions, so contracts/invoices should exist.
- Funding the foreign company: the $10k-per-year individual limit applies to non-US companies; a US entity falls under the 2026 carve-out, but FERUz registration and quarterly reporting likely apply; paying incorporation or agent fees looks like a current payment for services rather than capital - to be confirmed.
- Under structure (b), the Uzbek vehicle exporting services to the foreign company is a "resident exporter": the repatriation clock (180 + 45/90 days) applies if payment is late; an IE's position is unclear (Q6).

### Gaps
- Current in-force text of the Currency Law after 2019 amendments, and CBU Resolution 151's text (read via press only).
- Whether individual entrepreneurs fall under the "resident" repatriation duty or are treated as individuals.
- The year of the repatriation-fine bill and whether it is enacted.
- Whether paying a foreign formation agent from a personal card counts as capital movement, and whether an individual shareholder's quarterly report is required.
- AML thresholds are from search excerpts only.

## 5. IT Park residency in 2026: eligibility, SaaS as export, tax benefits and duration, fees, foreign subsidiaries and related-party billing

### Takeaway
IT Park residency is open only to Uzbek-registered legal entities (not IEs) whose activity is on the approved list, which names design/development/sale or licensing of software and export of IT services over the internet; own-product SaaS is not named but fits those entries and the Expert Council can clarify. Benefits: 0% profit tax, 0% social tax and VAT relief to 1 Jan 2028; 7.5% PIT on employee pay; from 2028 to 2040 relief on all taxes except VAT only if export is more than 50% of income. From 1 Apr 2026 residents pay a differentiated contribution of about 1% of aggregate income for small residents (secondary reading). No rule barring foreign subsidiaries or billing related foreign companies was found.

### Cited Findings
- [Official] Eligibility: a legal entity registered in Uzbekistan with an economically justified business plan and an activity on the Cabinet-approved list (Resolution No. 589 of 15.07.2019, as amended); IEs do not appear to qualify; the Expert Council decides whether an activity is covered - [IT Park requirements](http://www.it-park.uz/ru/itpark/residents/requirements); explicit FAQ: "Individual entrepreneurs cannot become residents" (2019 FAQ) - [IT Park FAQ](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/11-frequently-asked-questions-and-answers-when-applying-for-it-park-resident-status).
- [Official] Permitted activity list (23 categories): (1) design, development and sale or licensing of software incl. games for any platform; (2) implementation/support/training; (5) data processing with software; (17) IT education; (18) export of IT services via the internet; (23) organisations with higher export share serving two or more foreign countries; SaaS not named - [IT Park requirements](http://www.it-park.uz/ru/itpark/residents/requirements). Cabinet Resolution No. 303 of 29.05.2024 widened the list to service companies delivering over the internet who export to at least two countries with export share at least 50% - [IT Park membership page](https://www.outsource.gov.uz/fr/media/how-to-become-a-member-of-it-park-uzbekistan).
- [Excerpt] Export proof: a search synthesis (page not opened) says that since 1 Apr 2024 (PP-129 of 15.03.2024) electronic export contracts for digital services sold through foreign online platforms up to $100k are accepted without a paper copy, as a one-year experiment with settlements through a local bank account - [spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2024/03/18/export-documents/). No official definition of whether own-SaaS sales count as "export of IT services" was found.
- [Official] Tax benefits: full exemption from corporate income tax, VAT, social tax and turnover tax through 1 Jan 2028; 7.5% employee income tax (national 12%); 5% cap on dividends to foreign shareholders to 1 Jan 2040; export-oriented companies (more than 50% of revenue from abroad) keep relief through 2040 and from 2028 are exempt from all taxes except VAT; customs-duty exemption on imported technology equipment and components to 2040; foreign IT providers (UP-157, Oct 2024) CIT exemption 2025-2030 if the resident's exports exceed $10M - [IT Park, 7 Jan 2026](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/tax-incentives-for-startups-and-foreign-investors-in-uzbekistan-s-it-sector).
- [Big4] EY on Decree UP-157 (14 Oct 2024): current benefits (all taxes and mandatory contributions to targeted funds incl. social tax; customs duty exemption; PIT benefits for employees) last until 1 Jan 2028; extension 1 Jan 2028 - 1 Jan 2040, excluding VAT, if export income exceeds 50% of total income (or the education-graduate test); 5% dividend rate for non-resident founders to 2040 if exports exceed 50% - [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uz/technical/tax-alerts/2024/10/ey-uz-decree-on-additional-incentives-to-support-it-park-residents).
- [Official] PP-388 of 26 Dec 2025, effective 1 Apr 2026: payment organisations, payment-system operators, marketplaces/e-payment systems and microfinance lose IT Park tax and customs incentives; contributions to the directorate become differentiated by income level and export activity; export requirements to rise in stages - [IT Park EN](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/new-rules-are-being-introduced-for-it-park-uzbekistan-members), [IT Park RU](https://www.it-park.uz/ru/itpark/news/vvodyatsya-novye-pravila-dlya-rezidentov-it-park-uzbekistan) (no rates published there).
- [Press/Pro] buxgalter.uz's reading of the PP-388 table: contribution to the IT Park directorate, based on total annual income: up to UZS 5bn - 1%; UZS 5-100bn - 1% if the export minimum is met, 2% if not (from 1 Apr 2026); UZS 100bn and above - 1% / 2% / 3% (3% from 1 Apr 2026 if not met). Export minimums (Cabinet Resolution No. 303 of 29.05.2024): 10% from 1 Oct 2024, 20% from 1 Apr 2026, 35% from 1 Jan 2027 (a search excerpt of a 2024 IT Park item gives 1 Jan 2026 for the 20% step - unverified). The source table was poorly formatted - [buxgalter.uz PP-388](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text212804_kto_lishaetsya_lgot_spisok_krupnyh_nalogoplatelshchikov_i_drugie_izmeneniya_po_pp-388). At UZS 5bn about $423k, so a small resident at $60k/yr pays about 1% (about $600).
- [Official] Resident duties and attrition: quarterly reports, annual audit report and opinion, mandatory payments on time, activity per the approved business plan; in July 2026 IT Park revoked 152 memberships for missing the 2025 audit report and opinion, and 616 earlier in 2026 for late payments, inactivity over six months, missed quarterly reports or off-plan activity; loss of status means loss of tax and customs benefits - [outsource.gov.uz](https://www.outsource.gov.uz/media/in-july-152-companies-lost-their-it-park-uzbekistan-resident-status), [IT Park membership page](https://www.outsource.gov.uz/fr/media/how-to-become-a-member-of-it-park-uzbekistan).
- [Official] Process: application via my.it-park.uz with a business plan, Expert Council review in about 15 working days - [IT Park guide](http://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/useful-guide-what-support-programs-does-it-park-provide). LLC registration: about UZS 270,000 state fee ($22), no minimum charter capital, 1-2 business days, foreign shareholders allowed - [IT Park foreign-founder guide, 8 Sep 2025](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/how-to-register-a-business-in-uzbekistan-a-complete-guide-for-foreign-founders).
- [Official] A 2020 IT Park note on the Res. 589 amendments says residents are exempt (apart from main-activity income) from taxes on royalties, joint-activity income, exchange-rate differences and dividends - [IT Park, 3 Nov 2020](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/deyatelnost-rezidentov-it-park-usovershenstvovana-1). Nothing found prohibits a resident from owning a foreign subsidiary or invoicing a related foreign company; EY's UP-157 summary and PP-388 coverage are silent on both, and the only related-party reference in PP-388 coverage concerns large-taxpayer status through mutual invoices - [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uz/technical/tax-alerts/2024/10/ey-uz-decree-on-additional-incentives-to-support-it-park-residents), [buxgalter.uz PP-388](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text212804_kto_lishaetsya_lgot_spisok_krupnyh_nalogoplatelshchikov_i_drugie_izmeneniya_po_pp-388).
- [Big4]/[Official] The two opened sources agree that VAT relief ends on 1 Jan 2028 (EY: VAT excluded from the 2028-2040 extension; IT Park Jan 2026: from 2028 exemption from all taxes except VAT) - see citations above. Neither says whether the 7.5% staff PIT continues after 2028 (EY lists "PIT benefits" only among the benefits running to 2028). A search excerpt of a Sep 2026 Jobbers guide says terms are locked through 2028 - [Excerpt: Jobbers](https://www.jobbers.io/?p=152982).

### Inferences
- The IT Park route requires an Uzbek LLC (cheap to form) and does not exist for an IE; for a solo founder at about $60k/yr revenue the contribution (about 1%) is similar to the IE's 1% turnover tax, but the LLC adds annual audit, quarterly reporting and accountant costs, plus 5% dividend tax (Art. 381) or 7.5% PIT on salary, so all-in about 6% on money taken out versus about 1% for the IE.
- IT Park is the scale-up path: 0% profit tax to 2028 regardless of export share, to 2040 if export share is above 50%; it also suits adding a partner (shares in an LLC) and a later sale.
- The foreign Stripe-holding company would be the IT Park resident's customer. The sale of own-SaaS to end users would be booked by the foreign company; the resident's export revenue would be development/licensing fees from the foreign company. Whether fees from a related foreign company satisfy the export-share test and the export-contract documentation is not answered by any source found; the business-plan review (Expert Council) is the place to ask.
- If an IT Park LLC owns the foreign company it becomes the CFC controller: CFC profit would enter its profit-tax base (Art. 297(3)(25) per buxgalter) - within the exemption period this is likely neutral, but the interaction was not confirmed. Outbound investment is also capped for private companies ($200k/year, US carved out) and reportable (Q4).

### Gaps
- Official text of PP-388 (contribution table) and Resolution No. 303; rates are a secondary reading.
- Whether SaaS sold by a related foreign company, or fees invoiced to it, count as "export of services" for the 50% test and the contribution tiers.
- End-date and conditions of the 7.5% staff PIT after 2028; VAT treatment after 2028 (sources conflict).
- Any restriction on subsidiaries or related-party billing in the membership agreement (not public).

## 6. Individual entrepreneur regime from 2026-01-01: 1% turnover tax, FX export revenue, IT Park for IEs

### Takeaway
From 1 Jan 2026 IEs (and self-employed) with revenue from goods and services up to UZS 1bn per year (about $84.7k) pay a 1% turnover tax (Tax Code Art. 467); the fixed PIT for IEs is gone. Presidential Decree UP-100 (26 May 2026) raises the threshold for mandatory transition to VAT/profit tax to 12,000 BRV (about UZS 4.9-5.3bn) from 1 Jun 2026, but it is unclear and unresolved whether that applies to the 1% IE regime; the Code text read still says 1bn. An IE can receive foreign-currency export payments into an Uzbek bank account as individuals can, but cannot be an IT Park resident.

### Cited Findings
- [Code] Art. 461(1): turnover tax may be paid by legal entities with aggregate income up to UZS 1bn, and by IEs and self-employed persons whose goods/services revenue for the period is up to the same limit; Art. 467: general rate 4%, IEs and self-employed 1%, with the fixed-sum options of Art. 467(2)-(4) ceasing on 1 Jan 2026; Art. 462: above the limit an IE moves to VAT and profit tax from the date the threshold is crossed (self-employed above the limit pay as IEs except social tax); IEs may elect VAT/profit tax instead; Art. 465: payment platforms act as tax agents for IE/self-employed income up to UZS 1bn; Arts. 469-470: monthly returns by the 15th, annual by 15 Feb - [mirror p34](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/34/3400000).
- [Code] Art. 365: income of IEs and self-employed persons taxed under turnover tax is outside the PIT object - [mirror p28](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/28/2800000).
- [Press/Pro] Law ZRU-1108 of 25.12.2025: 1% for IEs and self-employed up to UZS 1bn, whether working through digital platforms with individuals or through e-document systems with legal entities; fixed PIT for IEs abolished; self-employed with income up to UZS 100M exempt from turnover tax; newly registered self-employed above the limit move to VAT/profit tax from the next year - [buxgalter.uz, 29 Dec 2025](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text212712_nk-2026_izmeneniya_po_nalogu_s_oborota_dlya_ip_i_samozanyatyh). The 2026-09-26 branch-c evidence cites Acsour for the same 1% from 1 Jan 2026 under PP-247 - [Acsour](https://acsour.kz/news_eng/tpost/t7xlvgjb11-uzbekistan-introduces-1-tax-for-sole-pro) (not re-opened; consistent with Art. 467 above).

### Inferences
- At the $5K MRR target (about UZS 709M/yr) an IE paying fees-only turnover would owe about 1% (about $600/yr) and stay under 1bn; the cap is reached at roughly $7k MRR ($84.7k/yr), after which VAT and profit tax apply unless UP-100's higher limit is confirmed to cover IEs.
- An IE can invoice a foreign company in USD and be paid by wire to an Uzbek account; the repatriation clock (Q4) and bank paperwork (contract, invoice) are the practical constraints.
- IE status cannot take a partner, cannot be sold as a business, and is not IT Park eligible; this is my reading of IE form, not a sourced statement.

## 7. Transfer pricing between a resident and a related foreign company

### Takeaway
Transfer-pricing control applies only to "controlled" transactions. For a resident-to-foreign related-party deal such as development services billed to the founder's own foreign company, the Code's controlled categories are narrow: Art. 180 covers deals between Uzbek tax residents, and Art. 181 covers foreign-trade deals only for five exchange-commodity groups and for any counterparty registered, resident or tax-resident in an offshore jurisdiction (no threshold, no relatedness test). Because Delaware and Wyoming are on the current offshore list (Q2), billing a Delaware or Wyoming LLC is a controlled transaction if Art. 181 uses that list; billing a UK Ltd or a US entity in an unlisted state is not controlled under these categories.

### Cited Findings
- [Code] Art. 37: persons are related if, among other grounds, an individual holds more than 20% of a legal entity directly or indirectly; two entities share a holder of more than 20% in each; an entity and its sole executive body; relatives are aggregated - [mirror p4](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/4/330000). The founder, his foreign company and any Uzbek LLC he owns are related under Art. 37.
- [Code] Art. 176: transfer pricing covers deals between related parties whose prices differ from independent terms; Art. 177: self-adjustment after year-end (companies by the profit-tax return deadline, individuals by the annual income declaration deadline); Art. 178: market-price presumption in controlled deals unless the tax committee proves otherwise or the taxpayer self-adjusted - [mirror p14](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/14/1400000).
- [Code] Art. 180: related-party deals between Uzbek tax residents are controlled if annual totals exceed UZS 5bn, or exceed UZS 500M where one party has a special regime/zone/exemption/reduced rate/benefit and the other does not (or subsoil minerals); interest-free loans between Uzbek residents performed in Uzbekistan are excluded (Art. 180(10) added by ZRU-1108); Art. 181: foreign-trade deals are controlled only for world-exchange goods in five groups, and for any deal with a party registered, resident or tax-resident in an offshore jurisdiction; a court may treat grouped deals as controlled - [mirror p14](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/14/1400000), [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uz/technical/tax-alerts/2026/01/uzbekistan-tax-code-amendments-2026).
- [Code] Art. 182: notify the tax authority of controlled deals of the calendar year no later than the annual financial statements deadline (paper or electronic; individuals identified by name and citizenship) - [mirror p14](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/14/1400000); electronic filing via my3.soliq.uz - [buxgalter.uz](https://buxgalter.uz/publish/doc/text187375_kogda_i_kak_podat_uvedomlenie_o_kontroliruemyh_sdelkah).
- [Code] Art. 226: fine for underpayment resulting from transfer pricing (amount not read); Art. 224 general 20% - [mirror p18](https://prg.kz/m/amp/document/30421027/18/1800000). [Excerpt] Mazars: failing to file the controlled-deals notice carries a fine of 1-3 BRV (Admin Liability Code Art. 174) - [Mazars Uzbekistan](https://www.forvismazars.com/uz/ru/novosti/nashi-publikacii/tax-alerts/tax-alert-uzbekistan).
- [Big4] ZRU-1108 (25 Dec 2025), effective 1 Jan 2026 unless stated: adds a "transfer pricing formation audit" to tax-audit types (Art. 137) - [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_uz/technical/tax-alerts/2026/01/uzbekistan-tax-code-amendments-2026).
- [Press/Pro] A "counterparties in countries with profit tax of 15% or lower" controlled-deal criterion appears in 2026 search results but belongs to Russia (Tax Code Art. 105.14, Law 425-FZ, Minfin letter 28.01.2026 No. 03-00-08/5827); the Uzbek Arts. 180-181 text read has no such ground - [Excerpt: buh.ru](https://buh.ru/news/s-2026-goda-primenyaetsya-novyy-kriteriy-dlya-kontroliruemykh-sdelok.html); the Acsour page that prompted this is ambiguous and should not be cited for Uzbekistan - [Acsour](https://acsour.com/en/news-and-articles/tpost/ku5d5tvat1-since-2026-the-list-of-controlled-transa).

### Inferences
- Billing a US/UK company in a non-listed jurisdiction is not a controlled transaction on the Code text read, so no TP notification duty under these categories (general expense-justification rules, not read, may still apply); if the foreign company is in Delaware or Wyoming (both on the current list), every invoice is a controlled deal (no threshold, no relatedness test): annual notification with the financial statements, market-price support, and Art. 226 exposure.
- A mixed structure (IE or IT Park LLC dealing with each other) is controlled under Art. 180 if annual totals exceed UZS 500M ($42k) between an exempt/special-regime party and a normal one - relevant if the founder uses both an IE and an IT Park LLC.
- In the IE-to-foreign-LLC structure the price is also the lever that controls the foreign company's CFC profit; TP risk is the tax authority re-pricing upward (raising the IE's 1% base slightly) rather than a major tax leak.

### Gaps
- Official list of offshore jurisdictions and its current content; the amount of the Art. 226 fine; whether TP documentation duties apply to individuals and IEs.
- Whether market-price adjustment powers outside "controlled" categories exist (general anti-avoidance); not read.

## 8. Structure comparison for the Tashkent founder and what a local accountant must confirm

### Takeaway
On the Uzbek-side evidence alone, the lowest-friction structure for a solo founder at or below about $85k/yr revenue is (b-IE): the founder as an individual entrepreneur on the 1% turnover tax invoices development/licensing services to the foreign Stripe-holding company, which keeps its retained profit under UZS 300M, with the one-month participation notice and the annual declaration filed. (a) direct ownership is simplest on paper but concentrates the CFC, POEM, PE and (for Delaware/Wyoming) offshore-list risks on the founder. (b-LLC) an IT Park LLC is the scale-up/partner path (0% profit tax, about 1% contribution, 5% on dividends) but costs more in admin and does not beat the IE below the cap. These are inferences from the cited rules, not advice.

### Cited Findings
- [Code] Rates and rules supporting the comparison are cited in Q1-Q7: CFC Art. 203/208 (UZS 300M cliff, 12% PIT, dividends reduce), POEM Art. 34, PE Art. 36, dividends 5% Art. 381, declaration 1 April / payment 1 June Arts. 397-398, turnover tax 1% Art. 467.
- [Official] IT Park membership: legal entity only; Expert Council ~15 working days; LLC registration ~UZS 270k, 1-2 days, no minimum capital - [IT Park guide](http://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/useful-guide-what-support-programs-does-it-park-provide), [IT Park foreign-founder guide](https://www.it-park.uz/en/itpark/news/how-to-register-a-business-in-uzbekistan-a-complete-guide-for-foreign-founders).
- [Official] Cabinet Resolution No. 489 Annexes 5 and 6 (notification forms) apply from 1 Jan 2022 - [lex.uz](https://lex.uz/docs/-4948595).
- [Official] CBU outbound-investment rules effective 16-17 Sep 2026 (individual $10k limit; US carve-out; FERUz registration) - [spot.uz](https://www.spot.uz/ru/2026/06/15/investment-outbound/).

### Inferences
Illustration, my arithmetic, assuming $60k/yr revenue (UZS 709M), 10% costs (profit $54k, UZS 638M), all in Uzbek terms only:

| Structure | Uzbek tax on money reaching the founder | Main filings | Main residual risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Founder owns US LLC / UK Ltd directly | If profit distributed as dividends: 5% (about $2.7k). If left in the company and above UZS 300M with no exemption: 12% CFC charge (about $6.5k). Below UZS 300M (about $25.4k): no CFC charge, only 5% on actual distributions | Participation notice within a month; CFC notice by 20 Mar in years when profit is counted; annual declaration 1 Apr, pay 1 Jun | POEM-resident company (15% profit tax), PE, Delaware/Wyoming offshore status, SaaS passive-income test, no US treaty |
| (b-IE) Founder as IE invoicing the foreign company | 1% turnover tax on fees received (about $540 on $54k) if the foreign company keeps profit under UZS 300M; nothing further on the IE's own money | IE registration; monthly/annual turnover returns; participation notice; controlled-deal notice only if counterparty is offshore-listed | UZS 1bn cap (or 12,000 BRV?); related-party pricing; IE social contributions unknown; foreign company still a CFC and may still be POEM-resident |
| (b-LLC) Uzbek IT Park LLC services (and optionally owns) the foreign company | 0% profit tax to 2028, then conditional to 2040; about 1% IT Park contribution (about $540); 5% on dividends to the founder (about $2.6k) or 7.5% PIT on salary | LLC accounts, annual audit report to IT Park, quarterly reports, Art. 182 notice if offshore counterparty | Overhead; export-share test after 2028; benefit status of related-party fees unconfirmed; if LLC owns the foreign company: CFC at entity level and outbound-investment reporting |
| (c) Other jurisdictions (e.g., UAE, Georgia, Estonia, Singapore, Cyprus, Hong Kong) | Same Uzbek CFC mechanics; the ETR ground needs a treaty with Uzbekistan and an ETR of 15% or more (a low-tax jurisdiction fails it); the active ground needs a non-listed jurisdiction (Cyprus and Hong Kong are on the current Annex 1 list; the UAE, Singapore, Estonia and Georgia-as-a-country are not) | As in (a) | Treaty status of each unverified; Stripe eligibility is another researcher's scope |

- The 300M cliff and the dividend-offset window mean the CFC rules cost little if profit is distributed (5%) or paid to an Uzbek vehicle (1%); the dominant risks are the structural ones (POEM, PE, offshore-listed state, classification of SaaS income), not the headline rate.
- Entity choice matters more than domicile of the founder: UK Ltd fits CFC exemptions better; a US entity in a non-listed state can still use the "active" ground; any entity run alone from Tashkent invites POEM treatment.
- Partner later: shares of non-relative partners count towards the Art. 40 thresholds only if Uzbek residents; relatives are aggregated; an LLC accommodates partners and exit more easily than an IE.
