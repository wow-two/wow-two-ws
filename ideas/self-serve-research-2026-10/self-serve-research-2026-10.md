# Self-serve product research and MRR priorities

*Research and decision date: 2026-10-09*

## Decision

The mismatch starts in the **old 100-product catalog's selection rules**. The roadmap inherited that
mismatch when its next recommendations favored migration, acceptance-testing and service operations.
Reordering another five of those business products would not answer the user's clarified preference.

I rebuilt the analysis around 100 self-serve candidate concepts. **Kids Curiosity is user-selected.**
The four additional research priorities are **Formula Drill Gym, Fiction Scene and Continuity Studio,
Surface-Pattern Studio, and Hifz Revision Companion**. These are comparative product experiments,
not five approved simultaneous builds or predictions of commercial success.

The [central roadmap](../../system/planning/pln-tasks.md#venture-product-order) records Kids Curiosity
after the four active products. ForeverPin, Pose Coach, Transcript Forge and Hijinx retain their existing
execution lanes. This report changes the research pool and preparation priorities, not their release order.

## What was wrong with the previous pool

| Audit question | Verified finding | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Was the problem only roadmap ranking? | The old [qualification rule](../saas-research-2026-10/research-protocol.md#qualification) required a persistent record, three connected workflow stages and collaboration between distinct roles. | Consumer learning, entertainment and independently adopted creative products were filtered before ranking. |
| Did that rule affect the actual 100 records? | The saved [old data](../saas-research-2026-10/products.json) contains 100 records; all 100 name at least two roles. The four lanes contain 25 business, creator, developer and operations concepts each. | Selecting a different five from that pool preserves the bias. |
| Was all the old research useless? | Its sources and workflow analysis answer the earlier B2B question. | Preserve it as B2B research; do not present it as the best pool for the new brief. |
| Did the existing roadmap prove the B2B criterion wrong? | Hijinx and Pose Coach were already accepted as consumer experiences; their product-defined behavior is the user's preferred pattern. | The research filter lagged the user's actual product preference. |

This diagnosis concerns fit to the clarified brief. It does not prove every B2B concept fails or that
self-serve products will succeed. The former approach also emphasized concierge workflow demonstrations;
the new approach tests whether users can obtain value and return without founder-led onboarding.

## Coverage and limits

The rebuilt pool contains 25 learning, 25 creative, 25 play/hobby and 25 everyday candidates. Each has a
first result, complete repeat loop, proposed paid boundary, acquisition experiment, dependencies,
content/compute burden, strongest objection and stop rule. Every candidate received a challenge from
someone other than its author. The final data contains **121 primary-source references across 119
distinct URLs**, opened on the research date. All five priorities have two relevant alternatives.

The analysis distinguishes **48 subscription hypotheses**, **47 one-time hypotheses**, and **five pack
models**. That is not a claim that 48 subscription businesses are viable. Final dispositions are five
shortlist, 44 reserve and 51 defer. A low-compute product can still have expensive editorial production,
support or acquisition. None has verified willingness to pay, retention or an underserved market here.

Four assessed concepts are explicitly consolidated for roadmap purposes: household science simulations
into Kids Curiosity (`E007` → `E001`); batch-cooking execution into meal planning (`E087` → `E086`);
conversation/interview rehearsal packs into speaking rehearsal (`E093`/`E094` → `E092`). They are not
four extra companies. A duplicative film taste journal was replaced by art discovery and visual literacy
before the final count. Thus 100 means assessed concepts, not 100 independent build recommendations.

This fresh assessment does not establish completion of the earlier 1,000-to-100 programme. Source
offers establish real alternatives, not competitor revenue, market size, conversion or demand for ours.
Unknown prices remain unknown; no signup, outreach, purchase, publication or product implementation occurred.

## Five research priorities

Order below reflects practical preparation sequence, including the user's selection. Differences under
five score points are one broad tier. The order is not a forecast of which business will win.

| Priority | Product | First complete experience | Continuing paid job | Proposed monthly price |
|---:|---|---|---|---:|
| 1 — selected | [E001 Kids Curiosity](catalog-100.md#e001) | Predict an answer → explore an interaction → explain the result | Connected, reviewed investigations that families return to | USD 6 |
| 2 — research | [E002 Formula Drill Gym](catalog-100.md#e002) | Solve a small spreadsheet task → see the smallest failing example → retry | Repeated weak-skill practice and error review | USD 6 |
| 3 — research | [E040 Fiction Scene and Continuity Studio](catalog-100.md#e040) | Draft a scene → record changed story facts → resume the next scene | Maintain continuity and reliable history across a manuscript | USD 9 |
| 4 — research | [E031 Surface-Pattern Studio](catalog-100.md#e031) | Motif → repeating tile → coordinated colorways → usable export | Repeated collection production with reusable settings and history | USD 9 |
| 5 — research | [E003 Hifz Revision Companion](catalog-100.md#e003) | Mark memorized passages → recall → record difficulty → receive next revision | Maintain memorization and recover a schedule after missed days | USD 4 |

All prices are untested hypotheses. The four research additions are proposed preparation priorities;
only Kids Curiosity has the user's explicit product selection. A product can be technically self-serve
while requiring specialist content authorship. That work must be budgeted rather than hidden inside
the software estimate.

### Kids Curiosity: selected, with an editorial gate

Start with one coherent question cluster, reviewed explanations and a complete predict–explore–explain
experience. Reusable interactions and narration can keep variable processing low. An open-ended child
chatbot is not necessary to test the proposition. The adult controls purchase and the initial family flow.

[Tappity](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/science-for-kids-by-tappity/id1265019371) already describes interactive
science lessons; [Mystery Science](https://mysteryscience.com/) is a relevant adjacent curriculum alternative.
The opportunity is an experience hypothesis, not an empty category. The strongest objection is that quality
explanations, demonstrations and follow-up questions may cost more to produce than families will pay.

**Decisive test:** a child completes an investigation without the founder teaching, explains an unseen
example, and the family starts another session on a later day. Offer the parent a genuinely usable paid
continuation once content and billing are ready. Stop expansion if children only tap through, parents
must repair explanations, or returning families consistently reject the paid boundary. Secure qualified
content review before treating software completion as product readiness.

### Formula Drill Gym: the clearest inexpensive experiment

Use synthetic datasets and one supported formula dialect. Deterministic evaluation can show a concrete
failing row and later revisit that error. There is no need to import company spreadsheets, connect a
customer's stack or infer private business rules. Formula semantics, equivalent correct answers and hints
still require careful review.

[Excel Exercises](https://excelexercises.com/premium.html) sells practice, while
[Excel Practice Online](https://excel-practice-online.com/) supplies free browser exercises. The commercial
risk is graduation: learners may solve a short-term skill gap and leave.

**Decisive test:** observe independent diagnostic completion, a later voluntary return for targeted practice,
and a paid choice between continuing practice and a finite course. If the course wins, retain the product
only as honest one-time revenue; do not count it toward the subscription target.

### Fiction Scene and Continuity: users supply the content

Start with a new story or a small owned excerpt, not a whole-manuscript migration. Link characters, places
and timeline facts to scenes; expose only what helps the writer continue. Deterministic search and explicit
links keep initial processing bounded. Do not promise automatic detection of every plot contradiction.

[Novlr](https://www.novlr.org/) already combines drafting, planning notes and paid history.
[Plottr](https://plottr.com/pricing/) sells story planning and a series bible, including monthly Pro access
and one-time options. A smaller interface is not automatically a reason to switch.

**Decisive test:** writers resume an actual unfinished scene more effectively than in their existing tool,
return to write another scene, and pay for continued organization/history. Stop if they only use free
prompts, immediately export, or cannot identify a repeated improvement over existing tools.

### Surface-Pattern Studio: repeated production, not novelty generation

Focus on people repeatedly producing coordinated collections from their own motifs. Local tiling,
colorway editing, seam inspection and scale-aware export make a visibly complete experience without
inventory, fulfillment or custom client integration. Customer-provided artwork avoids an endless
editorial catalog, although rendering, export correctness and rights-aware sample content still need work.

[Repper](https://repper.app/en/pricing/) already offers collections, previews and commercial exports through
membership. [Patterninja](https://patterninja.com/) supplies another pattern-making alternative. The core
competitor gap is unproven; attractive output alone is insufficient.

**Decisive test:** a regular pattern maker completes a collection unaided, returns with different motifs,
and identifies a repeat task made meaningfully easier than current tools before paying. Stop if the use
is occasional amusement or the proposed advantage is already adequately covered by Repper.

### Hifz Revision: strongest natural recurrence, specialist prerequisite

Keep the initial product self-assessed: a trusted passage map, a flexible revision queue, mistakes recorded
by the user and recovery after missed days. This avoids mandatory audio inference and teacher administration.
Accurate text, permitted assets, respectful presentation and review by experienced practitioners remain
essential. Access to those reviewers and prospective users has not been established.

[Tarteel Premium](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414387-what-s-tarteel-premium) already includes
planning, mistake history and recitation feedback. [Quranly](https://www.quranly.app/) is an adjacent reading
habit alternative. A cheaper manual subset may be insufficient even when revision happens every day.

**Decisive test:** existing memorizers independently use the queue through normal and missed days, resume
without manual plan repair, and pay without requiring automatic recitation checking. Stop if a notebook or
existing app serves them equally well, or trusted reviewers cannot support the chosen revision method.

## Why the nearest alternatives did not enter the five

| Alternative | Why it remains behind the five | Evidence that would reopen it |
|---|---|---|
| Guided Drawing | Strong visible experience, but credible demonstrations and progression create continuing expert-content work. A finite course is currently as plausible as MRR. | Secure a capable author and observe paid continuation into another practice path. |
| Next Set | [Liftosaur](https://www.liftosaur.com/) already exposes progression rules and automatic adjustments. Natural workout recurrence does not establish a reason to switch. | A specific repeatedly better training decision using the same inputs. |
| Arcade | Current cosmetic-only Stars purchases are one-time transactions; changing this to subscription would change the existing product policy. | Actual repeat play and cosmetic purchases; a separately decided recurring-value proposition for MRR. |
| Speedcubing / cryptic academy | Immediate guided practice fits the brief; free tools and direct educational competitors weaken paid differentiation. | Paid drill/course continuation after independent improvement on unseen tasks. |
| Speaking rehearsal | The retry loop is strong, but trustworthy feedback, audio costs and event-driven churn are unresolved. | Comparative feedback quality and repeat paid sessions under a usage cap. |
| Meal planning / houseplant care | Frequent real-world needs, but ongoing specialist content and environment-specific advice can reintroduce support burdens. | Repeated self-serve use that changes decisions without individualized advice. |

## Ranking sensitivity: no robust winner

| Candidate | Base score / all-candidate rank | Recurrence-heavy rank | Discovery-heavy rank |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Kids Curiosity | 71 / 19 | 17 | 24 |
| Formula Drill Gym | 75 / 4 | 18 | 2 |
| Fiction Scene and Continuity | 74 / 9 | 5 | 22 |
| Surface-Pattern Studio | 75 / 5 | 19 | 3 |
| Hifz Revision | 74 / 7 | 1 | 41 |

Kids Curiosity stays selected because the user selected it, not because scoring made it first. Hifz rises
when natural repeat use dominates; Formula Gym and pattern creation rise when immediate visible value
dominates. This instability is a reason for bounded comparative experiments, not more precise scoring.

The highest raw-ranked concept is a detective casebook sold in packs; it is not an MRR leader. A focus
timer scores well on ease but remains deferred because payment and differentiation are weak. These
examples show why aggregate ranking cannot substitute for the commercial judgment in the five-product list.

## Revenue and execution boundary

At the proposed monthly prices, USD 5,000 MRR from one product alone would require 834 active full-price
accounts at USD 6, 556 at USD 9, or 1,250 at USD 4. These are arithmetic illustrations, not acquisition
forecasts. The earlier target is portfolio revenue across one to three products, not a demand to build
all five. Churn, discounts, fees, taxes, refunds, editorial labor and support reduce the practical result.
Annual subscriptions normalize by twelve; one-time purchases, packs and lifetime fees contribute zero MRR.

The USD 20–50 monthly pre-revenue cash envelope remains a pilot target, not a validated operating budget.
Prepare a measured usage budget for hosting, storage and any inference; separately estimate content,
support and acquisition effort. Shared billing infrastructure still needs real provider acceptance before
an actual purchase experiment. A local button or simulated payment is not paid demand.

Progress Kids Curiosity's definition first. Formula Gym is the smallest useful comparative experiment
among the four additions; keep the others as ordered research options rather than creating new parallel
implementation lanes. Public demos, participant recruitment and payment tests described here are future
experiments; this turn did not execute them.
