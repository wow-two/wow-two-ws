## E001 — Kids Curiosity

**Origin:** Existing idea P140; explicitly selected by the user, independently of ranking  |  **Disposition:** shortlist  |  **Score:** 71/100 (rank 19)

**User and first value.** A parent pays for a child aged roughly 6–10; they explore together initially. The child predicts an answer to a why-question, manipulates a simple demonstration, and explains the result.

**Experience.** Choose a reviewed why-question → Predict and explore one interactive explanation → Check the prediction and explain what changed → Return to a saved follow-up question or next short investigation. New child questions and a family discovery ritual; sustained return remains a hypothesis.

**Paid proposition.** An expanding, reviewed library of connected investigations and remembered interests, with useful follow-up questions. **Price test:** USD 6/month; hypothesis. Free: Three complete investigations without an account.. Paid: Full reviewed library, saved paths and new investigation releases..

**First complete scope.** Question shelf covering a single everyday-science cluster; Predict–explore–explain interaction with narrated text; Saved follow-ups and a parent recap.

**Dependencies and operating burden.** Primary-age science educator review; Illustration/audio rights and factual source review; Parent-controlled account and age-appropriate data handling. Curated explanations and local interactive diagrams; no live generative chat. Pilot library capped at 20 investigations and no per-session model calls. High: explanations, misconceptions, narration and usable interactions require review. Start one coherent question cluster; do not promise encyclopedic coverage.

**Acquisition experiment.** Test a publicly shareable one-question demonstration with parent audiences; access and acquisition cost are unverified.

**Observed alternatives** (opened 2026-10-09):

- [Mystery Science](https://mysteryscience.com/) (adjacent): Elementary science curriculum combines question-led lessons, investigations and activities; the site offers a free trial. Price: Paid price not verified..
- [Science for Kids by Tappity](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/science-for-kids-by-tappity/id1265019371) (direct): Developer listing describes interactive, narrated science lessons, daily challenges, offline use and parent discussion prompts. Price: US App Store lists multiple subscription SKUs; a single currently offered checkout price was not verified..

**Proposed distinction.** Test a tightly connected predict–explore–explain path around a child's question, with a visible end. Tappity already serves curiosity; this is a UX hypothesis, not an empty market.

**Argument against building.** Incumbents already supply interactive answers and recurring lessons. Editorial production can dominate software costs, while a generic chatbot is a free substitute.

**Test and stop rule.** Give parents one complete unaided investigation; observe whether the child explains it and chooses another later without researcher prompting; then offer a clearly priced reviewed collection. Pause expansion if children only consume narration, parents must teach the interface, or returning families decline paid access in favor of existing resources.

**Portfolio boundary.** Other STEM entries teach specific subject skills; this serves family why-questions. Do not split topic packs into separate products. Excludes Pose Coach and Hijinx.

**Challenge.** The interactive child experience already exists in Tappity. Mystery Science is adjacent classroom-oriented evidence, not a like-for-like consumer subscription. Preserve the user selection separately from the score. The first test must show a child explaining a new example, parent acceptance, and a later voluntary session; completion taps alone do not demonstrate learning or repeat value. Root classifies Mystery Science as adjacent school-oriented evidence; Kids Curiosity remains selected by the user independently of rank.

**Evidence confidence.** low; competitor evidence only, user selection is not demand validation

## E002 — Formula Drill Gym

**Origin:** Existing idea P037, narrowed to independent browser practice  |  **Disposition:** shortlist  |  **Score:** 75/100 (rank 4)

**User and first value.** An individual spreadsheet user pays to become reliable with formulas without connecting a workplace workbook. Solve a small spreadsheet task and see why a formula succeeds or fails on unseen rows.

**Experience.** Choose a concrete spreadsheet outcome → Write a formula in a small browser grid → Inspect result differences and targeted hints → Revisit a weak skill through a new dataset. Recurring spreadsheet use and spaced practice; upskilling may instead be a finite course purchase.

**Paid proposition.** Diagnostic exercises, varied test data, error explanations and a maintained practice path beyond reference documentation. **Price test:** USD 6/month; hypothesis. Free: A complete beginner diagnostic and its correction exercises.. Paid: Full practice paths, error history and fresh mixed-skill challenges..

**First complete scope.** Browser grid with a small supported formula set; Hidden-case grading with readable counterexamples; Weak-skill revisit queue.

**Dependencies and operating burden.** Licensed or compatible spreadsheet formula engine; Spreadsheet educator review and cross-engine semantics checks. Local formula evaluation on synthetic sheets capped at 200 cells per exercise; no uploaded workbooks or AI calls. Moderate: author realistic datasets, accepted equivalent answers, edge cases and hints. Choose one formula dialect first.

**Acquisition experiment.** Publish an ungated formula challenge with its error explanation; measure qualified practice starts from spreadsheet tutorial audiences. No audience access established.

**Observed alternatives** (opened 2026-10-09):

- [Excel Exercises](https://excelexercises.com/premium.html) (direct): Paid practice includes interactive tutorials, exercises, assessments and downloadable data. Price: Page displays $19.99/month, $59.99 billed yearly, and $199 lifetime; dollar currency code is not shown..
- [Excel Practice Online](https://excel-practice-online.com/) (direct): Provides free browser-based Excel practice across beginner and advanced topics. Price: Free practice is advertised; no paid price verified..

**Proposed distinction.** Test feedback showing the smallest failing data example and resurfacing that error later. Interactive spreadsheet practice is already available commercially and free.

**Argument against building.** A one-time learning burst may not support MRR; mature free exercises and reference assistants already answer common formula questions.

**Test and stop rule.** Offer a complete formula diagnostic without setup; watch a later unprompted mixed exercise; compare actual purchase of monthly practice with a clearly bounded course offer. Reject subscription if learners finish their immediate gap and do not return, or if they prefer free practice after trying the distinctive feedback.

**Portfolio boundary.** E024 teaches database querying; E016 teaches interpretation. No workplace import, team dashboard or custom workbook service.

**Challenge.** Two opened direct alternatives cover both paid and free practice. Failure-case feedback is a testable experience distinction, not an established gap. Recurrence 3 correctly reflects graduation risk. Keep a subscription hypothesis only while repeated error-based practice produces later visits; otherwise sell a finite course rather than inflate MRR. Accepted the independent review; no additional root correction.

**Evidence confidence.** low; competitor evidence only

## E003 — Hifz Revision Companion

**Origin:** Existing idea P057; remove teacher-management workflow from initial scope  |  **Disposition:** shortlist  |  **Score:** 74/100 (rank 7)

**User and first value.** An independent Quran memorizer pays for sustainable revision of passages already learned. Mark familiar passages and complete a short recall session that creates tomorrow's revision queue.

**Experience.** Choose memorized portions and available revision time → Recall from concealed text and mark hesitation → Compare against the licensed text and record self-assessed difficulty → Return when specific passages are due. Maintaining memorized material requires continuing revision; adoption of this particular scheduler is unverified.

**Paid proposition.** A flexible revision schedule, mistake-location history and recovery after missed days without manually rebuilding a plan. **Price test:** USD 4/month; hypothesis. Free: One memorized section with a working revision loop.. Paid: Larger revision library, flexible scheduling and history across devices..

**First complete scope.** Memorized-portion setup; Concealed-text recall and hesitation markers; Adaptive self-rated review queue with missed-day recovery.

**Dependencies and operating burden.** Verified Quran text and permitted recitation assets if included; Hifz practitioner review of scheduling and presentation; Access to independent memorizers for validation is not established. Local scheduling and text conceal/reveal; no automated recitation recognition in the pilot. Cap optional downloaded audio to selected sections. Moderate specialist burden: accurate text mapping, respectful presentation, revision methods and accessibility; no generated religious interpretation.

**Acquisition experiment.** Demonstrate recovery from an overloaded revision queue to independent memorizer communities; distribution permission and access are unverified.

**Observed alternatives** (opened 2026-10-09):

- [Tarteel Premium](https://support.tarteel.ai/en/articles/12414387-what-s-tarteel-premium) (direct): Premium includes memorization mistake detection, planning, historical mistakes and peeking; monthly and yearly renewals are described. Price: Premium amount not verified..
- [Quranly](https://www.quranly.app/) (adjacent): Provides reading goals, progress tracking, daily streaks and optional friend/family participation. Price: Site offers free starting access; paid plan price not verified..

**Proposed distinction.** Test a quiet self-assessed revision queue designed around missed-day recovery. Tarteel already combines planning and mistake history; superiority is unproven.

**Argument against building.** Tarteel offers more capable recitation feedback, and a notebook or free scheduler may be sufficient. Religious expertise and trusted distribution cannot be replaced by code.

**Test and stop rule.** Let existing memorizers configure real known passages unaided, use the due queue through a missed day, then choose whether to buy ongoing scheduling at the stated price. Stop if learners cannot trust self-assessment, require speech correction to find value, or return to their existing revision routine despite successful onboarding.

**Portfolio boundary.** E022 serves arbitrary study notes; this handles passage continuity and memorization maintenance. Not a mosque/teacher administration product.

**Challenge.** A self-assessed passage queue needs no customer-system integration or teacher administration, so adoption independence is high. Accurate text, specialist scheduling and trusted testing constrain feasibility. Tarteel already covers planning and mistake history; no speech-detection superiority should be implied. The prospective repeat occasion is strong but paid preference remains unknown. Accepted independence/feasibility corrections. Quranly is adjacent reading-habit evidence, not a like-for-like memorization error tool; Tarteel is direct competition.

**Evidence confidence.** low; competitor evidence only
