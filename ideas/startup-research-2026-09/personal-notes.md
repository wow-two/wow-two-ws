# Personal, collector and research software

Research date: 2026-09-28. The inventory contains 250 hypotheses, W0751–W1000. Forty received live competitor research; 210 received an analytical screen. None received customer validation. The source register lives in `personal.json`.

## What the market evidence changes

The opportunity is not “find an unoccupied collectible.” Multi-category tools already sell collection management, and many specialist tools are free. Libib offers a free personal tier and paid lending; iCollect sells a multi-category subscription; fountain pen, coffee and tea software establish substantial free substitutes. A narrow workflow must beat the existing spreadsheet or specialist app on a job people repeat.

The better collector hypotheses solve reconstruction or collaboration problems: reproduce a miniature paint recipe, remember the successful alteration to a sewing pattern, maintain condition history, or resume a long board-game campaign. Their proposed $3–6 monthly prices make distribution more demanding than a $39–59 business workflow. They belong in bounded experiments, not the initial $5K MRR forecast.

Research workflows provide a more plausible paid boundary: trace a manuscript claim to supplied sources, compare disagreements in evidence extraction, or reconcile qualitative codebooks. Paid Rayyan, Covidence, Paperpile and Readwise offers support adjacent budget categories; they do not prove demand for these add-ons. Zotero, Anki, Taguette and ordinary documents remain strong free substitutes.

## Dependency and scope constraints

- Start with owner-supplied records. Do not require a comprehensive identification or valuation database.
- Numista's published paid API floor is €100/month plus €100 activation, excluding VAT; that fails the initial $20–50 product budget unless the limited free allowance suffices. It is not proof that coin data can be reused under every proposed use case.
- Keep ownership inventories private by default. An expensive-object catalogue can reveal a user's possessions and location.
- Avoid insurance, provenance authenticity and appraisal guarantees. An evidence organizer cannot certify an object's value or origin.
- Keep scientific tools advisory and source-linked. Citation presence is not evidence quality, and an absent retraction flag is not a validity certificate.
- Use deterministic comparisons before paid AI. Meter optional AI and cap documents; do not promise unlimited inference under a lifetime license.
- Global distribution requires units, date formats, fonts, language support and eligible payments. English-first is an entry point, not worldwide readiness.

## Monetization conclusions

Use freemium only where the free artifact can attract qualified users and the paid boundary is recurring: sync, shared review, history or bounded automation. Local bibliography merge and static archive export tools fit a one-time purchase; their receipts contribute no MRR. Consumer annual billing may reduce transaction overhead but must be divided by twelve when reporting MRR. No paid plan here establishes actual market size, retention or willingness to pay for the proposed wedge.

## Screening limitations

The 250 rows represent different jobs, buyers or outcomes, with many correlated variants. They are not 250 independent market opportunities. The 40 researched leads were selected during generation, not produced by a controlled random sample. Later rows can score higher than a researched lead yet remain below it in evidence confidence. Central selection must retain this distinction and family overlap.
