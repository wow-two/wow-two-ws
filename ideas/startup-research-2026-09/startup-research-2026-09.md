# WoW2: 1,000 software hypotheses → 100 experiments

Research date: 2026-09-28. Decision: pursue a small number of paid workflow experiments before financing a broad product portfolio. The initial operating budget is $20–50 monthly per product; approximately $300 becomes an option when early revenue can support it.

The completed scope is **1,000 analyst-screened ideas, 160 desk-researched leads, and 100 retained experiments**. The 840 other rows do not have individual live market verification. No customer interviews, payment tests, acquisition campaigns or retention observations were performed. None is customer-validated, and no revenue channel is yet empirically validated. The source register verifies commercial offers and features, not competitor revenue or market size.

The lane register contains 164 source records covering 162 distinct URLs. The 1,000 hypotheses span 633 conservative family labels, so they are not 1,000 independent markets. The final 100 span 34 business, 28 creator, 21 developer and 17 personal/research candidates. Their proposed models are 57 bounded freemium, 36 subscriptions, four alternative subscription/one-time offers, and three lifetime local tools. These counts describe the selected hypotheses, not empirical portfolio probabilities.

This is a research-backed selection funnel, not evidence that 1,000 attractive markets exist. The inventory includes explicit rejects, correlated variants and low-confidence hypotheses. A source-backed competitor does not prove our proposed difference is valuable.

## Recommendation: test three routes to recurring revenue

Run at most three small validation experiments concurrently. Start one production build only when its paid-use gate passes. These are the recommended first tests based on low-cost implementation boundaries and identifiable repeat buyers; they are not guaranteed winners or simply the three highest spreadsheet scores.

| Experiment | Initial buyer and repeated job | Proposed price | Narrow paid-value hypothesis | Main substitute and stop condition |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| **W0252 Retainer allowance evidence** | Small agencies reconciling prepaid hours or fixed allowances across recurring client work | $39/month/company | Import existing time records and produce a client-readable allowance/overage evidence packet without replacing time tracking | Bonsai/Harvest/spreadsheets. Compare two cycles at five agencies; stop if four accept current reports as sufficient or fewer than three accept a paid pilot. |
| **W0511 Caption delivery quality gate** | Small caption/localization studios delivering files to clients every week | $39/month/team | Import existing QA results and bind client exception acceptance to the exact delivered version, independent of the editor | Free Subtitle Edit plus Drive and CaptionHub. Require 30% less coordination time, three paid pilots and three second-month renewals; otherwise stop or narrow. |
| **W0012 Executable documentation checks** | Small SDK/API vendors maintaining runnable examples across releases | $39/month/repository bundle, scope to be tested | Find stale tagged examples and route a reproducible failure to the responsible documentation owner | Free doctest/CI plus GitBook/Mintlify workflows. Stop if setup exceeds one hour for most pilots or existing tests already cover the pain. |

The prices are proposals. Do not charge for a feature already available free without demonstrating a workflow improvement. Exact-feature checks downgraded earlier CSV-import, localization-placeholder, domain-monitoring, scope-approval and billing hypotheses. They remain visible where useful, but generic versions should not lead a build wave.

Each first test has a different acquisition hypothesis:

1. **Retainers:** an annotated allowance-reconciliation worksheet, followed by opt-in walkthroughs with agency owners already handling prepaid hours. Compare current reports and billing questions, then test an imported client evidence packet. Public problem reports exist, but they do not establish willingness to pay for this product.
2. **Captions:** a local sample-file checker and a before/after handoff report, demonstrated to studios already reviewing repeated deliveries. Bring evidence of time saved; avoid broad consumer video-editor positioning.
3. **Documentation:** an open example repository showing one broken snippet caught during an upgrade, followed by opt-in trials with SDK maintainers. Public maintenance activity identifies a possible buyer; it is not permission for automated outreach.

These channels are reachable in principle but unproven for the founder. No mailing list size, partnership, community access, traffic volume or conversion rate is assumed. A channel graduates only after it produces paying accounts and a repeated second batch, not after positive comments.

The [qualitative evidence check](qualitative-evidence.md) found self-reported retainer tracking/underbilling problems and historical documentation-maintenance problems. Strong first-person evidence for the exact renewal-notice and caption-signoff wedges remains missing. Renewal notice windows remain a low-cost reserve experiment, not the first business recommendation.

## How the 100 were distilled

Four lanes contributed 250 candidates each: developer/data tools, business workflows, creator/digital-commerce tools, and personal/collector/research tools. Every row has a buyer, job, proposed distinction, model, price hypothesis, acquisition hypothesis, failure mode and eight dimension ratings.

The actual work was concurrent: promising leads were researched while each lane completed its wider inventory. The 300-row comparison cohort contains 40 researched plus 35 other high-scoring candidates per lane. The 160 researched leads were compared centrally. A further falsification pass checked exact substitutes for several early favorites. This was not a controlled sequence of 1,000 independent market studies.

The score weights are pain 20%, willingness-to-pay evidence 15%, reach 20%, recurrence 15%, differentiation 10%, build simplicity 10%, margin potential 5%, and portability 5%. Each dimension is an analyst ordinal rating from one to five. A score of 75 does not imply a 75% success probability. Small differences are not meaningful.

The central view retains operational comments without applying a second numeric risk deduction: several risks already affect the lane scores, so deducting them again would double-count the same concern. A separate evidence adjustment subtracts two points for adjacent-category evidence and eight for individually unresearched rows. This is a confidence-sensitive ordering convention, not a measured economic loss. The script retains raw dimensions and explanations so assumptions can be challenged. The final set uses one representative per conservative family, with identified cross-lane overlaps consolidated; semantic overlap can still remain.

**Ninety positions follow adjusted priority; ten are explicitly exploratory.** The exploration reserve preserves requested collector/personal and one-time purchase options that would otherwise lose to business workflows under the MRR objective. Their lower priority is visible. Selection means “worth an experiment,” not “build approved,” and a lane's reserve decision remains visible in the data. None of the 100 should be treated as independent odds in a portfolio success calculation.

One explicit family preference retains the narrowed W0511 caption client-signoff test over W0512 subtitle revision propagation. This overrides the sibling's higher score to keep the experiment small and use the deeper competitor falsification. W0511 remains a reserve hypothesis; its position among the first three comparisons is not a build recommendation.

Rejected and reserve rows remain in the catalogue. Common reasons: existing free or bundled features, occasional use, unproven distribution, data licensing, expensive integrations, uncapped AI/storage, sensitive-data obligations, physical operations, marketplace cold starts and country-specific rules.

## Pricing and a $5K MRR path

MRR is recurring contracted revenue normalized to a month, before fees, hosting, support, refunds and tax. Annual subscriptions contribute annual recurring price divided by twelve. Lifetime purchases contribute **zero MRR**, even if cash receipts repeat through new sales. Free products contribute no revenue unless a measurable path to a paid product is established.

| Realized recurring revenue per account/month | Paying accounts needed for at least $5K gross MRR |
|---:|---:|
| $5 | 1,000 |
| $9 | 556 |
| $19 | 264 |
| $29 | 173 |
| $39 | 129 |
| $49 | 103 |
| $79 | 64 |
| $99 | 51 |

“Realized” means after discounts but before the operating costs modeled below. The proposed headline price is not necessarily realized ARPU. For example, a $49 monthly plan sold annually for ten monthly payments realizes about $40.83 monthly, requiring 123 active annual customers rather than 103.

| Illustrative portfolio | Exact gross MRR | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| 103 accounts × $49 | $5,047 | One concentrated winner |
| 61 × $49 + 70 × $29 | $5,019 | Two products; 131 total paying accounts |
| 61 × $29 + 42 × $39 + 41 × $39 | $5,006 | Three products; 144 total paying accounts |

These are arithmetic scenarios, not forecasts. They contain no assumed market share, launch date or success probability. A higher account price reduces the customer count but usually requires stronger value, trust and support.

### Acquisition and retention sensitivity

At $39 realized monthly revenue, 129 customers reach the target. At an **assumed** 2% qualified-visitor-to-paid conversion, acquiring 129 requires 6,450 qualified visitors before churn. At 0.5%, the same arithmetic is 25,800; at 5%, it is 2,580. Raw impressions are not qualified visitors. Neither conversion rate has been measured here.

For founder-led selling, an **assumed** 20% qualified-demo-to-paid rate requires 50 completed qualified demos to acquire ten customers. A 10% rate requires 100. Track founder minutes per customer as well as cash advertising spend.

At 129 active accounts, **assumed monthly logo churn** of 3%, 5% or 8% means replacing approximately 4, 7 or 11 customers every month merely to maintain account count. Three-month survival of an initial cohort is approximately 91%, 86% or 78% respectively under a constant independent churn assumption. These sensitivities are not estimates for any candidate.

Do not infer reliable lifetime value from an early churn percentage. Measure first payment, second payment, repeat use of the core job, support minutes, refunds and the acquisition source for each cohort. A full annual prepayment can mask poor retention; it does not establish monthly habit.

### Operating contribution example

For 103 monthly customers at $49, gross MRR is $5,047. Using **illustrative—not vendor-quoted—fees of 6% plus $0.50 per monthly transaction**, processing costs $354.32. Assuming $2 variable delivery cost per customer and $50 fixed monthly product infrastructure leaves **$4,436.68 before founder labor, tax, refunds, chargebacks and other overhead**. With $300 fixed cost, this falls to $4,186.68.

If those customers require 15 support minutes each per month, that is 25.75 hours. Valuing founder time at an illustrative $40/hour deducts another $1,030, leaving $3,406.68 in the $50 infrastructure scenario. Acquisition labor and development remain additional. Annual billing changes transaction-fee timing; adjust the model rather than counting twelve fixed fees on a single charge.

This is why a $5K gross MRR target is different from $5K take-home income. Neither near-zero compute nor a reusable SDK guarantees high operating margin.

## Choosing freemium, subscription or lifetime

| Model | Use when | Paid boundary | Stop or change when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free utility feeding paid workflow | A free artifact reaches the same buyer as a recurring paid job | Exportable sample check → shared review/history | Free users do not activate a paid workflow or consume meaningful support |
| Bounded freemium | Individual use is cheap, collaboration/volume is valuable | Small record/file limit; paid owners, history, recurring checks | Free tier solves the whole job or attracts users unable to pay |
| Subscription | Value recurs through repeated work or maintained monitoring | Active projects, organizations, bounded checks or storage | Job is episodic and customers cancel once complete |
| Lifetime local tool | The useful outcome runs locally with bounded support obligations | Versioned desktop/browser utility, local export | Hosted storage, changing integrations or recurring inference are required |
| Subscription plus usage | AI, media processing or paid data materially varies with use | Included allowance plus prepaid/overage credits | Buyers cannot predict spend or gross contribution becomes negative |

W0006 support fixture scrubber, W0774 bibliography merge preview and W0777 thesis cross-reference preflight are retained as local one-time purchase experiments. Their proposed prices appear in the shortlist. They can produce cash or attract users, but cannot count toward the recurring target. W0787 repeat-trip packing reset and W0758 fragrance blind rating were rejected from the final 100 because recurring value and willingness to pay were weak.

Do not launch a separate free app merely to increase the product count. Begin with a useful free component inside a chosen paid workflow and measure activated paying customers attributable to it. Avoid unlimited AI, unlimited storage and perpetual hosted promises under one payment.

## Budget stages and portfolio capacity

**Before revenue:** $20–50/month per live product is a design constraint, not a measured provider quote. Prefer deterministic parsing, local processing, supplied CSV/files, bounded metadata, shared infrastructure and capped notifications. Include essential email, storage, monitoring, third-party data and minimum platform charges when assessing the cap. Founder labor, development equipment and general business overhead must be tracked separately.

**After recurring contribution supports it:** consider approximately $300/month per product for measured bottlenecks, paid APIs or more reliable infrastructure. A proposed funding gate is two consecutive months of positive contribution with enough contribution to cover at least twice the new recurring commitment. This is an operating recommendation, not a user authorization to spend. A small paid pilot alone does not finance open-ended dependencies.

| Active products | $20 each/month | $50 each/month | $300 each/month |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | $60 | $150 | $900 |
| 10 | $200 | $500 | $3,000 |
| 100 | $2,000 | $5,000 | $30,000 |

At 100 products, the high end of the initial operating budget alone consumes the entire $5K gross MRR target. Even 15 maintenance minutes per product per week becomes 25 hours weekly, excluding feature work and customer acquisition. Shared infrastructure may reduce hosting; it does not eliminate release, security, billing, support and distribution obligations.

Keep **100 experiments in the backlog**, validate three at a time, operate only products that earn continued investment, and expand toward five to ten active products after repeat revenue evidence. This preserves the user's broad option set without assuming every prototype deserves a permanent service.

Reuse common primitives—billing, authentication, file import, deterministic validation, reminders, evidence export and permissions—after the first paid workflows establish requirements. Group compatible workflows into one product where the buyer and acquisition channel are the same. Do not count ten closely related features as ten diversified businesses.

## Global feasibility and payment rails

Software-only does not mean country-independent. Start with an English-language workflow, portable dates/currencies, owner-supplied records and no jurisdiction-specific professional advice. Test language, timezone, payment and data expectations for the initial buyer segment before broad localization.

If the operating entity is in Uzbekistan, payment eligibility needs explicit confirmation: the inspected [Stripe availability page](https://stripe.com/global) does not list Uzbekistan, while [Lemon Squeezy's supported-country documentation](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/supported-countries) lists Uzbekistan for bank payouts. This does not establish merchant approval, product eligibility, usable bank settlement or tax treatment. The user has not specified the selling entity, and no account onboarding or payment test occurred.

Choose a payment route before promising paid access globally. Evaluate actual fee schedules, reserve/refund rules, customer-country restrictions, subscription support and payout currency for that entity. The illustrative fee model above is intentionally not a quote from either provider.

## Paid-validation sequence

The following thresholds are proposed capital-allocation rules, not statistical proof of market size. They are deliberately stricter than positive survey answers. Outreach, billing or publication is a later action; this research has not executed it.

1. **Problem replay:** identify ten qualified buyers per first experiment. Observe a recent real workflow, the current substitute and a concrete consequence of failure. Reject vague enthusiasm without a recurring job.
2. **Concierge prototype:** use permissioned sample files and one end-to-end result. Measure setup time, correction effort and time saved against the current tool. Keep the initial recurring cost within $50.
3. **Paid gate:** seek three paid pilot accounts or signed, price-specific paid commitments. Record the difference: a commitment is weaker than a payment, and neither is retention.
4. **Repeat-use gate:** require at least two pilot customers to repeat the core job in another natural cycle and continue paying. For annual renewal workflows, test recurring portfolio review and decision handling; do not pretend the same contract renews monthly.
5. **Channel gate:** obtain a second batch of paying customers through the same documented acquisition method. Track cash and founder time per acquisition. Until this happens, “validated channel” is premature.
6. **Scale or stop:** invest in integrations and the $300 tier only when contribution and repeated use justify them. Stop, narrow or merge products with weak repeat use or support-heavy economics. Export customer data and honor existing obligations if a launched service is retired.

Keep a simple experiment record: candidate ID, buyer qualification, observed substitute, evidence permission, price shown, result delivered, payment/commitment status, second-cycle use, acquisition source, support minutes, variable cost and decision date. Missing data stays missing.

## What would change this ranking

Existing access to a specific buyer group can outweigh several score points. Three paid caption studios would be stronger evidence than this entire desk ranking. Conversely, one incumbent demonstration that fully solves the proposed job should lower its priority immediately. A paid API minimum, unsupported payment entity, restricted data source or repeated support burden can eliminate an otherwise appealing candidate.

The research does not estimate the chance that five to ten of 100 will generate revenue. Outcomes are correlated through founder attention, distribution, technical dependencies and target customers. Build selection should follow observed paid behavior; product count alone does not improve those constraints.
