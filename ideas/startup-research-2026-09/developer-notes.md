# Developer software opportunity lane

*Research checked: 2026-09-28*

## Result and evidence boundary

- Exactly 250 hypotheses, `W0001`–`W0250`, with explicit buyers, recurring jobs, proposed prices, risks and weighted scores.
- Forty dossiers have current primary competitive evidence, implementation boundaries, acquisition hypotheses and kill tests.
- Forty-five distinct primary URLs were inspected and are referenced in `developer.json`.
- Outcomes: 28 advance to experiments, 180 reserve, 42 reject under current constraints.
- None is customer-validated. Paid offers establish suppliers' commercial terms, not proven demand for our proposed distinction.
- Eighty-eight families identify correlated alternatives. These are 250 candidate propositions, not 250 independent markets.
- All scored prices, channels, costs and commercial judgments are analyst hypotheses. No outreach, interviews or purchase tests occurred.

## Iterations

1. Curated 250 buyer/job hypotheses; excluded hardware operations and direct physical fulfillment.
2. Compared a 75-candidate pool for buyer specificity, bootstrap cost and family overlap. Its exact IDs are in `review.comparison_pool75`.
3. Inspected forty diverse candidates against live product/pricing pages. The research sample is not a blind top-score cutoff.
4. Added five feature-level sources for three initially attractive revenue candidates; downgraded all three to reserve.
5. Preserved failed distinctions and explicit future experiments rather than deleting inconvenient competitor evidence.

Scores use the shared weights. Reused coarse score profiles intentionally avoid spurious numerical precision; identical totals are ties,
not evidence that opportunities have equal expected returns. Reach never exceeds 3: no existing founder audience or privileged channel was verified.
Wedge never exceeds 3, and feature-level overlap reduces it. Parent review should prioritize evidence and family diversity over one-point differences.

## Stress test: three initially attractive picks

### W0001 — Partner CSV preflight portal

**Verdict: reserve; generic validation and browser privacy are occupied features.**

[CSVbox features](https://csvbox.io/features/) include remembered mapping, cell/row/table error handling, backend checks and browser-only private
processing. [Its pricing](https://csvbox.io/pricing/) displays Startup at $16/month billed yearly and Pro at $39/month billed yearly.
Our $49/month proposal therefore cannot win merely through mapping, validation, low price or keeping payloads local.

Remaining hypothesis: an operator handling several external customer imports needs provider-independent repair and accepted-file evidence,
while retaining an existing importer. No source establishes that this buyer exists in sufficient numbers.

- Narrow MVP: browser-only CSV checks, downloadable correction report and explicit accepted-file receipt.
- Data dependency: buyer-supplied schema and authorized sample; no paid data feed, model API or arbitrary customer code execution.
- Cost hypothesis: $20–40/month hosting with 10MB input cap and metadata retention; founder support labor excluded.
- Distribution test: public failed-import examples and an opt-in repair demo for SaaS onboarding engineers.
- Kill gate: fewer than two of fifteen prospects choose $49/month for at least two recurring monthly handoffs.
- Comparison gate: at least 30 minutes saved per repair versus the customer's existing CSVbox/script workflow.
- Reopen only for the handoff job. Do not reinterpret established importer features as an underserved market.

### W0016 — Localization placeholder gate

**Verdict: reserve; the exact proposed checks already exist in a product supporting CI.**

[SimpleLocalize QA](https://simplelocalize.io/qa-checks/) describes placeholder, ICU, HTML and length checks through editor, API and CLI imports.
It also supports configurable ignored issues. A CLI or pull-request check alone is therefore not a distinct market wedge.
[BabelEdit](https://www.codeandweb.com/babeledit) offers perpetual local use, demonstrating an alternative purchasing model but not recurring revenue.

Remaining hypothesis: teams refusing a translation-management platform pay for repository-only exception/reviewer history beyond free lint.
This could instead be a free developer acquisition tool attached to a broader localization product.

- Narrow MVP: JSON/ICU fixtures, exact source pointers and protected-string exception history.
- Data dependency: repository translation files and buyer-approved policy; no translation AI spending.
- Cost hypothesis: $20–35/month hosted metadata; customer CI performs all analysis.
- Distribution test: useful free GitHub Action with documented failure examples; paid history only if requested.
- Kill gate: free lint plus existing translation QA catches the entire target set, or fewer than two of fifteen pay $29/month.
- Do not build a generic localization platform merely to justify the small feature's subscription.

### W0030 — Customer-domain health roster

**Verdict: reserve; test tenant repair coordination, not domain monitoring.**

[Oh Dear client reporting](https://ohdear.app/features/client-reporting) already supports site-specific recipients, guests and DNS/certificate
report sections. [Cloudflare's hostname documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/domain-support/hostname-validation/)
already exposes separate hostname and SSL states. [Its plan table](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-for-platforms/cloudflare-for-saas/plans/)
includes 100 hostnames and displays $0.10 for each additional hostname; that page does not specify the added-hostname billing cadence.
These are infrastructure terms, not a directly comparable support-workflow subscription.

Remaining hypothesis: domain-heavy B2B SaaS support teams need cases joining failures to customer owners, required repairs and resolution evidence.
A provider API query plus the existing ticket system may fully satisfy this job.

- Narrow MVP: tenant/domain/owner CSV, expected DNS target, verified domain ownership and optional read-only Cloudflare status.
- Observations: public DNS, TLS metadata and provider statuses; no proxying, certificate issuance or DNS mutation.
- Cost hypothesis: $20–40/month with cached daily checks, bounded domains and email volume.
- Distribution test: custom-domain debugging guide and opt-in ten-domain repair audit.
- Kill gate: native API plus a ticket template resolves eight of ten historical cases in under ten minutes.
- Purchase gate: two domain-heavy SaaS teams commit $39/month after demonstrated median 30-minute ticket savings.

## Budget, recurring revenue and operating burden

Default pre-revenue spending is $20–50/month per product. Approximately $300/month becomes available only after products earn.
Dossier estimates are provisional operating envelopes, not provider quotes. They assume modest pilot counts, bounded retention, no paid acquisition,
customer-owned runners and substantial unpaid founder time. Payment fees, taxes, customer compute and exceptional support must be modeled separately.
Transferring computation to customers reduces our bill but adds install friction and their costs.

At proposed realized prices, one product needs 103 customers at $49, 129 at $39, or 173 at $29 to exceed $5,000 MRR.
A hypothetical two-product mix of 65 customers at $39 and 51 at $49 yields $5,034 MRR. This is arithmetic, not an acquisition forecast.
At an assumed 3% monthly customer churn, 116 customers would need roughly four replacement customers monthly just to hold account count.
No churn estimate was observed. Lifetime-only sales and free tools contribute zero recurring revenue unless a separately purchased recurring service exists.

Freemium fits deterministic local checks when the free artifact is useful and paid history/coordination has a genuine buyer.
Subscriptions fit continuous monitoring and repeat release workflows. Lifetime fits local occasional utilities, but support/update obligations still need limits.
Avoid unlimited browser execution, arbitrary scraping, production event routing, managed databases and broad security guarantees before revenue.
A larger $300 hosting allowance would not solve unclear distribution or missing purchase intent.

## Portfolio correlation and rejected traps

- `W0001`, `W0002`, `W0083` and related import propositions share an import family; do not count variants as independent revenue bets.
- `W0003`, `W0013` and `W0033` overlap API-change infrastructure; `W0033` is a likely feature inside a broader release product.
- Localization candidates share buyers and distribution; choose one experiment before committing multiple apps.
- Backup alerts and restore evidence share operators; observed completion is explicitly weaker evidence than application recovery.
- Generic uptime, feature flags, JSON formatting, code review and documentation chatbot clones fail differentiation against current free/bundled tools.
- Hosted infrastructure clones fail operational scope even if an initial demo can be built cheaply.
- Consulting-heavy data migration, security assurances and legal license interpretation cannot be priced as unattended micro-SaaS without scope limits.

## Source limitations and verification

- All forty-five source entries are primary pages accessed in this research; none represents interviews or independent customer outcomes.
- Price strings preserve annual billing, currency and ambiguous toggle states. Unknown numeric prices remain unknown.
- Some vendor pages changed positioning or redirected; no historical remembered price was substituted.
- Numerical vendor savings, customer counts and testimonials were not used as measured market size or guaranteed demand.
- Current vendor promises were inspected, not trialed. Feature quality and operational reliability remain untested.
- Screened rows retain `source_ids: []` and `evidence_fit: none`; they are hypotheses, not hidden claims of market verification.
- Schema checks passed: contiguous unique IDs, 250 distinct names, required fields, 40 complete dossiers, 45 unique source URLs, valid source references,
  permitted pricing models and exact weighted-score arithmetic. Every registered source is referenced by a dossier.
- Changes are limited to `developer.json` and this file. No staging, commits, deployment, product code or external messages were performed by this lane.
